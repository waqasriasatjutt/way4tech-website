Shopify Connector User Guide

How to connect a Shopify store to Odoo, decide what it may sell, bring in products, customers and orders, ship them, refund them, and prove every payout against the invoices behind it. Follow the first seven sections in order when you start; after that, sections 8 to 13 are the daily routine.

1

Try it first

Five minutes, no Shopify account needed.

There are two ways to see the connector working before you point it at your shop.

  • The live demo. Open shopify-test.way4tech.com and log in as demo with password demo. It is the Northwind Outdoor Supply store: forty orders, refunds, payouts, abandoned checkouts and a queue with a few failures left in on purpose. It resets every night, so change whatever you like.
  • Your own database. Install the module, open Shopify > Stores and press Load the Northwind demo store. The same store is created, answered by an offline Shopify that ships inside the module. Delete the store when you are done.
2

Connect your store

Ten minutes, once per store.
  1. In your Shopify admin open Settings, Apps and sales channels, Develop apps and create an app. Give it the Admin API scopes the connection wizard lists: products, inventory, customers, orders, fulfillments and fulfillment orders, locations, shipping, discounts, price rules and payouts, plus publications if you want to publish products from Odoo.
  2. Install the app and copy the Admin API access token.
  3. In Odoo open Shopify > + Connect new store. Paste the shop address and the token.
  4. Press Test Connection. It shows the shop name, the plan, the currency and the API version Shopify answered with.
  5. Choose REST or GraphQL for the store. Both run the same jobs; GraphQL is where Shopify adds new features.
3

Locations and stock

Decide what Shopify is allowed to sell.

Press Import Locations on the store and map each Shopify location to the Odoo warehouse that ships it. Orders assigned to that location ship from that warehouse, and stock for that location is read from it.

Each location pushes stock through a policy. The store carries the default on the Sync Behaviour tab; untick Use store defaults on a location to give it its own.

  • Quantity source. Free is on hand less what confirmed deliveries have reserved, and it is the safe choice. On hand ignores reservations. Forecast adds incoming receipts and takes off outgoing deliveries.
  • Percentage. Push only a share, for example 80, and keep the rest for other channels.
  • Offset. A safety stock the shop never sees, taken off after the percentage.
  • Minimum. Below it, zero is pushed, so the shop shows sold out rather than the last two units.
  • Also count. Other warehouses summed into this location, and the stock of child companies when branches share one shop.
Kits

A product sold as a kit (a bill of materials of type kit in Manufacturing) reports what its components allow. When a component moves, every kit containing it is pushed again.

4

Products and prices

One catalogue, any number of stores.

A product carries one row per Shopify store it is on. Each row has its own Shopify id, handle, status, publish state, SEO title and description, price and sync state. Open the Shopify tab on any product to see them.

  • Publish and unpublish from the row, or from the list with the actions menu.
  • Export as draft on the store sends new products to Shopify as drafts for someone to review.
  • Exclude from sync keeps a product in Odoo only, whatever else changes.
  • Export to Shopify in the actions menu of the product list adds the selected products to a store and queues the exports in batches.

Prices are set on the store, Sync Behaviour tab, Pricing:

  • Store pricelist: the price exported for each variant. Without one, the sales price.
  • Compare-at pricelist: the crossed-out price Shopify shows next to it.
  • Price import pricelist: prices changed in Shopify are written as rules on this pricelist, and the product sales price stays yours.
5

Webhooks

Changes arrive in seconds.

Press Register Webhooks on the store. Shopify then tells Odoo about new orders, edits, payments, refunds, fulfilments, product, customer and stock changes as they happen. Every message is checked against the shop secret, and a message Shopify sends twice is counted, not imported twice.

Each topic has its own switch on the Webhooks tab. Turning one off removes it in Shopify; the schedules still catch anything it would have carried.

Odoo must be reachable

Webhooks need your Odoo on a public HTTPS address. Without one, the schedules still keep everything in step, just not in seconds.

6

Accounting and order rules

Tell it where the money goes.
  • On the store, Accounting mapping: the payout journal (a bank journal), the sales journal and the fiscal position.
  • On the Payment Methods tab: for each Shopify gateway, the journal its payments are registered in.
  • On the Order Workflow Rules tab: for each payment status, whether to confirm, invoice, post and register the payment. A sensible start: paid confirms, invoices and registers the payment; pending confirms only.
7

The first import

Link what you have, create what you do not.
  1. Open Shopify > Operations > Import from Shopify, choose Products and tick Dry run. Nothing is written. The job lists, for each Shopify product, whether it would be linked to an Odoo product with the same SKU or barcode, created, or updated.
  2. Fix anything the dry run flags, then run it for real.
  3. Import customers. They are matched on email; switch on Also match customers on phone if your customers often have no email. There is no matching on name, on purpose.
  4. Choose which orders come in, on the Sync Behaviour tab: by payment status (paid only, paid or partially paid, or authorized as well) and by fulfilment status. Then import orders since a date of your choice.
  5. Press Push Inventory to Shopify once so Shopify shows what Odoo can sell.
8

Orders day to day

What happens without anyone pressing a button.

A Shopify order arrives as an Odoo sales order with its lines, the shipping line, Shopify taxes, exact discounts, the currency, delivery and invoice addresses and the risk Shopify scored. The total is checked against what Shopify charged and flagged when it differs. Then the workflow rule for its payment status runs.

When the order is edited in the Shopify admin, the Odoo order follows: a line added becomes a new line and the delivery grows, a line removed or reduced goes down, a price changed follows. A line already delivered or invoiced is not changed, and the chatter says why: the money Shopify gives back for it arrives as a credit note through the refund import instead.

Orders from Shopify POS are marked, can be filtered, and ship from the warehouse of the till. Orders already shipped when they arrive, such as history or a 3PL shipment, come in with their delivery validated and the tracking copied.

9

Shipping

Ship from the right place, tell Shopify once.

Shopify assigns each order to a location. The connector reads that and ships from the warehouse mapped to it. When you validate the delivery, Shopify gets the fulfilment with the tracking reference, for exactly the quantities shipped. A tracking number changed later follows.

  • Shipping from a different warehouse moves the fulfillment order in Shopify first.
  • With Split deliveries by Shopify location on, an order spread over two locations gets one delivery per warehouse.
  • With Shopify fulfilments validate Odoo deliveries on, a fulfilment made in Shopify validates the Odoo delivery.
10

Refunds

Both ways, never more than was paid.

A refund made in Shopify becomes a draft credit note in Odoo for exactly the refunded money, and a return for the units that were restocked. Nothing is posted for you.

A credit note posted in Odoo on a Shopify order can refund the customer in Shopify: automatically when Push credit notes to Shopify is on, or with the Refund in Shopify button on the credit note. Shopify is asked first what it still holds; a credit note above that is refused with the figures, and nothing is refunded. Units whose return is done in Odoo are restocked in Shopify.

11

Payouts and fees

Prove the money that reached the bank.

Each Shopify Payments payout arrives as a draft bank statement line on the payout journal, with every transaction behind it: charges, refunds, adjustments, reserves and disputes, each with the amount, the fee Shopify kept and the net.

  1. On the store, Accounting mapping, switch on Book Shopify Fees and choose the fee account. Each paid payout gets one draft journal entry: the fee account debited, the payout journal suspense account credited.
  2. Switch on Reconcile Payouts Automatically, or press Reconcile on a payout. Each charge is matched to the invoice payment in the Shopify Payments journal, each refund to its credit note. Adjustments and disputes are marked for review.
  3. Read the state: Fully Matched, Partially Matched with a note saying what is open, or Not Reconciled.
When a payout does not add up

If the transactions do not sum to the payout amount, something Shopify listed is missing. The payout says so, stays partially matched, and gets no fee entry until the figures agree. Press Refresh from Shopify later, or ask Shopify support about the payout.

12

Reports

Under Shopify > Reporting.
  • Net profit by store: untaxed sales, product cost at today's cost price, Shopify fees and what is left. Refunds are on their own report; read the two side by side.
  • Payout report: amount, fee and net by store, month and transaction type.
  • Tax report: tax collected on Shopify orders by tax, store and month, from posted invoices and credit notes.
  • Sales analysis and refunds by month.
13

Keeping it running

Five minutes a day.

Every call to Shopify is a job in Shopify > Operations > Queue Jobs. A job that fails keeps the reason Shopify gave and retries on a schedule. When it runs out of retries, the person named under Alerts on the store gets an activity and an email, at most one an hour.

The daily digest sends one email per store at the hour you choose: orders and revenue, what failed, what is waiting. The log book keeps every success and error for as many days as you set, and exports any period to CSV from the actions menu of the log list.

14

Upgrading

Before you press Upgrade on a live shop.

Take a database backup, replace the module folder, then Apps > Shopify Connector > Upgrade. Version 19.0.5.9.0 switches off Shopify fulfilments validate Odoo deliveries on stores that existed before the upgrade, so no delivery starts validating itself the day you upgrade. Turn it on from the store when you want it. New stores have it on.

15

If something looks wrong

The usual causes, and the fix.
What you seeWhyWhat to do
An order is missing.The import filters on the store skipped it, or the job that fetched it failed.Open the latest order import job: a skipped order has a line saying why. A failed one has the reason Shopify gave.
An order total is flagged.Odoo and Shopify disagree by more than rounding, usually a tax mapped differently.Compare the taxes on the order with Shopify's; fix the tax mapping and import the order again.
An edit in Shopify did not change the order.The line was already delivered or invoiced.Read the chatter note. The refund Shopify issues arrives as a credit note.
A credit note was not refunded in Shopify.It was above what Shopify still holds, in another currency, or the store toggle is off.The chatter on the credit note says which. Fix it and press Refund in Shopify.
A payout has no fee entry.Book Shopify Fees is off, no fee account is set, or the payout does not add up.The note on the payout says which.
Shopify shows the wrong stock.The location is not mapped to a warehouse, or its policy holds stock back.Check the Locations tab: the policy line says exactly what is pushed.
Nothing arrives by itself.Webhooks are not registered, or Odoo is not reachable from the internet.Register Webhooks on the store; the schedules keep things moving meanwhile.

Anything else: write to info@way4tech.com with the store name, the job or order number and a screenshot.
