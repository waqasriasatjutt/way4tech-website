The edition decision is usually made in about ten minutes, on the wrong number. Somebody compares the subscription line against zero, concludes the obvious thing, and the internal effort, the hosting and the support never enter the model. The cheaper licence often loses once they are in.

So this is the comparison done the other way round: start from what Community actually does not include, then match that against how your business runs. If any of it lands, the edition question answers itself. If none of it lands, you have your answer too.

## The part nobody disputes

Community and Enterprise share a core. Same ORM, same models, same fields, same business logic, same API for custom modules. Enterprise is that core plus a set of extra applications, an interface, and a support relationship.

That matters for one reason: "is Community production ready" is not a real question. It is the same code the Enterprise applications sit on top of. What differs is which applications are installed, how the backend looks, and who answers when something breaks at month end. Treat the last one as the real question.

## What Community genuinely leaves out

The list is short and specific, and it is on our [Community edition page](/odoo-community/) in full:

- [Studio](/modules/studio/), the no-code editor for views, fields, reports and automations.
- The official iOS and Android apps. Community gives you the responsive web client in a phone browser.
- Accounting beyond Invoicing. Bank statement synchronization, follow-up automation, deferred revenue and asset depreciation are Enterprise.
- Four view types: Gantt, cohort, map and dashboard.
- Odoo Online and Odoo.sh hosting, and support direct from Odoo.
- Several apps that exist only in Enterprise: [Documents](/modules/documents/), [Sign](/modules/sign/), [Planning](/modules/planning/), [Field Service](/modules/field-service/), Marketing Automation, Social Marketing, [Helpdesk](/modules/helpdesk/), Appraisals, Approvals, [Quality](/modules/quality/), [Barcode](/modules/barcode/), [IoT](/modules/iot/), and VoIP.

One caveat that belongs next to that list every time it is printed: Odoo moves things between editions between versions, so check it against the exact version you are installing rather than against a blog post, this one included.

## The four triggers that actually decide it

| If this is true | The answer is |
|---|---|
| Someone who does not write code has to change forms and reports themselves | Enterprise |
| The official mobile apps matter to field staff | Enterprise |
| Two or more Enterprise-only apps sit in the middle of how you work | Enterprise |
| You want Odoo hosting and supporting you directly | Enterprise |
| None of the above, and you have or can buy technical capability | Community |

That is the honest short form. Community suits cost-sensitive teams with technical maturity. Enterprise suits teams that want the vendor-supported apps and the support behind them. The comparison worth doing includes internal effort, not just the subscription line, because that is usually what decides it.

Take the first row seriously. Most of what Studio does can be done in module code by a developer, and the code version is more durable and less immediate. If you want an administrator changing a form on a Tuesday afternoon without calling anyone, that is a real reason to pay for Enterprise, and we will say so.

The third row is where the arithmetic turns. One Enterprise-only app can often be worked around. Two or more sitting in the middle of your process usually means Enterprise is the cheaper answer before you count anything else.

## The fact worth checking before you assume you are locked in

Our own catalogue holds 139 Odoo modules, every one built at 19.0, and not one of them requires an Odoo Enterprise subscription. They install on a plain Community database and none depends on an Enterprise application.

That is not a marketing line, it is a constraint we build to, and it changes the shape of the decision in one specific way. A gap in Community is not automatically a reason to change edition. It is a reason to ask what closes the gap and what that costs against the alternative.

Reporting is the usual example. Edition comparisons frequently hinge on it, and knowing what Community can already produce changes the cost case. If the requirement is financial reporting depth, [Dynamic Financial Reports](/products/wt-dynamic-financial-reports/) is the thing to price against the edition change rather than assuming the edition change. If it is budget and commitment control, that is [Budgets and Commitment Control](/products/wt-budget-control/), and it is worth checking before the decision is made on it. If it is governance and change tracking, an [audit log](/products/wt-audit-log/) settles what is available either way. If it is managers who will not open a report, a [dashboard builder](/products/wt-dashboard-builder/) is a smaller purchase than an edition.

None of that makes Community right for everyone. It makes the comparison an actual comparison.

## The costs Community does not remove

The code is open source and there is no per-user licence fee. Hosting, implementation, training, support and any paid module are not free. Community removes one line from the budget, not the budget.

It also moves a responsibility. Odoo Online and Odoo.sh both require an Enterprise subscription, and so does support direct from Odoo. Community runs on your server or on ours. That is a change of supplier rather than a missing feature, but it is a decision you make on day one, not later, and it comes with a real question attached: do you have someone who will patch a server at ten on a Friday night? If the answer is no and you are not buying that from anyone, Community is not cheaper, it is just unbilled until something breaks. Our position on the hosting half is on the [SaaS and hosting page](/services/odoo-saas-hosting/).

There is one architecture where the answer is not close. Running your own multi-tenant Odoo platform, selling Odoo to your own customers, is a Community model.

## Changing your mind later

Community to Enterprise is straightforward: same database, same data, with the Enterprise modules installed on top.

The other direction has no one-click route. Every Enterprise-only application has to be replaced or retired, and anything built in Studio has to become module code, before the Enterprise modules come out. Both directions are a project rehearsed on a copy, not a switch.

If an upgrade is already on the table, doing the edition change in the same project is usually cheaper than sequencing them. It enlarges the testing scope, so it gets planned deliberately rather than added mid-way. That trade-off is set out under [migration and upgrade](/services/odoo-migration/), and the framework for choosing the target version is in [upgrade now or wait](/blog/odoo-18-vs-odoo-19-upgrade-now-or-wait/).

## How to run the decision in one afternoon

1. List the apps you will genuinely use. Mark the ones on the Enterprise-only list.
2. Ask who changes a report when it is wrong. If the answer is an administrator with no developer behind them, weight Studio heavily.
3. Ask what your field staff use. A phone browser and an installed app are not the same thing, and people who work outside all day notice.
4. Price the internal effort, the hosting and the support for the Community option. Not the licence. Those three.
5. Check the gaps against what a module closes before treating any of them as an edition decision.
6. Write down the assumption that would change the answer, so you can revisit it without redoing the whole exercise.

If that produces a close call, it is close, and either edition will work. The projects that go wrong are not the ones that picked the other edition. They are the ones that never wrote the reasoning down and could not defend it eighteen months later when someone asked.

## FAQs

**Is Odoo Community free?**
The code is open source and carries no per-user licence fee. What you still pay for is everything around it: hosting, implementation, training, support and any paid connectors the market you sell into requires. That last item is the one people forget, and it is market-specific rather than edition-specific.

**Do your modules need an Odoo Enterprise subscription?**
No. All 139 of them install on a plain Community database and none depends on an Enterprise application.

**Is Community production ready?**
Yes. It is the same ORM, the same models and the same business logic Enterprise sits on top of, so the question is never whether it runs. The question is whether the apps you need are in the edition you picked.

**When should we pay for Enterprise instead?**
When someone who does not write code has to change forms and reports themselves, when the official mobile apps matter to field staff, when two or more Enterprise-only apps are central to your process, or when you want Odoo hosting and supporting you directly.

**Can we start on Community and move to Enterprise later?**
Yes. Same database, same data, with the Enterprise modules installed on top. The other direction has no one-click route, and both are a project rehearsed on a copy rather than a switch.
