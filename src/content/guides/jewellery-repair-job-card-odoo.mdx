The hard part of a jewellery repair is not the labour. It is the gold. A customer hands over two worn bangles to be melted into one wider one and expects to get the same metal back in a new shape. If the card only says "repair" and nobody weighed anything, a missing two grams becomes an argument with no record behind either side. The same is true of stock that leaves the shop unsold: a tray on approval with a customer, a salesman's bag, an exhibition stand. This chapter covers the workshop job card, which makes the metal add up, and the memo, which makes every piece out of the shop signed for at a value.

## Take a job in

Open **Jewellery, Workshop, Repairs and Custom Orders** and press **New**.

1. Pick the **customer** and the **counter**, and the **kind** of job: repair, resizing, custom order, melt and remake, polish and clean, stone setting, rhodium or plating, engraving.
2. Under **The piece**, describe what was handed over and set the metal and purity.
3. Under **What is wanted**, write it in the customer's own words. A card that says "fix" is the one that comes back wrong.
4. Weigh the customer's metal in front of them and enter it as **Metal in**.
5. Set the **promised for** date the customer was told, and take an **advance** if it is a custom order.
6. Press **Take In**. The module refuses if a weight has been entered with no metal against it.

## Send it to the bench

Name a **bench** (one of your own goldsmiths) or an **outside goldsmith** on the card, then send it. Metal does not leave the counter until somebody is named; the button refuses an empty bench.

A goldsmith signs in and opens **Workshop, My Bench**, which lists only the jobs assigned to them. They see no tickets, rates or accounts.

Materials and charges go on the **Materials and Charges** tab: metal, stones, findings, bench work, work sent out and plating. If shop metal is used in the piece, it goes on these lines and as **Shop metal added** on the Metal tab.

## Make the metal add up

When the piece comes back, weigh it and record **Metal out** and the **Filings returned**. The job card does the sum:

| Figure | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Metal in | The customer's metal, weighed at the counter |
| Plus shop metal added | Metal from the shop worked into the piece |
| Less metal out | What the finished piece weighs |
| Less filings returned | Dust and offcuts handed back |
| Equals the variance | Positive means metal is missing |

Three checks protect that figure:

- **Mark Ready** refuses if no metal out has been weighed, and refuses if the metal charged on the material lines does not match the shop metal added.
- If more metal came back than went out, the card refuses to save and asks whether shop metal was used without being written down.
- **Hand Back** refuses while the variance is above the weight tolerance in the settings and nobody has written down where it went: polishing loss, solder burnt off, a piece cut away.

Handing back records the variance for good. **Invoice** then bills the labour and materials less the advance. A job cannot be invoiced while it is still at the bench, and a posted invoice has to be credited rather than the job cancelled underneath it.

**Print Job Card** gives the slip that goes to the bench with the piece, carrying the weights it has to be reconciled against and lines for the counter, the bench and the customer to sign.

## Goods out on approval or memo

Stock leaves the shop without being sold more often than an owner would like. Open **Jewellery, Stock, Approval and Memo** and press **New**. Pick the kind:

- **Customer approval**: goods go home with a named customer. The customer is required.
- **Salesman memo**: goods go out in a named salesman's bag. The salesman is required.
- **Exhibition**: goods go to a stand or show.
- **Out to the workshop**: goods go to the bench or an outside goldsmith.

List the pieces and issue the memo. At that moment the weight and the value at that day's rate are stamped on each line, so the figure the memo was signed against does not move with the market. A memo with no pieces, or a line with no value, is refused.

**The manager limit.** Above the memo value needing a manager in the settings, only a Jewellery Manager can issue the memo, and their name goes on it as Authorised By.

**Back, sold, still out.** As pieces return or sell, the memo moves to Partly Back and its front shows what came back, what sold and what is still out, in pieces, grams and value. **Turn Into a Sale** makes a counter ticket from the kept pieces, carrying the customer and the weights across. **Everything Came Back** returns the rest in one step. **Close** refuses while any piece is unaccounted for.

**Print Note** gives the memo note to be signed when the goods leave, listing every piece and its value.

<Callout title="Reminders are switched off until you turn them on">Three scheduled jobs ship inactive: carrying the rate forward, chasing finished and overdue repairs, and chasing goods out on memo. The two customer emails (repair ready, approval goods due) only go once the jobs are on and an outgoing mail server is set up. Customers have no portal to check a repair or an approval themselves.</Callout>
