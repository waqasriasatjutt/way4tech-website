A spare parts wholesaler does not want a web shop in the usual sense. The prices on the screen are trade prices, and the day a retail customer or a competitor sees them, the wholesaler has a problem with every garage that buys from it. What it wants is a trade counter that stays open at night: the dealer signs in, finds the brake pads for a 2016 Corolla by the car or by the number on the old box, sees his own price, orders by the carton and goes back to work. This guide covers what that takes in Odoo 19, what the standard eCommerce app does on its own, and how the Auto Parts Wholesale and Dealer Portal module closes the gap.

## What a parts wholesaler actually needs

The people ordering are parts men at garages, retailers and fleet workshops. They know the trade, they order the same things every month, and they have no patience for a shop that makes them hunt. Six things decide whether they use it:

1. **The catalogue shut to the public.** No prices, no product list, nothing a price-checker can scrape. The public sees who you are and how to apply.
2. **Approval by you.** An account is not a dealer until somebody at the wholesaler says so. Until then, the shop stays closed.
3. **The price tier of that dealer.** A small retailer and a big workshop chain do not pay the same. Each should see only their own price.
4. **Search by car and by number.** A parts man searches by the vehicle ("Suzuki Alto") or by a part number: the genuine number, an aftermarket number or an old number that has since been replaced.
5. **Sold in the units you ship.** Spark plugs go out in boxes of ten, filters in cartons of twelve. An order for seven of a box of four should arrive as eight, not as a call from the warehouse.
6. **Repeat orders in one click.** Most trade buying is the same order again. Rebuilding it line by line is the main reason dealers phone instead of using the shop.

## What standard Odoo does and does not do

Odoo 19 ships an [eCommerce app](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/websites/ecommerce.html) in Community. It has a product catalogue, a cart and checkout, customer accounts and a portal where a customer sees past orders. It also has [pricelists](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/sales/sales/products_prices/prices/pricing.html), so different customers can be charged different prices for the same product. Those two pieces cover a lot of the list above, and the module builds on both rather than replacing them.

What the standard app does not give a parts wholesaler:

- **No trade-only gate.** The shop is built to sell to anyone. There is no setting that hides the whole catalogue and every price until the customer is approved by you.
- **No vehicle fitment.** A product has categories and attributes, but there is no record of which makes and models a part fits, and no "find parts for my car" filter.
- **No part number search beyond the product name and internal reference.** Cross reference and superseded numbers have nowhere to live.
- **No pack rounding on the website.** The cart takes whatever quantity is typed.
- **No list of what is actually selling.** There is no wall of fast movers built from confirmed sales.

You could get part of the way with product attributes for vehicles and a password on the website. The gate that redirects a non-dealer away from every shop page, the fitment filter in the search and the rounding of cart lines all need code. That is what the module adds.

## The module and what it covers

**Auto Parts Wholesale and Dealer Portal** (technical name `w4t_auto_parts_wholesale`) is a single module, USD 399, for Odoo 19 Community. It depends on eCommerce, Sales and Inventory and installs them with it. It is not on the Odoo Apps Store yet; ask for it through the [contact page](/contact/).

| Need | Standard eCommerce | With the module |
|---|---|---|
| Catalogue and prices hidden from the public | No | Yes, shop, product pages and cart redirect to a dealer landing |
| Customer approved before shopping | No | Yes, one tick on the dealer company |
| Different price per customer | Yes, pricelists | Same, each dealer on a pricelist |
| Parts linked to the vehicles they fit | No | Yes, makes and models with a year range |
| Filter the shop by vehicle | No | Yes, Find parts for your vehicle |
| Search by OEM and cross reference numbers | No | Yes |
| Order rounded to whole packs and a minimum | No | Yes, on website orders |
| Hot, new and low stock ribbons | No | Yes |
| Reorder a past order from the portal | Odoo's own Order Again button | A Reorder button that skips delisted parts |

## How it looks to a visitor and to a dealer

A visitor who is not signed in and tries the shop, a product page or the cart is sent to the dealer landing page. It shows your company name as a trade counter, a short line about what dealers get, a Dealer sign in button and an Apply for a dealer account button, which opens your website contact form. Below that sits a wall of what is moving this month: part names and brands only, no prices and no links into the catalogue.

A customer who signs in but has not been approved gets the same page with a different notice: the account is not approved for the trade shop yet. Your own staff always see the shop, so the counter team can work it without a dealer account.

Once the dealer is approved, the whole shop opens at their price. The shop page carries a Find parts for your vehicle bar above the products, part cards show Hot, New and Low stock ribbons, and the product page lists brand, OEM number, pack size, minimum order, the vehicles the part fits and its cross reference numbers.

## The back office

The module adds an Auto Parts app with four menus: Parts Catalogue, Dealers, Vehicles (Makes and Models) and Brands, plus Configuration for the parts manager. The catalogue is ordinary Odoo products with an Auto Part tab added, so stock, purchasing, costing and invoicing all work the way they already do.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-auto-parts-wholesale/parts-catalogue.webp" alt="Parts Catalogue kanban in the Odoo Auto Parts app with parts such as Air Filter AF-200, Battery 12V 45Ah, Brake Pads Front and Clutch Plate, each with price and on hand quantity" width={1440} height={900} caption="The Parts Catalogue: ordinary Odoo products with price and stock on hand, filtered to auto parts." />

Access is in two layers. Anyone with Sales rights sees the Auto Parts app. The Auto Parts Manager group manages makes, models, brands, cross references and dealer approvals, and every Sales manager gets that group automatically, so a small team does not have to hand it out separately.

## How to set it up

1. Install the module. eCommerce, Sales and Inventory come with it.
2. Open **Auto Parts, Configuration, Settings**. Leave **Shop open to approved dealers only** on, set the **hot products window** in days (30 by default) and the **hot products threshold** in pieces (1 by default), and choose whether cross references show on the product page.
3. Build your vehicle list in **Vehicles, Makes** and **Vehicles, Models**, with a year range on each model where it matters.
4. Add your brands in **Brands**: genuine, OEM and the aftermarket makers you stock.
5. On each product, tick **Auto Part** on the Auto Part tab and fill in brand, OEM number, pack quantity, minimum order quantity, the vehicles it fits and its cross reference numbers. The [fitment and part number chapter](/odoo-guides/odoo-auto-parts-fitment-oem-search/) goes through this step by step.
6. Create a pricelist per dealer tier in Sales, then put each dealer on one.
7. Approve dealers by ticking **Approved Dealer** on the Dealer tab of the dealer company. The [dealer portal chapter](/odoo-guides/odoo-b2b-dealer-portal-trade-pricing/) covers approval, prices, packs and reorder.
8. Publish the parts on the website and sign in as a test dealer to check what they see before you send the link to real customers.

<Callout title="What the module does not do">There is no VIN decoder and no link to an outside parts catalogue: makes, models, fitment and cross references are your own data. The fitment filter works by model; the year range is shown in the model name but is not a separate filter. Apply for a dealer account opens your normal contact form, there is no application workflow. And the module does not check a dealer's credit limit or add a payment method; checkout uses the payment options you enable in eCommerce.</Callout>
