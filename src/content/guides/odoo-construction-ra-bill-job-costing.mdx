On a contracting job, the money question every month is not "what did we spend" but "what can we bill, what will they certify, and are we still making money". The site engineer has the measurements in a field book, the quantity surveyor has the RA bill in Excel, accounts has last month's invoices, and the director gets a margin figure in November for a job that went wrong in March. Odoo can hold all of that, but not out of the box. This guide explains the documents, what standard Odoo does with them, and how we run them in Odoo 19.

## What a contractor actually needs

### The RA bill, in plain terms

A running account (RA) bill is an interim bill for work done to date. It is cumulative: each bill states the gross value up to the previous bill, the gross value of this bill, and the cumulative gross to date. Deductions then come off the cumulative figure, and what was already certified comes off last, so the net is this month's money. In the UK and FIDIC world the same document is called an interim payment certificate or progress claim. The arithmetic does not change.

A worked example with illustrative figures: contract sum 1,000,000, retention 5 per cent capped at 50,000, a mobilisation advance of 100,000 recovered at 10 per cent of the value earned each period.

| Line | Bill 1 and 2 (to date) | Bill 3 (this bill) | Cumulative after bill 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross value of work | 250,000 | 150,000 | 400,000 |
| Less retention (5%) | 12,500 | 7,500 | 20,000 |
| Less advance recovery (10%) | 25,000 | 15,000 | 40,000 |
| Net value | 212,500 | 127,500 | 340,000 |
| Less previously certified | | | 212,500 |
| **Due on bill 3** | | | **127,500** |

Working cumulatively matters because quantities get corrected. If bill 2 over measured a slab, bill 3 measures the true quantity to date and the difference comes out by itself. A bill built from "this month's quantities only" carries every old error forward.

### Retention and advance

Retention is money the client holds back from every bill as security for defects, typically released in two parts (moieties), one at practical completion and one after the defects liability period. The percentage, cap and release dates are contract terms. Retention that nobody tracks to its due date is retention that never gets collected.

A mobilisation advance is paid up front and recovered by a percentage deduction from each bill until it is fully repaid. Recovery must stop exactly when the advance is cleared.

### Job costing: commitment, accrual and actual

Cost and value run on different clocks. Value is what has been certified, which lags the work. Cost on the books is what has been invoiced, which also lags: the subcontractor who worked all March invoices in April. A useful job cost report keeps three numbers apart:

- **Committed**: a subcontract order or approved requisition you are bound to.
- **Accrued**: work done or goods received that are not invoiced yet.
- **Actual**: invoiced and posted.

Compare certified value with actual cost only, and the job looks healthier than it is in exactly the months when you could still act.

## What standard Odoo does and does not do

Standard Odoo 19 gives you Project (tasks, stages, milestones), Purchase, Inventory and invoicing. A sales order line can be invoiced by milestone: you set a service product to "Based on Milestones", mark milestones reached in Project, and then create the invoice from the sales order, because reaching a milestone does not create one by itself ([Odoo 19 documentation, milestone invoicing](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/sales/sales/invoicing/milestone.html)). Analytic accounts let you tag costs to a project.

| Need | Standard Odoo 19 | With the two construction modules |
|---|---|---|
| Bill of quantities with sections, rates and cost rates | No | Yes, with Excel import checked row by row |
| Measurement book (number by length by width by depth) | No | Yes, cumulative, over measurement refused |
| Cumulative interim valuation and certificate | No, milestone or percentage invoicing only | Yes, previous, this bill, cumulative |
| Claimed versus certified, with reasons | No | Yes, line by line |
| Retention with capped percentage and release dates | No | Retention ledger with both moieties |
| Advance recovery | No | Percentage recovery, stops at the advance |
| Variations with estimate, assessed and agreed values | No | Yes |
| Committed, accrued and actual cost per cost code | Partial, via analytic accounts and purchase orders | Budget control and monthly cost value reconciliation |
| Draft customer invoice from a certificate | Not applicable | Yes, one invoice per certificate |

Milestone invoicing is fine for a fit out paid in three stages. It is not a substitute for measured, cumulative billing against a bill of quantities, and there is no retention concept anywhere in standard Odoo. ## How to set it up in Odoo

1. Install Construction Management first, then Construction Management Enterprise. A **Construction** app and a **Commercial** app appear.
2. Create the job under Construction, Projects, Projects, with client, contract value and phases.
3. Under Commercial, Contracts, set up the contract: contract sum, retention percentage and limit, the two moieties, the defects liability period, the valuation day, days to certify and days to pay.
4. Load the bill under Construction, Projects, Bills of Quantities. Import from Excel against the template, or key it in with sections, quantities, selling rates and cost rates. The approved budget per item is frozen once the bill is agreed.
5. Set the print title under Settings: Certificate Print Title can be Interim Payment Certificate, RA Bill, Progress Claim or Payment Application.
6. Each month, record measurements under Commercial, Measurement, Measurement Book. Enter number, length, width and depth per bill item; the quantity computes. A sheet that would take an item past its billed quantity is refused with the excess, and you raise a variation instead.
7. Certify the measurement sheets, then run Commercial, Valuations, Raise a Valuation. It pulls measured work, agreed variations and signed dayworks as at the valuation date. Key materials on site and time related preliminaries for the period. On the first valuation, enter the advance paid and the recovery percentage; later valuations carry them forward.
8. Serve the application for payment, then record the payment certificate under Commercial, Valuations, Payment Certificates with claimed against certified on every line and a reason for each difference.
9. Issue the certificate. It creates a draft customer invoice with the work certified, retention and advance recovered on separate lines, using accounts you set once in Settings. Your accountant posts it.
10. Print the RA Bill from the certificate, and run Commercial, Reporting, Job Cost Sheet for one or several jobs.

## What the RA Bill print shows

The RA Bill print takes the same certificate and lays it out the South Asian way: claimed against certified for measured work, variations, dayworks, materials on site and preliminaries; then gross value up to the previous bill, this bill and cumulative; then retention this bill, retention held to date, advance recovery to date, other deductions and previously certified, ending with the net payable and the amount in words. The bill number is the certificate's position on that contract. Other deductions (a back charge or a disputed item) reduce the printed net payable without changing the certificate's own amount payable, which is what Accounting posts.

## Retention, advance and variations in practice

- **Retention** is a ledger, not a percentage field. Each certificate writes a dated deduction, capped at the contract limit. When practical completion is recorded, the two release rows are written with their due dates and their share of what was actually held. A release cannot exist without a due date, and a snag can be flagged as blocking retention release.
- **Advance recovery** is a percentage of each period's gross value and never recovers more than the advance paid.
- **Variations** carry three figures, your estimate, their assessment and the agreed value, and only the agreed value enters a valuation. Site instructions convert to variations and keep the link.

## Job costing: the Job Cost Sheet and the CVR

Every bill item carries its approved budget and shows committed, actual, remaining and percentage used. The rules avoid double counting: an approved material requisition is committed, then actual once issued, never both; a subcontract commits its revised order value less what has been billed; labour, expenses and plant hire are actual only. What happens when an item would go over budget is a company setting: none, warn, block or send for approval.

The monthly cost value reconciliation (Commercial, Cost Value Reconciliation) sets value earned against cost including accruals by cost code, and the Job Cost Sheet prints budget, committed, actual, cost to complete, forecast final cost and variance by section and cost type. Both export to Excel.

## Limitations

These come straight from the module's own listing:

- **It is not an accounting engine.** Certificates raise draft customer invoices and certified subcontract valuations raise draft vendor bills. Your accountant still posts them, reconciles payments and runs the ledgers.
- **It is built for contractors.** Main contractors and subcontractors, not client side, consultant side or developer appraisal.
- **There is no native mobile app.** Site check in, diary and forms are phone browser pages and there is no offline mode.
- **It is an add-on.** Construction Management Enterprise will not install without the USD 10 base module.
- English is the working language; Arabic translation covers the module's screens and menus.
