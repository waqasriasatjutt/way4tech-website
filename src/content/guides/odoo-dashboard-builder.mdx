Ready-made dashboards answer the common questions. Every business also has two or three of its own: unpaid invoices for one customer group, receipts waiting from overseas vendors, or a monthly target the owner set at the start of the year. The Dashboard Builder (`wt_dashboard_builder`) covers those without a developer. You make a board, add tiles, and point each tile at a model with a measure, a filter and, for charts and lists, a field to group by. This chapter goes through every part of it, with the two boards built on the demo as worked examples.

## Where it lives

Installing the module adds a **Dashboards** app with three menus:

- **Dashboards:** the boards themselves, one tab per board, with Refresh and Configure buttons.
- **Configure Dashboards:** the list of boards, for designers.
- **All Tiles:** every tile on every board in one list, for designers.

Anyone with an internal user can view boards. To build them, add the person to the **Dashboard Designer** group on their user. The module also creates a **Business Overview** board with six tiles on contacts and users, so the app is never empty.

## The four tile types

| Type | What it shows | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| KPI | One number with an icon and a label | A measure |
| Bar | Horizontal bars, one per group, largest first | A measure and a group-by field |
| List | A ranked list of groups with their value | A measure and a group-by field |
| Progress | The value as a share of a target, with a bar | A measure and a target value |

Every tile has a **measure**: **Count** of records, or **Sum**, **Average**, **Minimum** or **Maximum** of a numeric field (integer, decimal or money). For anything but Count you must pick the field, and bar and list tiles refuse to save without a group-by field.

The **group-by** field can be a link to another record (salesperson, customer, stage), a selection (status), a date or date-time (grouped by month), a text field or a yes/no field. Bar and list tiles show the top groups by value; **Limit** sets how many, eight by default.

Each tile also has a **colour** (teal, blue, green, orange, purple, red or slate), an **icon** for KPI tiles using a Font Awesome name such as fa-users, and a **size**: small, medium, large or wide.

## Building a board, step by step

1. Open **Dashboards, Configure Dashboards** and create a board. Give it a name, a sequence (lower comes first in the tabs) and a short description.
2. Leave **Groups** empty for everyone, or pick the groups that should see the tab.
3. Add tiles in the tiles list. For each one choose the **type**, the **model**, the **measure** and its field, and for bar and list tiles the **group-by** field.
4. Add a **filter** if the tile should count only some records. The tile form uses Odoo's own rule editor: "Match all of the following rules", then a field, an operator and a value, with a live count of the matching records underneath. Behind it the filter is an Odoo domain, for example `[('state','=','sale')]` for confirmed sales orders, which you can also type in developer mode.
5. Save and open **Dashboards**. The new board is a tab. Drag tiles into the order you want.

### Filters with dates

The filter can use `context_today()`, `relativedelta`, `datetime` and `uid` (the viewer's user id). That is enough for rolling periods. The demo's 90-day tiles use:

```
[('state','=','sale'), ('date_order','>=', (context_today() - relativedelta(days=90)).strftime('%Y-%m-%d'))]
```

The tile form's rule editor cannot preview a filter like this and shows Invalid domain under it, as in the screenshot above; the board still evaluates it correctly, so check the figure on the board rather than in the form. For "my own records" use `[('user_id','=',uid)]`. There is no period switch on the board, so a tile's period lives in its filter.

### A target tile

A progress tile divides the value by its **Target Value**. The demo's Quarterly Target tile sums confirmed revenue over 90 days against 900,000 and shows the share as a bar. Change the target in the tile when the quarter's number changes.

**Stock and Purchasing** shows what can be done without any stock-specific module: receipts and deliveries waiting (counts of transfers by type and state), bills to pay (the open amount of posted vendor bills), purchase orders in 90 days, spend by vendor as bars, units on hand by product as a list, and receipts by month as a wide bar tile grouped on the done date.

<Callout title="Limits of the builder">A tile works out its figure by loading every matching record, so a tile on a very large model such as journal items or stock moves without a filter can be slow; add a filter. A filter that cannot be read is ignored and the tile then counts the whole model, so check a new tile's figure against the app. Tiles grouped by month are ordered by value, not by date. The Groups field hides a board's tab but does not stop someone opening it another way, so it is not a security setting. Tile order after dragging is kept in the browser, not on the user. In the current version, users without the Access Rights technical group also need read access to Odoo's model and field lists for tiles to load; on the demo that access is granted to internal users.</Callout>

## When to build and when to buy

Use the builder for counts, sums, rankings and targets on any model, especially your own custom ones. Use the ready-made boards when you want a period switch, comparisons and charts over time, which tiles do not do. Many businesses run both: the app boards for each department and one builder board for the owner with the five figures they ask about every day. The other boards are described in the [suite overview](/odoo-guides/odoo-dashboards/).
