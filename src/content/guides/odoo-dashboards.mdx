Most owners open Odoo in the morning with three or four questions: how much did we sell, what is waiting to go out, who owes us money, and is anything late. Odoo holds every one of those answers, but they sit in different apps behind different reports, and a pivot view has to be set up before it says anything. A dashboard does that work once, so the answer is on the screen the moment the app opens. This guide covers Way4Tech's dashboard suite for Odoo 19 Community: seven ready-made boards, one per app, and a builder for boards of your own.

## What a manager actually wants on one screen

The questions are the same in most trading businesses, whether they sell office supplies, run a showroom or manage a service team.

- **Sales:** revenue and confirmed orders this month, how many quotations are still open, the top customers and products, and who on the team is selling.
- **Buying and stock:** what was spent and with whom, which bills are waiting or overdue, what is on hand, which products are below their reorder point, and what is due in or out.
- **The till:** takings today, by terminal and cashier, which hours are busy, how customers pay and how much was given away in discounts.
- **Money:** invoiced revenue against bills, what customers owe and how old it is, and what the business owes its suppliers.
- **Pipeline:** new leads, open opportunities and their value, deals won and lost, win rate, and follow-ups that are overdue.
- **Projects:** tasks done, overdue and due today, who carries the load and how far each project has come.

Each of those needs a period. "This month" for the weekly meeting, "today" at the till, "this year" for the trend. And each figure is more useful next to the same figure for the period before.

## What Odoo 19 Community already shows

Standard Odoo is not blind. Every app has a **Reporting** menu with pivot, graph and list views over its own records, and you can group, filter and save those views as favourites. Odoo 19 Community also includes a **Dashboards** app built on spreadsheets, with ready-made boards for the installed apps; you can read them, but editing them is part of Odoo Enterprise.

What is missing is a screen that answers the morning questions without being built first: a fixed set of headline cards per app, a period switch that moves every card at once, a comparison with the previous period, and lists such as overdue bills or low stock that you can act on. That is the gap these modules fill.

## How every dashboard behaves

The seven ready-made boards share one layout, so once you can read one you can read them all.

1. **A header with the period.** The period list has Today, Yesterday, Last 7 Days, Last Week, This Month, Last Month, This Quarter, This Year and Custom Range. Custom Range shows two date boxes and applies once both are filled. The dates in use are printed under the title. Every board opens on This Month except POS, which opens on Today.
2. **Headline cards.** Nine cards per board. Most are clickable and open the matching Odoo list, for example the confirmed orders or the overdue bills, so a figure is one click from the records behind it.
3. **Tabs.** Charts and tables grouped by subject. Tables such as recent orders or overdue tasks have an **Export CSV** button that downloads the rows on screen.
4. **Comparison.** The last tab sets the chosen period against the period of the same length just before it, so This Month on the 5th compares the 1st to the 5th with the five days before.
5. **Refresh.** A Refresh button reloads every figure. The sales, purchase, inventory, accounting, CRM and project boards also reload on their own every five minutes while open.

<Callout title="Who sees the figures">The ready-made dashboards count straight from the database for the whole of the current company. Odoo record rules, such as a salesperson seeing only their own orders or a private project, do not narrow the totals. The menus are limited to the app's users and managers, except CRM Analytics, which every internal user can open. If company-wide totals should stay with managers, take the dashboard menus away from the other groups. Clicking through to a list still respects each user's rights. The Dashboard Builder is the exception: it reads with the viewer's own rights.</Callout>

## Setting the suite up

1. Install the standard apps first: Sales, Purchase, Inventory, Point of Sale, Invoicing, CRM and Project, whichever you use.
2. Install the dashboard for each app you want. There is nothing to configure: no settings page, no targets to enter.
3. Check the menus against your user groups. Sales Analytics is open to salespeople and sales managers, Purchase Analytics to purchase users and managers, Inventory Analytics to stock users and managers, POS Analytics to POS managers only, Accounting Analytics to accounting managers and the full accounting group, Project Analytics to project users and managers, and CRM Analytics to every internal user.
4. For the Inventory board's low stock list, create reorder rules on the stock location you count, such as WH/Stock. The board compares on hand at exactly that location with the rule's minimum.
5. For the Builder, add the people who should design boards to the **Dashboard Designer** group. Everyone else can view boards.

<Callout title="Limits worth knowing before you buy">The store listings promise more than the code does in a few places. Only the POS board has terminal and cashier filters and only the Project board has a project filter; there is no salesperson, vendor, warehouse, journal or stage filter elsewhere. On Odoo 19 the Inventory board's Stock Value, Average Product Value and value charts read zero, because they look for a stock valuation table that Odoo 19 no longer has. The Sales board's Pending Delivery card is always zero. Dates and the POS busy-hours chart are worked out in server time (UTC), so late-evening sales in the Gulf can land on the next day. The chapters cover each board's own limits.</Callout>
