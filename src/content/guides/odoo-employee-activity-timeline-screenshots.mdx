The data a tracker collects is only as useful as the page you read it on. A table of 1,492 rows tells a manager nothing; a coloured bar for every hour of the day tells them in five seconds that the morning went on the report and the afternoon on meetings. This chapter covers the pages you read every day: My Activity for one person and one day, Work History for one person over weeks, the raw segments behind them, and screenshots for the companies that switch them on.

## My Activity: one person, one day

Open **Work Tracker > My Activity**. Every employee lands on their own day. If today has no data yet, the page jumps once to the latest day that has some. Arrows move a day back or forward, **Today** returns, and a date field jumps anywhere.

The tiles under the name give the day in seven numbers: **Active**, **Idle**, **Productive**, **Neutral**, **Distracting**, **Effort** and **Focus sessions**. With the Attendance bridge two more appear, Attendance and Gap.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-employee-activity-timeline-screenshots/timeline.webp" alt="Hour by hour activity timeline in the Odoo work tracker from 08:00 to 17:00, with green productive, blue neutral and red distracting bars, a hatched idle lunch hour and an activity strip underneath" width={1440} height={320} caption="The timeline for the demo's operations manager: lunch at noon is idle, a quarter hour after 16:15 is distracting." />

The **timeline** has one row per hour of the employee's day, in their own timezone, which the page names at the right. Each bar is a segment in its category's colour; idle is hatched grey and time outside working hours hatched beige. Hover a bar to see the application, the window title and the times. Under it, an **Activity** strip shows keyboard and mouse events per active minute, hour by hour. It is a count, never content.

Under the timeline, **Time per application** lists up to fifteen applications, longest first, without idle time. **Effort per task** fills in when the Project bridge is installed and segments carry a task.

At the bottom, the page tells the employee *Your manager sees this same page for you, nothing more*, next to a link, **Ask HR to delete this day**. That link does not delete anything. It creates a to-do on the employee record for their HR responsible, or for every tracker manager when there is none.

## Looking at someone else

A Manager has a search box at the top right. It offers **All employees** first, then everyone in the company who has a user, with job titles and a You tag on the manager's own name. Typing matches name, work email, department and job title.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-employee-activity-timeline-screenshots/employee-picker.webp" alt="Employee picker on the Odoo work tracker My Activity page open with All employees at the top and demo employees with their job titles below" width={680} height={425} caption="The picker as a Manager. A User only ever sees their own name." />

Pick a person and the same page loads for them, with an **Employee record** button and **Open segments** for the rows behind the page. **All employees** switches to a one-line-per-person table for the chosen day: active, productive share, effort, focus, first and last, with totals. Clicking a name opens that person's Work History. The choice is remembered in the browser.

A User who somehow asks for another employee's page is simply shown their own. The server decides whose page it is, not the browser.

## Work History: one person over weeks

**Work History** lines up one employee's days, newest first, for 7, 14 or 30 days ending on a date you pick. That is where patterns show: the Friday that always ends at three, the week with no focus, a day with attendance but no activity.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-employee-activity-timeline-screenshots/work-history.webp" alt="Work History page in the Odoo work tracker for one employee over 14 days with totals for active hours, productive, effort, focus sessions and days tracked, and a day by day table with productive bars" width={1440} height={595} caption="Fourteen days for one demo employee: five tracked days, 37:45 active, 32.8 hours of effort." />

The tiles total the period: active hours, productive, effort, focus sessions and days tracked. Each row shows active time, a productive bar with its percentage, effort, focus and first and last activity. Untracked days and weekends show a dash. Below the table come **Categories**, **Top applications** (eight) and, with the Project bridge, **Tasks in this period**. With the bridges installed the table also gets Attendance, Gap and Timesheet columns.

Managers reach any employee's history from the picker, the **Work history** smart button on the employee form, the clock icon on each row of the employee list, or **Open work history** on the employee kanban card.

## Segments: the raw material

Every figure traces back to **segments**: one stretch of time in one window. Open **Work Tracker > Segments** and remove the Today filter. The list shows start, seconds, employee, application, window title, category, task and idle, and groups by employee, date, hour, application, category, device, task or project. Filters pick out active, idle, productive, neutral, distracting and outside working hours.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-employee-activity-timeline-screenshots/segment.webp" alt="Segment form in the Odoo work tracker showing employee, device, start and end time, seconds, idle, outside working hours, input events, application Code.exe, window title, category Productive, date and hour" width={1440} height={380} caption="One segment: a Visual Studio Code window, six minutes, 180 input events." />

**Input events** counts the one-second checks that saw new keyboard or mouse input in that segment. It shows how busy the hands were, never what they did.

Segments are read only for everyone. Only a paired agent creates them, and a User asks HR rather than deleting their own.

## Fixing the classification from the list

The fastest way to get the rules right is to read a day and correct what is wrong. Tick the segments, open **Actions**, and choose **Mark as productive**, **Mark as neutral** or **Mark as distracting**.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-employee-activity-timeline-screenshots/mark-segments.webp" alt="Segments list in the Odoo work tracker with two rows selected and the Actions menu open showing Mark as productive, Mark as neutral, Mark as distracting, Request deletion and Re-apply rules" width={1000} height={520} caption="Actions on selected segments. Marking opens a new rule already filled in." />

Odoo opens a new rule built from the selected applications: an equals rule for one application, a regular expression for several, at sequence 5 so it is tried before the starter rules. Save it and the days of those segments are classified again straight away, instead of waiting for the hourly rollup. **Re-apply rules** does the same for a selection with the rules as they stand, and **Request deletion** asks HR to remove the rows.

<Callout title="New rules do not rewrite history on their own">A changed rule applies to new segments. To push it into the past, use Re-apply rules on a selection, or Configuration > Rebuild or re-apply rules for a date range of up to a year. Daily summaries whose segments retention has already deleted keep their figures.</Callout>

## Screenshots

Screenshots are off by default, and for most teams they should stay off. The timeline already says which application was open; a picture adds little and costs trust. Some companies want them anyway, for example for client billing disputes, so the module supports them with limits.

When an administrator turns **Screenshots** on in Settings, the agent captures the main monitor every 10 minutes by default (1 to 1,440), scales it to 0.25 of its size (0.1 to 1) and blurs it **inside the agent, before the image leaves memory**. All monitors, off by default, captures each monitor with its number.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-employee-activity-timeline-screenshots/screenshots.webp" alt="Screenshots kanban in the Odoo work tracker with blurred desktop captures for demo employees, each labelled with the employee name, time and monitor number" width={1600} height={1000} caption="Blurred captures, labelled with employee, time and monitor. Pictured on a test database; the live demo has screenshots off." />

A screenshot is visible to the employee it belongs to and to tracker managers, nobody else. Nothing is captured while paused, in private mode, locked or asleep, and none is accepted before the employee has accepted the consent text. Screenshots are deleted after 14 days by default. Screenshots on the employee form open grouped by day.

## How the day is built

All these pages read one summary row per employee per day, rebuilt every hour from the segments that changed. Active time is everything not idle and not outside working hours. The effort score multiplies each segment's seconds by its category's weight. A focus session is a run of at least 25 minutes of work with no idle, no distracting segment and no gap over a minute. When two PCs of the same person overlap, their segments are merged before anything is added, so an hour on two machines counts once.
