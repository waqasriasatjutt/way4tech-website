Ask a manager of office staff where last week's hours went and you usually get a guess. Timesheets are filled in on Friday from memory, attendance says when people badged in but not what they did, and the project that ran forty hours over budget only shows up when the invoice is short. A work tracker answers the question with data instead of memory. The hard part is doing it in a way your staff will accept, because the same tool that measures focus can easily turn into a camera over someone's shoulder.

WT Work Tracker is built around that tension. A small Windows agent notes which application is in front and whether the PC is in use. Odoo turns that into categories, an effort score, focus sessions, effort per task, suggested timesheet lines and the gap against attendance. It never reads keystrokes, and the page a manager opens about an employee is exactly the page the employee sees about themselves. This guide explains what it does, who it suits, and how to set it up on Odoo 19. I'm Waqas Riasat, an independent Odoo developer in Lahore, and I wrote the module.

## Who this is for

- **Software, design and agency teams** who sell time and need to know which client and which task it went to.
- **Back offices and support teams** where most work happens at a PC and the manager wants to see whether a day was spent in the ticket system or in a browser tab.
- **Remote and hybrid teams** where attendance alone says little about the working day.
- **HR teams** that already run Employees, Attendances and Timesheets in Odoo and want one more figure next to them, not another system.

It is not for shop floors, drivers or field staff, and it is not a way to read someone's messages. If you need that, look elsewhere, and check your local law first.

## What standard Odoo gives you

Odoo 19 has the pieces around this question but not the answer. **Attendances** records check-in and check-out, from a kiosk, a badge or the employee's own button. **Timesheets** lets people book hours to tasks, typed in by hand or started with a timer they have to remember to press. **Project** holds tasks with allocated hours. None of them knows which application was open at 3 pm, whether the PC was in use, or how much of a booked hour was real work. Nothing classifies Excel as work and YouTube as not, and nothing proposes the timesheet for you.

## What the work tracker adds

There are two halves. The **agent** on each Windows PC samples the window in front every five seconds, joins the samples into segments and posts them to your Odoo once a minute, with a local queue so a Wi-Fi drop loses nothing. The **module** in Odoo holds everything else:

1. **Invite and pairing.** One click on the employee form mails a link that pairs the agent without typing anything. A one-time code is the fallback.
2. **Rules and categories.** Applications and window titles become Productive, Neutral or Distracting, by contains, equals or a regular expression. Eighteen starter rules cover editors, Office, Odoo in a browser, GitHub, Jira, Figma, meetings, video and social sites, games and the lock screen.
3. **One summary per person per day.** Active, idle, productive, neutral and distracting time, an effort score in weighted hours, focus sessions of 25 minutes or more, and the first and last activity.
4. **Pages people actually read.** My Activity shows one day hour by hour, Work History lines up weeks, and Team gives the manager a kanban, list, graph and pivot of everyone.
5. **Privacy controls.** Consent before tracking, window titles on or off, screenshots off by default and blurred when on, retention periods, and a button for the employee to ask HR to delete a day.

The **effort score** is the figure that makes this more than a stopwatch. Each category has a weight between 0 and 1: productive counts in full, neutral counts half, distracting counts nothing. Six hours twenty of productive work, an hour and a half of neutral meetings and a quarter hour of distraction is 7.08 hours of effort out of 8:05 active. You set the weights, so a support team can count its chat tool as productive and a developer team can count it as half.

## How a setup goes

The order that works, and the chapter that covers each step:

1. **Settings.** Keep the defaults to start: sample every 5 seconds, sync every 60, idle after 180, consent required, titles stored, screenshots off, segments kept 90 days. Edit the consent text to name your company and your reasons. See [privacy and settings](/odoo-guides/odoo-employee-monitoring-privacy-settings/).
2. **Rules.** Read the eighteen starter rules and adjust them for the tools your people use. This is the most useful hour you will spend on the product.
3. **Invite.** Every tracked employee needs a linked Odoo user, because the agent signs in as that user. Invite one person first, yourself ideally.
4. **Install the agent.** The [desktop agent setup](/odoo-guides/odoo-work-tracker-windows-agent-setup/) chapter follows the installer, the invite link, consent and the tray menu step by step.
5. **Read a day.** After an hour, open My Activity and check the classification. Mark what is wrong straight from the segment list. The [activity timeline and screenshots](/odoo-guides/odoo-employee-activity-timeline-screenshots/) chapter covers every screen.
6. **Connect the rest.** If you run Project, Timesheets or Attendances, read [attendance and timesheets](/odoo-guides/odoo-tracked-time-attendance-timesheets/) and [Project and Project Pro](/odoo-guides/odoo-time-tracking-project-tasks/).

## Who sees what

There are two levels. A **User** sees their own segments, days, devices and screenshots and nothing else, enforced by record rules on every list. A **Manager** sees the whole company, invites people, pairs and revokes devices, writes rules and suggests timesheets. An Odoo administrator is a Manager automatically and is the only one who opens Settings. Nothing a User does can create or change activity: only a paired agent writes it.

<Callout title="There is no team level">A Manager sees every employee of the company, not just their own department. If team leads should only see their own people, keep them at the User level and have HR share the figures. The module does not split visibility by department.</Callout>

The employee keeps control on their own PC. The tray menu has Pause, a Private mode that ends by itself after 30 minutes (you set the length), Sync now, Settings with an Unpair button, and Quit. Every pause and quit is recorded as an event, so the dashboard shows the gaps honestly rather than hiding them.

## The devices

Every paired PC shows up under Configuration > Devices with its hostname, Windows version, agent version, last contact, the size of its unsent queue and whether consent is recorded. Revoke one and its next call is refused. A check every fifteen minutes flags a device that was tracking and has gone silent for two hours inside working hours, as a to-do for the employee's HR responsible, so a crashed agent or a dead network gets noticed the same afternoon.

## Limitations

- **Windows only.** There is no Mac, Linux or phone agent.
- **No web addresses.** The agent reads what a browser puts in its window title, not the URL.
- **No team-level visibility.** Manager means the whole company.
- **Effort, not proof.** A tracker measures the PC, not the work. Meetings, calls and thinking away from the screen land in idle time or the attendance gap. The idle prompt lets employees keep that time as work, and you should read the figures with it in mind.
- **The rollup runs hourly.** Today's summary can be up to an hour behind the raw segments.
