An events business does not lose money on the night. It loses it months earlier: a hall held for a client who never paid the deposit, a package sold forty times without anyone adding up what the canapes cost, a caterer booked whose insurance lapsed in March, a final headcount that arrived two days before the kitchen needed it. The work is mostly paperwork with a deadline, and most agencies keep it in a diary, a spreadsheet per wedding and a folder of supplier PDFs. This guide covers what a planner, a wedding agency or a venue running its own functions needs from Odoo, what standard Odoo gives you, and how the Event Management module runs the whole file on Odoo 19 Community.

## What an events business actually needs

Strip it back and the job has six parts:

1. **A diary that cannot double book.** A hall is not free the minute the last guest leaves. It has to be stripped and reset, so the next booking needs a gap, and a venue with two suites can run two functions at once but not three.
2. **Packages that are costed before they are sold.** Per head for a dinner, a flat fee for a marquee, a contracted minimum so a party that shrinks still pays for what it booked, and the cost of every inclusion so you know the margin.
3. **Suppliers with their paperwork in date.** Public liability insurance and trade licences expire. Booking a caterer whose cover ran out is the kind of mistake a venue notices at the door.
4. **A run sheet for the day.** Timed cues with one owner each, the cues that cannot slip marked, and the actual times recorded so the debrief has numbers on it.
5. **A guest list the kitchen can cook from.** RSVPs, seats, tables that cannot be overfilled, and dietary totals by the final numbers deadline.
6. **Money tied to the date.** A deposit that holds the date, stage payments with due dates, and a margin per event after supplier and crew cost.

## What standard Odoo does and does not do

Odoo 19 Community ships an **Events** app. Its manifest describes it as covering trainings, conferences, meetings, exhibitions and registrations, with emails that confirm each registration ([odoo/odoo event manifest, 19.0](https://github.com/odoo/odoo/blob/19.0/addons/event/__manifest__.py)). The [Odoo 19 Events documentation](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/marketing/events.html) goes through tickets, registrations, attendee check-in and selling tickets on the website. The [Odoo Events overview](/modules/events/) on this site covers it in more detail.

That is the right tool for a conference that sells seats to the public. A wedding or a corporate dinner is the other way round: one client, one contract, a room to hold and a lot of suppliers. For that kind of work the Events app has:

- No venue record with capacity per layout, turnaround time, curfew or licences, and no refusal of a clash.
- No package priced per head with a contracted minimum and costed inclusions.
- No supplier list with insurance expiry, no supplier quotes and no event budget.
- No timed run sheet, staffing ratios or crew call times.
- No seating plan with dietary totals, and no deposit or stage payment schedule.

You can bend Sales, Project and Calendar into some of this, but the checks that matter (refusing a date without a deposit, refusing a quote from an uninsured supplier) need code.

## How an event moves

Every event starts as an **Enquiry** and moves only by the buttons on it: Proposal Sent, Confirmed, On the Day, Delivered, Closed, with Cancelled to the side. Writing the status straight onto the record, through an import or by dragging a card on the board, is refused. Each button has a check behind it:

- **Send Proposal** needs a venue and something to charge for. It prices the package for the headcount, turns the package inclusions into an estimated budget, builds a three stage payment schedule and emails the client proposal.
- **Confirm the Date** checks the venue holds the licences the event type needs (alcohol, public entertainment) and that they are in date on the day, refuses an event that runs past the venue curfew unless an extension is ticked, and by default refuses until the deposit is marked paid.
- **Open Up** on the morning refuses an event with no run sheet or with roster slots nobody is in. Reserved equipment is marked as gone out.
- **Event Delivered** skips any cues still open, with a reason, and records how many were worked through.
- **Close the File** refuses while any equipment is still out or any budget line has no settled cost, because the margin is not real until both are done.
- **Cancel** needs a reason, is refused while a posted invoice exists against the event, rejects open supplier quotes with the same reason and releases reserved equipment.

## How to set it up in Odoo

1. Install Event Management. Accounting installs with it if it is not there already. Installing with demo data gives you a sample agency with venues, packages, suppliers and a season of events at every stage.
2. Open **Configuration, Settings** in the Events app: deposit percentage, whether the deposit holds the date, how many days before the event final numbers are due, payment grace days, the overbooking allowance, the document expiry warning and when to chase equipment.
3. Add your venues and their layouts under **Configuration, Venues**. Add your event types under **Configuration, Event Types**, with the licences each needs.
4. Build your packages under **Configuration, Packages** with their inclusions and what each costs you per head.
5. Add staffing roles with their ratios and rates under **Configuration, Staffing Roles**, then your crew under **Configuration, Crew**.
6. Add supplier categories and suppliers, with insurance and licence expiry dates, and approve the ones you use.
7. Turn on the four scheduled jobs (payment chase, final numbers request, supplier paperwork check, equipment chase) once your outgoing mail server works. They ship switched off.
8. Run one real enquiry end to end, from proposal to closed file, before moving the diary over.

<Callout title="Four roles, and crew never see the money">The module has four groups. **Crew** see only the events they are rostered on, their own call times and the run sheet. **Event Coordinators** run the day: guests, seating, run sheet, roster and equipment, but cannot price the job or see the margin. **Event Planners** quote, book suppliers and own the budget and payment schedule. **Events Managers** set up venues, packages, the approved supplier list, rates and reporting. Assign them before go-live.</Callout>

## Limitations

These are what the code does not do, so you can judge the fit before you install:

- **No ticketing or public registration.** Use Odoo's own Events app for that.
- **No online RSVP or guest portal.** Replies are entered by your team. The Invitation Sent button records that an invitation went out; it does not send one.
- **No client portal or online proposal signing.** The proposal is emailed as a message and printed as a PDF.
- **No purchase orders or vendor bills.** Supplier costs reach the budget from accepted quotes and are settled with the figure from the supplier invoice by hand.
- **Payments are marked by hand.** Stage invoices are raised into Accounting, but a milestone is marked paid with its own button; it does not follow the invoice payment by itself.
- **No drag and drop floor plan.** Tables are a list with capacity, shape and position notes.
