Ask a farmer what a field of wheat made last year and the answer usually comes from three places that do not agree: the spray book in the tractor cab, the store book that says what was bought, and the accountant's figures six months later. The spray book is what an inspector asks for, the store book is what the money went on, and the accounts are what the bank sees. This guide covers what a mixed farm has to record, where standard Odoo stops, and how our Farm Management module keeps all three in one place on Odoo 19 Community.

## What a mixed farm has to record

| Record | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Each field: cropped area, parcel reference, soil, pH | Everything per hectare divides by the area; the parcel reference is what a scheme and an inspector use |
| Each pass over a field, with people, machine hours and inputs | The cost of the crop, and the evidence of what went on |
| Every application: product, active substance, rate, batch, interval | The statutory record, and the date the field is clear to cut |
| Each harvest: tonnes, moisture, grade, buyer, lot | What is sold, what is paid for, and the trace back to the ground |
| Herds, animals, breeding, milk, grazing | The other half of a mixed farm |

The point of keeping them together is that one entry serves all three needs. The spray booked by the driver is the application record and the cost on the crop at the same time.

## What standard Odoo does and does not do

Odoo 19 Community has products, stock locations, purchase and sales, and invoicing. It has no field, no crop, no season and no record of what went on which ground. A pre-harvest interval, a spray wind limit, a moisture shrink on a grain load or a gestation table are nowhere in it. Farms that try to run on standard Odoo end up with the agronomy in spreadsheets and only the money in Odoo, which is exactly the split this module removes.

## Fields, crops and seasons

A farm has a code that prefixes every number it issues, a holding reference, an input store, a grain store and a sales journal. Each field carries its cropped area, the land parcel reference and soil details. A crop is a named variety with a rotation group, growing days, a target yield, an expected price, a seed rate and a pH range. A season is a harvest year running from the autumn drilling to the following harvest; once closed it takes no new cycles.

## Crop cycles and the gross margin

A crop cycle is one crop on one field for one season. Planning it checks the dates against the season and the area against the field, and writes rotation and pH warnings into the record. Every pass booked against it adds labour, machinery and input cost; seed and other costs such as rent and drying go on the cycle. Harvests bring the tonnage and the revenue, and the cycle shows gross margin, margin per hectare and cost per tonne. A cycle that comes in under the yield alert is flagged.

## Spray records and pre-harvest intervals

Every input line on a pass is also the statutory application record: product, active substance, quantity, rate per hectare, batch number off the container, target and the interval off the label. The module works out the date the field is clear and refuses to confirm a harvest before it. Two more label rules hold the same way: a rate above the maximum dose per hectare is refused, and a spray cannot be booked as done on a day the weather log says was over the wind limit.

## Harvests, lots and the trace back

A harvest is a load or a day off a cycle. Grain above the target moisture is shrunk back to it, because that is the weight the contract pays on, and the value follows the shrunk tonnage. Each harvest gets a lot reference from the farm, the parcel and the record number, is invoiced to the buyer at the shrunk tonnage. Its traceability tab and the printed harvest passport list everything applied to that ground from drilling to the cut.

## Livestock and grass

Herds and flocks hold individual animals on their official ear tags, with dam and sire, weights and a daily gain figure. Breeding records work out the expected calving from the service date and the species, and recording a calving creates the calves. Milk, eggs and wool go into a production log with butterfat, protein and cell count. Paddocks carry an area, a sward and a stocking limit; turning a herd back on before the paddock has had its rest is refused, and so is putting on more head than it carries.

<Callout title="What the module does not do">
There is no subsidy claim form, no field map, no link to a weather station and no official movement reporting: parcel references, ear tags and exits are recorded for you to use in those. Buying inputs is done in Purchase. In the current build the stock moves for inputs and harvests are not made on Odoo 19 (the code looks for a location reference Odoo 19 no longer has), so keep the store in Inventory until that is fixed. The nightly jobs for crops coming ready and calvings due ship switched off.
</Callout>
