A forwarder's margin is decided the moment the rate goes out. If the quote leaves off terminal handling, sells documentation at a price nobody checked, or rates a light air shipment on the wrong divisor, no amount of good operations will put the money back. The usual fix is a spreadsheet of buy rates next to the quote. The freight module puts the buy rate and the sell rate on the same line of the quotation instead, shows the margin against the lane's target while you type, and carries the rates onto the job file when the customer says yes. This chapter covers raising, sending, winning and losing a quotation.

## Raising a quotation

1. Open **Sales, Quotations** and press **New**.
2. Pick the **Customer**, and type a **Contact Name** if the enquiry came from one person.
3. Set **Mode** (Air, Sea FCL, Sea LCL or Road) and **Direction**.
4. Set **From** and **To**. The **Trade Lane** fills itself in from the two countries.
5. Enter what you know about the cargo: **Pieces**, **Gross Weight**, **Volume** and the **Commodity**. **Chargeable Weight** is worked out from the mode, with the same divisors the job file uses.
6. Set the **Valid Until** date and **Transit (days)**. Both print on the quotation and customers ask about both.
7. Add the rate lines, below, and save.

## Rate lines with two sides

Each line on the **Rates** tab is one charge with a cost side and a sell side.

1. Press **Add a line** and pick the **Charge Code**. The description, basis and standard cost and sell come across from the rate card.
2. Check the **Basis** and **Quantity**. Two forty foot boxes is a quantity of two on a per container line; a per kilo line takes the chargeable weight.
3. Type the **Cost Rate** and **Sell Rate**, each in its own currency if they differ.
4. Repeat for every charge the customer will pay. A quote that leaves out terminal handling is not cheaper, it is wrong.

Put the zero cost lines in too. Documentation and the customs entry fee often cost nothing to buy and sell for real money, and leaving them off understates both the price and the margin.

## Margin against the lane target

As lines go on, **Amount Cost**, **Amount Sell**, **Gross Profit** and **Margin (%)** update. If the margin falls below the target on the trade lane, or below the company minimum when the lane has none, the quotation shows an Under Target ribbon.

The flag is a prompt, not a block. There are good reasons to quote under target: a first shipment from a customer who will bring more, or a backhaul that would otherwise run empty. The point is that the decision is made in front of the number, not found three months later in the gross profit report. The margin is also a column on the quotation list, so a thin quote is visible before anyone opens it.

## Sending it

1. Press **Print** to produce the PDF and read the validity date and transit time on it.
2. Press **Send**. The quotation moves to Sent and the date is recorded.

## Won: the file opens with the rates on it

1. Open the quotation and press **Won, Open a File**.
2. The system opens a job file, copies every rate line onto it as a charge, and takes you to the file.
3. The quotation shows which file it became, and the file shows which quotation it came from.

The rates stay exactly as quoted. The quantities are recalculated against what is actually on the file, so if the customer quoted two containers and shipped one, the per container lines come down to one. This is what stops the invoice disagreeing with the quote.

A quotation whose validity date has passed cannot be won. The system names the date and asks you to requote. A rate from six weeks ago is not a rate, and winning against it only moves the loss onto the file.

## Lost, and why

1. Press **Lost**.
2. Pick a **Lost Reason**: beaten on rate, transit time, service or schedule, credit terms, no answer, shipment did not happen, or other.
3. Write a line in **Lost Note**. "Beaten by a direct service, nine days quicker" is useful in three months. "Too expensive" is not.

The reason is asked for at the moment the quote is marked lost, because that is the only time anyone remembers it.

## Expiry

A quotation still in Draft or Sent after its validity date is moved to Expired by a nightly scheduled job. It is not lost, because nobody said no; it is simply no longer a live rate. Expired quotations are kept out of the lost analysis for that reason, so the report below measures real decisions by customers.

## The Why We Lose report

**Sales, Why We Lose** groups lost quotations by reason, with the revenue and gross profit each reason cost you.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-freight-quotation/why-we-lose.webp" alt="Why We Lose pivot in Odoo grouping lost freight quotations by reason with amount sold and gross profit for beaten on rate, transit time and service or schedule" width={1600} height={359} caption="In the demo, service or schedule cost more lost revenue than rate did." />

Most forwarders believe they lose on rate. Measured over a quarter it is often transit time, schedule or credit terms, and those are fixed by different people: a conversation with the carrier, or with the finance team about limits, rather than another price cut. The report is only as good as the reasons people pick, so make choosing a real reason part of how the sales team works.

## A salesperson's routine

- Quote with every charge on it, including the ones that cost nothing to buy.
- Set a validity date you are willing to be held to and a transit time you have checked.
- Watch the margin while you type, not after.
- Mark a quotation lost the day you know, with the real reason.
- Answer the activities on your unbilled files, covered in the [reports chapter](/odoo-guides/odoo-freight-gross-profit-reports/). They are your revenue.

A salesperson with the Freight Sales role sees only quotations where they are the salesperson, and files where they are the salesperson or opened the file themselves. That is a record rule, so it holds on lists, searches and reports. If someone says quotations are missing from their list, that is usually why.

When the customer confirms, the work moves to the [job file](/odoo-guides/odoo-freight-job-file/).
