A tanker brings tens of thousands of pounds of fuel onto the site in under an hour, and the driver wants a signature before he leaves. Whatever was not checked in that hour is very hard to claim afterwards. Two things decide whether the site paid for what it received: whether the litres on the ticket and the litres measured are on the same temperature basis, and whether somebody dipped the tank before the hose went on and after it came off. This chapter of the [petrol pump management series](/odoo-guides/odoo-petrol-pump-management/) takes a delivery through the Petrol Pump Management module.

## What a delivery records

The header holds the site, the supplier (terminal or oil company), the date and time of discharge, the tanker plate, the driver, the **delivery note** (bill of lading) number and the **seal numbers** as they were on arrival. A broken seal before discharge is a claim, not a paperwork problem, so it belongs on the record.

Below that is one line per compartment, each into one tank:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| On the Ticket | Standard litres the terminal says it loaded |
| Observed | Gross litres at the temperature it arrived at |
| Density | At 15 C, from the loading ticket |
| Temperature | Product temperature at discharge |
| Correction Factor and Standard | Worked out from the three above |
| Dip Before and Dip After | Tank readings either side of the discharge |
| Dip Gain and Variance | What the tank gained, and that less the standard litres |
| Cost and Duty per litre | From the terminal's invoice, kept apart so margin reads honestly |

## Temperature correction

Fuel grows with heat. The module takes the density at 15 C and picks the ASTM table 54B coefficients: one set for petrol and other light products below 770 kg/m3, another for diesel and heavier products. From them it works out how much the product expands per degree and applies it, in the ASTM form rather than a straight line, across the gap between the discharge temperature and the reference temperature in the settings (15 C by default). The result is the **Correction Factor**, and Observed times the factor is **Standard**.

Standard litres are what goes onto the tank's book stock and what the bill is checked against. A diesel compartment discharged at 9 C shows fewer observed litres than were loaded; the same load on a summer afternoon shows more. Either way, standard litres put the ticket and the tank on the same footing. The setting **Correct Deliveries for Temperature** can be turned off, in which case standard equals observed.

The module also refuses figures that cannot be real: a density outside 500 to 1200 kg/m3 and a temperature outside minus 40 to 70 C.

## Before the tanker is booked

The order to the terminal should be sized against room in the ground, not against the last order. Each tank shows its **Ullage**: the litres left between book stock and the safe fill line. That is the figure to give the terminal, and it is the same figure Start Discharge checks against, so a load ordered from it will not be refused at the hose. On a busy site that sells from a tank all the time the tanker is on its way, ullage only grows, which leaves a margin rather than a spill.

## Taking the load in

1. **Draft.** Fill in the header and the compartments. Choosing a tank fills Dip Before from its last dip; check it against the stick.
2. **Start Discharge.** Refused without a delivery note number, without compartments, with an empty compartment, or when a compartment's standard litres will not fit under the tank's safe fill line ("send the balance back on the tanker"). It creates a Before Delivery dip on each tank and moves the delivery to Discharging.
3. **Dip after.** Once the hose is off, type Dip After on each compartment. A dip that went down over a delivery is refused.
4. **Post to the Tank.** Creates the After Delivery dips, adds the standard litres to each tank's book stock and moves the delivery to Posted.

## A short delivery

The delivery is flagged short when the dip gain falls below the standard litres by more than half a per cent of them or 20 litres, whichever is more. Posting a short delivery writes the shortfall and the delivery note number on its chatter, so the claim goes to the terminal the same day with the before and after dips behind it.

The goods received note prints the same evidence line by line: observed litres, density, temperature, correction factor, standard litres, the dips and the variance.

<Callout title="A short delivery also shows in wet stock">
The tank's book stock goes up by the standard litres on the ticket, while the dip shows what actually arrived. If the shortfall is not settled with the terminal, the week's wet stock variance carries it, and its cause should be recorded as Short Delivery.
</Callout>

## The bill

On a posted delivery, **Create Bill** raises a draft vendor bill to the supplier on the site's purchase journal: one line per compartment, standard litres at cost plus duty, with the delivery note as the reference and the delivery as the source. The Vendor Bill button opens it. From there it is an ordinary Odoo bill, posted and paid as any other.

## Refused loads

A tanker turned away, for example for a broken compartment seal, is cancelled before it is discharged and the reason written on it. A posted delivery cannot be cancelled because its litres are already on the books, and a delivery whose bill has been posted has to be credited instead.
