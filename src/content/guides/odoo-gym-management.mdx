A gym does not usually go broke on one bad month. It leaks: a member who stopped paying in March still swiping in during June, a three week "holiday freeze" that somehow became three months, a spin class with twelve names on the waitlist while four booked riders never turn up, a personal trainer paid for ten sessions when the member only used six. Each of those is a small number. Together they are the margin. A club system earns its keep by making those leaks visible and, where it can, refusing them at the moment they happen. This guide explains what a fitness club needs from Odoo, what standard Odoo 19 gives you, and how the Way4Tech Gym Management module fills the gap. The four chapters after it go through each part step by step.

## What a gym actually needs

Strip out the brochure words and a club needs five things from its system:

1. **A contract with rules, not a membership flag.** A plan has a length, a price per period, a billing rhythm, a joining fee and a limit on how long it can be put on hold. The member's contract inherits those rules, and the end date should move by itself when a hold is taken.
2. **Invoices that raise themselves.** A monthly plan has to be invoiced every month without somebody remembering. Annual and up front plans need one invoice, with the joining fee on the first one only.
3. **A front desk that can say no, with a reason.** Expired, on hold, unpaid, out of credits. If the desk only ever sees "denied", staff learn to wave everybody through.
4. **A timetable that fills itself and polices itself.** Recurring slots written into the diary weeks ahead, capacity per class, a waitlist that moves up when someone cancels, and a late cancellation rule so a place is not held for nothing.
5. **Personal training sold as a block.** Ten sessions sold, each one delivered against the block and against the trainer, with an expiry date and the trainer's pay worked out per session.

On top of those sits the member file: a signed health declaration, emergency contact, consent flags, body measurements and a sense of who is drifting away.

## What standard Odoo does and does not do

Odoo 19 Community gives you contacts, products, the Invoicing app, Discuss and the activity tools. You can sell a membership as a product on an invoice, and you can keep a contact list of members. That is where it stops:

- No membership plan with duration, holds, credits or age limits.
- No recurring invoices. Odoo's [Subscriptions app](/modules/subscriptions/) does recurring billing, but it is an Enterprise app and it knows nothing about gym holds or door access.
- No check-in against a contract, no occupancy figure, no visit history.
- No class timetable, booking capacity, waitlist or no-show count.
- No personal training packages or trainer pay.

You can get part of the way with Enterprise apps and Studio, but the rules that matter (refusing entry when a member is in arrears, refusing a hold past the allowance, moving the waitlist up) need code.

## The module and what it covers

[Gym Management](https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/19.0/wt_gym_management/) (technical name `wt_gym_management`, version 19.0.1.0.0) is one application, USD 249, built for Odoo 19 Community. It depends on `base`, `mail`, `product` and `account`, so it installs next to the standard Invoicing app and needs nothing from Enterprise. It appears in Odoo as a **Fitness Club** app with five menus: Members, Classes, Personal Training, Team and Reporting, plus Configuration for managers.

### Who sees what

The module adds three roles under a Fitness Club privilege, each including the one before it:

- **Trainer**: the classes and personal training menus. A trainer linked to an Odoo user sees their own class roster rather than everybody's.
- **Front Desk**: adds contracts, the check-in list and the team menu, and sees the whole timetable.
- **Club Manager**: adds arrears, the at-risk list, reporting and configuration. Medical notes on the member file are visible to managers only.

## How the parts fit together

Everything hangs off the **member**. A member belongs to a home club, has a contact in Odoo that is invoiced, and holds one running contract at a time. The module refuses to activate a second contract while another one is active or on hold.

## The scheduled jobs that do the routine work

Most of the value is in what happens overnight without anyone clicking. The module installs seven scheduled actions:

- **Expire and renew contracts** (daily): an active contract past its end date is expired, or renewed if Auto Renew is ticked.
- **Raise recurring membership invoices** (daily): every active contract billed monthly, quarterly or annually whose next invoice date has arrived gets its invoice. If one fails, the reason is posted on the contract and the job carries on.
- **Send renewal reminders** (daily): members whose contract ends in the configured number of days (14 by default) get an email.
- **Start and end contract holds** (daily): a hold whose start date has come begins, and one that has run out ends, putting the member back on the contract.
- **Extend the class timetable** (daily): recurring slots are written into the diary as far ahead as the club wants (4 weeks by default).
- **Close finished classes** (every 2 hours): booked members not marked present become no-shows.
- **Lapse unused PT packages** (daily): packages past their expiry date are marked expired.

## Setting it up, in order

1. **Install** Gym Management from the Odoo Apps store on an Odoo 19 database that has Invoicing.
2. **Settings** (Fitness Club, Configuration, Settings): the renewal reminder window, late cancellation hours, PT package validity in months and how many weeks of timetable to generate.
3. **Clubs**: one per site, with a short code (it prefixes member and contract numbers), opening hours, capacity and, if each club invoices into its own sales journal, that journal.
4. **Studios**: the rooms classes run in, with their capacity.
5. **Products**: a service product per plan and one for the joining fee, with the right tax. Plans invoice these products, so the income account and tax come from them.
6. **Membership plans**, **class types**, **trainers** and **recurring slots**, then generate the timetable.
7. **Users**: give each person Trainer, Front Desk or Club Manager, and link trainers to their user so they see their own roster.
8. **Members**: import or create them, record the health declaration, and sell each one a contract.

Plan the order of steps 5 and 6 before migrating members: a contract takes its price and joining fee from the plan when you pick it, and every invoice after that uses the plan's product.

## Is it the right fit?

It fits a single club or a small group that wants contracts, the door, the timetable and PT in the same database as its accounting, on Community, with someone at a desk. It is a weaker fit for a chain that sells everything online or needs turnstile integration on day one; those need a portal or device work on top.
