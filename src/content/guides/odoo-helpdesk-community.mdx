Support usually starts in a shared inbox. Three people read the same email, two answer it, and the customer who wrote on Monday about a fridge that will not cool is still waiting on Thursday because everyone assumed someone else had it. Nobody can say how long customers wait, or whether the promise on the website ("we reply within a working day") is ever kept. A helpdesk fixes this by turning every request into a ticket with an owner, a place on a board and a deadline. This guide explains what that takes, and how to run it on Odoo 19 Community with our Helpdesk and Support Ticket Management module.

## What a support desk needs

Whatever the business, a desk that works has the same parts:

- **one record per request**, with a reference the customer can quote, so a conversation never splits across inboxes;
- **an owner**: the ticket is assigned to one agent, and unassigned work is visible;
- **stages**: new, being worked, waiting on the customer, solved, so anyone can see where things stand;
- **a promise and a clock**: how fast each kind of ticket must be handled, and a flag when that is missed;
- **more than one way in**: email for most customers, a portal for those who prefer to log in, and the agent typing a ticket during a phone call;
- **a closing reason and a rating**, so the manager learns something from finished work.

## What Odoo Community does and does not cover

Odoo Community has Discuss, contacts and the customer portal, but no helpdesk. The Helpdesk app is part of Odoo Enterprise. Without it, Community users end up handling support in a mailbox or in the CRM pipeline, which has no SLA clock and no customer portal for tickets.

| Need | Odoo Community | With the module |
|---|---|---|
| Ticket with reference and thread | No | Yes, TKT/2026/00001 and the full mail thread |
| Teams and assignment rules | No | Manual, balanced or random per team |
| Board with stages | No | Shared and private stages, closing stages |
| SLA deadlines and breaches | No | Per team, priority and tag, flagged hourly |
| Tickets from email | No | One alias per team |
| Customer portal for tickets | No | List, detail, reply, new ticket, rating |
| Closing reason | No | Close wizard with a required reason |

## Who it is for

Any business that answers customers: retailers and their after-sales service, software and IT support, property and facility managers, schools answering parents, clinics handling patient queries, distributors handling dealer issues. The module is deliberately general. It holds the support process and nothing else, so it fits next to whatever the rest of the business runs in Odoo.

## How it fits together

Everything that decides how a ticket is handled hangs off its **team**: the members, how new tickets are handed out (manually, to the agent with the fewest open tickets, or at random), the email alias, whether customers can raise tickets for it on the portal, any private stages, its SLA policies and an optional auto close for tickets nobody touches. There is no settings page; two teams can work quite differently.

## SLA policies in one paragraph

A policy belongs to a team, covers a minimum priority and above (High also covers Urgent), can be limited to tickets carrying a tag, and names a target stage and a number of hours. When a ticket is created or its team, priority or tags change, the strictest matching policy is attached and the deadline set from the opening time. Reaching the target stage stops the clock. Every change to the ticket, and a scheduled action every hour, keeps the breach flag right, so a ticket nobody opens still turns red. The [SLA chapter](/odoo-guides/odoo-helpdesk-sla-policies/) covers it step by step.

<Callout title="What it does not do">
SLA hours are wall-clock hours, not working hours. There is no public website form (the portal form needs a login; everyone else writes to the email alias), no live chat, no knowledge base and no timesheets on tickets. Ratings are given on the portal page of a closed ticket, reached from the account or from the link in the ticket closed email.
</Callout>

## Email and the portal

Give a team an alias such as support@ your domain, and every email sent to it becomes a ticket in that team; the customer's later replies land on the same ticket. Customers with a portal login see **Support Tickets** on their account page: their own tickets only, with the conversation, a reply box, a form to raise a new one and, once a ticket is closed, a rating from Very Poor to Very Good.

## Setting it up, in order

A desk is usually ready in an afternoon if the decisions are made first:

1. **Teams.** One per group of people who answer a kind of request, for example customer care, technical support and warranty. Add the members, pick the assignment method and give each an alias.
2. **Stages.** Keep the five shared stages unless a team really needs its own column, such as Engineer Visit Booked. Mark which stages close a ticket.
3. **Tags.** A short list the agents will actually use, plus any tag a policy needs to match, such as Business Account.
4. **SLA policies.** Write the promises per team in plain words first, then one policy per promise.
5. **Email.** Set the company's mail domain and the incoming mail, then send a test email to each alias.
6. **Portal.** Give the customers who should see their tickets online a portal login, and write each team's portal description.

Agents need the Handle Tickets role; the person who changes teams, stages and policies needs Configure Helpdesk.
