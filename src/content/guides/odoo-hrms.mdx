An HR department in a company of fifty people spends most of its week on small, repeated jobs. A leave form goes to the wrong manager. Somebody asks how many casual days they have left. A salary advance is agreed in the corridor and forgotten at month end. The payroll spreadsheet is copied from last month and one allowance is wrong for three people. None of this is hard, but every one of those jobs needs the same few facts: who reports to whom, what the policy says, and what was approved. When those facts live in one system, the jobs mostly do themselves.

This guide is about running that system on Odoo 19 Community with the WT HRMS Suite. It starts here with what the product is for and what standard Odoo already gives you, then each chapter takes one area from setup to daily use. Every screen in the guide comes from a fictional company, Crescent Textiles (Pvt) Ltd, a textile mill in Lahore with 20 staff in seven departments, paid in PKR.

## Who this is for

The suite suits a company that wants HR and payroll inside the same Odoo it already uses for sales, stock or accounting, and does not want to pay for Odoo Enterprise just to get payroll. It works well for:

- **Factories and workshops** where attendance drives pay, people work six days a week, and many staff have no email address.
- **Offices and service companies** where managers approve leave, overtime and expenses and HR wants one list of everything waiting.
- **Companies that pay through a bank file**, where finance needs every salary to post to the ledger and every transfer to go to a checked account.

It is written for four readers: the HR manager who sets it up, the line managers who approve, the employees who ask, and the payroll officer who runs the month.

## What standard Odoo does and does not do

Odoo 19 Community already includes the core HR apps. Its source tree on GitHub ([odoo/odoo, 19.0 addons](https://github.com/odoo/odoo/tree/19.0/addons)) has Employees (`hr`), Time Off (`hr_holidays`), Attendances (`hr_attendance`), Recruitment (`hr_recruitment`) and Expenses (`hr_expense`). With them you can keep employee records, run leave requests with an approval per leave type as the [Time Off documentation](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/hr/time_off.html) describes, and record check-ins from a kiosk or the systray as shown in the [Attendances documentation](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/hr/attendances.html).

What Community does not have is payroll. There is no payroll addon in that source tree; payroll in standard Odoo is an Enterprise app. Around it, a working HR department also misses:

- **Leave rules.** Days per year, carry forward limits, months of service before a type can be taken, probation rules, the longest single request, a medical certificate after a number of days, and selling unused leave back for money.
- **One approval engine** for everything that is not leave: overtime, loans, advances, expenses, work from home, resignations, training, shift swaps, with steps such as direct manager, then HR, then a named director.
- **Money that flows by itself.** Approved overtime hours, a loan instalment, an advance recovery or a leave encashment should appear on the next payslip without anybody typing the figure again.
- **The people records HR keeps.** Letters from templates, documents with expiry warnings, assets issued and returned, warnings and appreciations, onboarding and offboarding checklists.
- **A loading route.** Odoo's standard import works one list at a time; a new company needs departments, people, managers, logins, bank accounts, salaries, leave policies and holidays loaded in the right order.

## How the pieces fit

Almost every request works the same way, which is what makes the system quick to learn. An employee raises it from **My Space**, their home page. It walks an approval chain, for example the line manager and then HR. Each approver sees it in their own **To Approve** list and decides with one click. Once approved, anything with money in it is picked up by the next payroll run. Time off is the one exception: it uses Odoo's own Time Off approval, set per leave type, but it still lands in the same To Approve list.

## Who sees what

Decide access before you import, because the import creates the logins. There are four kinds of user. An **employee** is an ordinary internal user. A **line manager** has the same rights; approvals come from being set as the Manager on the team's employee records, not from a role. An **HR officer** gets Employees, Time Off and Attendances at Officer level. An **HR manager** gets Administrator on those three and Recruiter on Recruitment. The payroll officer also needs Invoicing: Accountant or higher to post salaries and trust bank accounts.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-hrms/user-access-rights.webp" alt="Odoo user form for the HR manager, Access Rights tab, with Employees and Time Off set to Administrator under Human Resources" width={1440} height={900} caption="The HR manager's rights. The workbook import sets the Employees level; Time Off, Attendances and Recruitment are set by hand." />

<Callout title="There is no separate payroll role">In this version, running payroll needs Employees: Administrator, and anyone with Employees: Officer can read every payslip. If HR officers should not see salaries, keep them on the plain Employee role and give Employees rights only to the HR manager and the payroll officer.</Callout>

Every internal user counts as a user for your Odoo subscription or hosting plan. Machine operators and drivers can work without a login (HR raises requests for them), with a login on a phone, or only through a punch on an attendance device.

## The chapters

The guide follows the order the work has to happen in. Each chapter is a page of its own:

1. [HR implementation plan and company setup](/odoo-guides/odoo-hr-implementation-plan/): the order of the work, a three week plan, who decides what, the company foundation, and the test script and go-live checklist.
2. [Importing employees into Odoo](/odoo-guides/import-employees-into-odoo/): the one-workbook route, the standard import templates, opening leave balances and what each import error means.
3. [Leave management and approvals](/odoo-guides/odoo-leave-management/): leave types, policies, allocating the year, carry forward, and the approval chains every other request uses.
4. [Attendance, shifts and overtime](/odoo-guides/odoo-attendance-shifts-overtime/): how people check in, correcting punches, the monthly summary, shift rosters and paid overtime.
5. [Running payroll in Odoo Community](/odoo-guides/run-payroll-odoo-community/): salary structures, rules, accounts, trusted bank accounts, the monthly batch, the bank file, loans and a leaver's final pay.
6. [Employee self service](/odoo-guides/odoo-employee-self-service/): My Space for staff and managers, requests, expenses and the payslip portal.
7. [Onboarding and offboarding](/odoo-guides/odoo-employee-onboarding-offboarding/): a new joiner from record to confirmation, letters, documents, assets, appraisals, resignations and reporting lines.
8. [Recruitment, job offers and hiring](/odoo-guides/odoo-recruitment-job-offer-online-acceptance/): a careers page and one apply link to share, the applications board, an offer letter the candidate accepts online, and a hire that fills the employee record and starts onboarding.
9. [Leave policy, lates and unpaid days](/odoo-guides/odoo-leave-policy-late-deduction/): one real company policy built end to end, with a monthly cap on casual leave, half days, unpaid leave, and late arrivals taken from leave first and salary second.

The last two chapters go past the 26 modules of the suite. Hiring uses Odoo's own Community Recruitment app with the Recruitment Plus add-on, and the late and unpaid deductions need the Payroll Unpaid Leave and Late Deductions module. Neither add-on is on the Odoo Apps Store yet; each chapter says so where it matters.

The app has its own help too. **Help, Guide** is a short tour inside WT HRMS, and each section has a button that opens the real screen.
