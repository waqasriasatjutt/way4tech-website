A commercial client rings on a Tuesday wanting property cover for a second unit. The account executive emails three carriers. One quotes, one quotes dearer with a wider wording, one declines because composite panel is outside its appetite. The client takes the dearer one, and six months later the finance director asks why. Meanwhile one carrier has paid this quarter's commission seven per cent short, a monthly payer has missed October, and a renewal sixty days out has nobody's name on it. That is the working week of a broker, and most offices run it across a broking system they have outgrown, a spreadsheet of commission and an inbox. This guide shows how the same week runs in Odoo 19 with our Insurance Broker Management module.

## What a broker has to keep track of

A broker does not write the policy. The carrier does. The broker finds the cover, explains it, collects for it, chases the carrier when it pays out, and is paid a commission by the carrier for the business placed. That puts five things on every file:

| Thing | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| The risk, captured once | Every carrier has to see the same facts, and the claims history and survey are what cover is made conditional on |
| Every carrier's answer | The quote taken, the quotes not taken and the declines, so the recommendation can be defended later |
| Premium and premium tax, kept apart | The tax is not income for anybody, and getting it into the commission figure overstates what the office earns |
| What the client was told, and when | Terms of business, a demands and needs statement, how the firm is paid, the policy documents, cancellation rights, the renewal notice |
| Commission expected against received | Carriers pay late and short, and nobody notices unless each placement books what it should bring in |

## What standard Odoo does

Standard Odoo, Community or Enterprise, ships no insurance brokerage app. Offices that try to make it fit usually reach for:

- **CRM for the enquiries.** It tracks an opportunity, but an opportunity has one expected revenue, not three carriers' quotes with tax, excess and commission each.
- **Sales or Subscriptions for the policy.** A sales order thinks the office is selling something. A broker is placing somebody else's product and earning a share of it, and the premium tax belongs to the carrier and the government.
- **A spreadsheet for commission.** It works until the first carrier statement that does not match.

Odoo does give a brokerage the parts underneath: contacts for clients and carriers, the chatter and activities for the audit trail and the to do list, and Invoicing for commission billed to a carrier. The [accounting overview](/modules/accounting/) covers what ships.

## How the module fits together

[Insurance Broker Management](https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/19.0/wt_insurance_broker) (USD 299) runs on Odoo 19 Community and installs Invoicing. It adds one app, **Insurance**, with seven menus: Clients, New Business, Policies, Claims, Commission, Reporting and Configuration.

1. **Configure the market.** Under **Configuration**, add each **Carrier** with a two or three letter code (it prefixes every policy number), its rating, how long it takes to pay commission, and a binding authority limit where you hold a binder. Check the eight **Classes of Business** the module installs (motor fleet, private motor, commercial property, combined liability, marine cargo, private medical, term life, professional indemnity): each has its premium tax rate or an exemption, a usual term, a standard excess, a minimum sum insured and a survey threshold. Then enter the **Commission Terms**: per carrier and class, the new business and renewal rates, whether they are paid on the net premium or the premium with tax, or a flat fee, with the dates they run from and to.
2. **Settings.** Your firm reference number, the company premium tax rate, the renewal window (60 days by default), whether a policy may go on risk without the disclosures, the claims chase interval, the tolerance for short paid commission, and the journal and product commission invoices use.
3. **Clients.** Each client sits on an Odoo contact and has an account executive, a type (personal lines, commercial or scheme member), a trade, an identity check and sanctions screening with dates, and the record the market sees: live policies, premium placed, claims paid, loss ratio and a risk grade.
4. **Enquiries.** Capture the risk once, add a line per carrier, press Approach the Market, record each answer, Present to the Client, and when the client chooses, Client Accepted raises the policy.
5. **Policies.** Put On Risk books the commission and builds the instalments; Issue Documents writes the disclosures and emails the client.
6. **After the sale.** Endorsements for mid term changes, Premium Collection for the instalments, Renewals for what is coming up, Claims for what went wrong, and Commission Receivable for what the carriers owe.

### The policy carries the numbers

When the client accepts, the policy is raised from the quote taken: carrier, premium, tax rate, excess and commission rate and basis. The policy number starts with the carrier's code, the expiry comes from the term, and a regulatory reference is built from the class code and the year so a return can be run straight off the book.

### Renewals and claims

A nightly job flags everything expiring inside the renewal window, sends the client a renewal invitation, writes the renewal notice onto their file and leaves a to do for the account executive. Each expiry ends Retained, Lapsed or Lost to another broker, which is what each carrier's retention rate is worked out from. Claims run from notification through acknowledgement, investigation and agreement to settlement, or repudiation with the wording the carrier relies on, and every settled claim feeds the client's loss ratio.

### Commission against the statements

Every placement books the commission it should bring in, expected a number of days after inception that comes from the carrier. Endorsements book their own commission, and cancellations and reductions book a clawback. When a statement arrives, each entry is reconciled: within the tolerance it is Received in Full, outside it is Short Paid and the shortfall is written in the chatter. Entries can be disputed, written off, or invoiced to the carrier where the carrier pays against an invoice.

## Limitations

We would rather you read these before you buy:

- **No carrier connection.** Quotes, policy numbers and claim numbers are entered from what the carrier sends. There is no carrier API, aggregator or rating engine link.
- **No client portal.** Clients do not log in. Documents go by email or post, and each is recorded as a disclosure.
- **Premium does not reach the ledger.** Premium, tax and instalments are tracked on the policy, and collecting an instalment marks it paid without posting a payment. Only commission invoiced to a carrier becomes an accounting document.
- **Two jobs ship switched off.** The renewal pipeline and the claims chase are scheduled actions that have to be switched on before anything is flagged or chased.
- **Endorsements do not touch the instalments.** Applying a mid term change updates the premium on the policy but leaves the instalment schedule as it was, so the difference has to be collected or refunded outside it.
- **Cancelling needs a reason the form hides.** In this version the cancellation reason sits in a group on the policy that only appears once a reason is set, so a live policy cannot be cancelled from the form without help from an administrator. We cover the detail in the policy chapter.
- **It is not life policy administration.** Term life and private medical are classes like any other; surrender values, fund units and medical underwriting are not in it.
