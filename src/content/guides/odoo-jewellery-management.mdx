A jewellery shop has almost nothing with a fixed price. The same 22 karat bangle is worth one figure on Monday and another on Thursday, because the gold in it moved, not because anyone touched it. So the counter does arithmetic all day: the weight of metal, times the rate for that purity on the day, plus a making charge, plus wastage, plus whatever stones are set in it. When that arithmetic lives on a calculator and a whiteboard, two customers get two prices for the same design, buy-backs are credited on trust, and the tray weighs less than the book without anyone noticing. This guide explains what a jeweller needs from Odoo, what standard Odoo does not cover, and how the Jewelry Management module runs the shop on Odoo 19, chapter by chapter.

## What a jewellery shop actually needs

Strip it down and a jeweller needs six things from the system:

1. **One rate a day that prices everything.** The shop enters the fine rate for the metal, and 22K, 18K and every other purity are priced from it by fineness.
2. **A piece priced in four parts.** Metal, making, wastage and stones, kept apart on the ticket, the invoice and the reports, because they earn very differently.
3. **Old gold credited on the test, not the stamp.** What the customer claims and what the touchstone or XRF says are two different numbers, and the gap needs a name against it.
4. **Metal that adds up at the bench.** The customer's metal goes in, a finished piece and some filings come out, and the difference has to be explained before the job is handed back.
5. **Goods out without being sold.** Approvals, salesman memos and exhibitions, each signed for at the value on the day the goods left.
6. **Stock counted in grams.** A tray of forty rings can still hold forty rings and be three grams light. Only a count by weight shows it.

## What standard Odoo does and does not do

Odoo 19 Community gives you Sales, Inventory and Accounting. A product has a sales price, can be tracked in stock, and is invoiced with taxes. That is enough for a shop that sells boxed goods at a list price.

For a jeweller it misses most of the list above:

- No daily metal rate, no purity or fineness, and no way to price a product off a rate rather than a fixed price.
- No gross, stone and net metal weight on the product, and no making charge or wastage.
- No hallmark or HUID number on a piece, and nothing that stops a heavy piece selling without one.
- No old gold buy-back with a purity test and a buy-back margin.
- No repair job card that weighs the customer's metal in and out.
- No memo issue, no stone certificate register, and no stock count in grams priced at today's rate.

You could add fields with Studio on Enterprise, but the rules (refuse to sell without an HUID, refuse to hand back a job with unexplained loss, refuse to let a big memo out without a manager) need code. That is what the module adds.

## How a piece is priced

Everything else in the module follows from this one calculation, so it is worth seeing once.

Every piece has three weights. The **gross weight** is what it weighs on the scales, stones and all. The **stone weight** is everything set into it that is not metal. The **net metal weight** is gross less stones, and that is the weight the gold rate is charged on, because nobody pays the gold rate for an emerald.

Purity is stated two ways. Karat is parts of gold out of 24, so 22K is 22 parts in 24. Fineness is the same thing as a fraction, so 22K is 0.916, which is why the trade also calls it 916. The shop enters one rate, normally against the fine standard (999), and the module works out every other purity of that metal by its fineness. If the shop quotes 22K at a different number than the ratio gives, it can enter a rate against that purity directly and that rate wins.

On top of the metal sit three more charges:

| Part | How it is worked out |
|---|---|
| Metal | Net weight times the rate for that purity on the rate day |
| Making | Per gram for handwork, a percentage of the metal value for machine chain and imported pieces, or a fixed amount for a piece bought in finished |
| Wastage | A percentage of the weight, priced at the same rate, for metal lost at the bench |
| Stones | Taken from the stones listed on the piece |

The module keeps those four apart all the way through. The metal is passed on close to cost; the making charge and the stones are where a shop earns its margin, and the reports show it.

## The module, the price and what it covers

[Jewelry Management](https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/19.0/wt_jewelry_management/) (technical name `wt_jewelry_management`) is USD 299, built for Odoo 19 Community. It installs Inventory and Accounting with it and adds one menu, Jewellery, with five areas:

| Menu | What lives there |
|---|---|
| Counter | Sales tickets, Old Gold Exchange, Rate Board |
| Workshop | Repairs and Custom Orders, My Bench |
| Stock | Jewellery Items, Approval and Memo, Count Sheets, Stone Certificates |
| Reporting | Margin Split; Metal, Making and Stones; Stock by Weight; Metal Reconciliation |
| Configuration | Settings, Counters, Metal Rates, Metals, Purity Standards, Stone Types |

Three groups decide who sees what. **Counter Sales** sells, takes in old gold and repairs, issues memos and counts the tray. **Goldsmith** sees only the jobs on their own bench, with no rights on tickets, rates or the books. **Jewellery Manager** sees everything, including the settings, the rate table, the reports, and the approvals on large memos.

## How to get started

1. Install Jewelry Management from Apps. Inventory and Accounting come with it.
2. Open **Jewellery, Configuration, Settings** and decide the VAT basis, the hallmark weight, the weight tolerance and the memo limit before anything else.
3. Check the metals and purities, set a buy-back margin per metal, and create your counters.
4. Enter today's fine rate, then load a handful of items with real weights and check that the price on screen matches what the counter would quote on paper.
5. Run one ticket, one buy-back, one repair and one memo end to end on a test database before going live.
