A kitchen display is only as good as its routing. If the grill sees cocktails or the bar misses a dessert, the cooks stop trusting the screen and go back to shouting. This chapter goes through every setting of the [POS Kitchen Display System](/odoo-guides/odoo-pos-kitchen-display-screen/) on Odoo 19, in the order a new kitchen would set them, using the demo's Kitchen and Bar screens.

## Step 1: products in POS categories

Routing is by **POS category**, the category set on each product's Point of Sale tab. Before creating any screen, make sure every product the kitchen or bar makes has one: Grill, Bar, Desserts on the demo. A screen that takes a parent category also takes its children, so a Drinks parent with Beer, Wine and Cocktails underneath can feed one bar screen with a single tick.

Products the kitchen never touches, such as bottled water handed over the counter, can stay outside every screen's categories. They are simply not sent anywhere.

## Step 2: a screen per station

**Kitchen, Configuration, Screens** lists every physical display with its code, the tills and categories it takes, how many tickets are open and late, and its average preparation time. **Open Display** on each line opens that screen full size, which is the page to bookmark on the station's tablet.

A screen form is split into four blocks.

### What it shows

- **Name** and a two or three letter **Short Code** (KIT, BAR), printed on the docket so staff can tell which screen it came from.
- **Point of Sale**: the tills this screen listens to. Empty means every till in the company, which is what a single-kitchen site wants.
- **Product Categories**: what it cooks, children included.
- **Every Category**: show everything regardless of category. Use it for a one-screen kitchen or as an expediter screen that sees every order.
- **Stages**: the stage names this screen uses (step 4).

A screen with no categories and Every Category off would never show anything, so it cannot be saved. A till that belongs to another company is refused too, because routing looks screens up by the till's company and it would never reach this one.

### Behaviour

- **Send Automatically**: raise tickets as soon as the till saves an order. Turn it off for a screen you only feed by hand with Send to Kitchen from the POS orders list.
- **Keep Served on Screen**: leave served tickets visible until they are cleared, instead of dropping them the moment they are handed over.
- **Clear After (min)**: how long after serving a ticket is archived. 10 by default; 0 keeps them.
- **Refresh Every (s)**: how often the display asks the server for new tickets. 8 by default, never below 1.

### Colour bands

**Amber After** and **Red After**, in minutes, per screen. The demo's Kitchen goes amber at 5 and red at 10; the Bar at 3 and 6, because drinks should be quicker. Red cannot be set lower than amber, and neither can be negative.

### Reporting

**Average Over (days)** is the window for the screen's average preparation time, 7 days by default, shown on the form, in the list and in the top bar of the display.

## Step 3: watch the live tickets

The **Live Tickets** tab at the bottom of the screen form lists that screen's tickets with reference, table, item count, sent time, minutes waiting and state. **Clear Served** at the top archives every served and cancelled ticket on this screen at once, which is handy after a test service.

## Step 4: stages

**Configuration, Stages** holds the names the kitchen uses for the four states. Four come with the module:

| Stage | Maps to | Instruction |
|---|---|---|
| Queued | New | Sent by the till, nobody has picked it up yet |
| Cooking | Preparing | Being made now |
| On the Pass | Ready | Plated and waiting to be taken out |
| Away | Served | With the customer (folded) |

A kitchen can rename them or add its own, as long as each one **Maps To** one of the four states, and a stage can be kept to one company. Timing is always measured on the state, not the name, so a site that calls it the Pass and a site that calls it Expo still produce figures that compare.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-kds-screens-categories-setup/stages-list.webp" alt="Odoo Kitchen Display Stages list with Queued, Cooking, On the Pass and Away, each mapped to a state" width={1440} height={900} caption="The four stages loaded with the module." />

<Callout title="Stages do not make columns on the display">
The full-screen display shows tickets as one grid of cards, rush first then oldest first. It does not split them into a column per stage, and it does not show a stage's instruction line. Stages appear on the ticket form, in the ticket filters and groups, and in the reports.
</Callout>

## Step 5: target time per dish

Averages alone only say what happened. A target turns them into something to manage. On each product, open **Point of Sale, Products** and set **Target Preparation (min)** in the Kitchen Display block at the bottom of the form. Only users with Configure Screens see the field.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-kds-screens-categories-setup/product-target-time.webp" alt="Odoo product form for Ribeye Steak with the Kitchen Display block showing a target preparation time of 14 minutes" width={1440} height={900} caption="Ribeye Steak should take 14 minutes." />

A ticket's target is the longest target among its items, because a table's food goes out together. A ticket called ready after its target is **Over Target**, and so is any single item ticked done after its own. Leave the target at zero for anything you do not want to measure.

## Step 6: who can do what

Two rights sit under **Kitchen Display** on the user form:

- **Work the Kitchen**: open the display, move tickets along, tick items off. Sees Display and Tickets.
- **Configure Screens**: everything above, plus Configuration, Reporting, target times, and the right to correct a stamped time. The administrator gets it on install.

Cashiers need neither. Tickets are raised in the background with the module's own rights, so selling never depends on kitchen permissions. Screens, tickets, items and stages are also limited to the companies a user may see.

## Step 7: the clean-up job

**Kitchen Display: clear served tickets** runs every five minutes for every company. It archives served and cancelled tickets once their screen's Clear After delay has passed. Archived tickets are not deleted: they stay in every report, and the **Archived** filter on Tickets shows them.

## Test it

Ring an order through the till with food and drink on it, then open each screen. You should see one ticket per screen with only its own items. Add an item at the till and the right ticket updates; tick one item done on the display and the ticket moves to preparing. When that works, look at the grouped ticket list to see which station holds the queue.
