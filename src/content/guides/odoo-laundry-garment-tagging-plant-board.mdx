A dry cleaner's worst conversations happen at collection: a shirt that is not on the hanger, a mark the customer swears was not there, a suit that went to the wrong branch. Every one of them is decided by what was written down when the clothes came in. This chapter of the [laundry management series](/odoo-guides/odoo-laundry-management/) follows an order from the counter through the plant to the rack in the Laundry Management module, and ends with the claim that comes when something goes wrong anyway.

## The counter: lines and the damage check

Open **Laundry, Counter, Orders** and press New. Pick the customer, and the phone and any account contract fill in by themselves. The branch defaults to the first one, Taken In is now, and the Promised Date is worked out from the branch turnaround: 48 hours at the demo's plant, 72 hours at a counter whose work travels to the plant.

On the Items tab, add one line per service and garment: Dry Clean, Shirt, 5. The unit price comes from the rate card, which is covered in the [rate card and contracts chapter](/odoo-guides/odoo-laundry-rate-card-hotel-contracts/). Each line has a box called **Checked at the Counter** and a field for instructions.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-laundry-garment-tagging-plant-board/draft-order.webp" alt="A draft laundry order at the Marina counter for one rug on curtain and rug cleaning at 30 AED, with the Checked at the Counter box on the line still unticked and the instruction 2.0 x 3.0 m, wool" width={1440} height={900} caption="A draft order with a line not yet checked for damage." />

That box is the point of the whole screen. With **Record Damage on Arrival** switched on in Settings, which is the default, pressing **Take In** on an order with any unchecked line is refused, and the message lists the garments still to look at. The staff member has to handle each piece, look for existing damage and tick the line before the order can go in.

<Callout title="Write the damage on the piece, not just the line">
The tick says somebody looked. What they found goes on each piece, in Condition on Arrival, once the order is taken in. That text is what you point to when a claim comes in.
</Callout>

## Take In: one record per piece

**Take In** moves the order to Received and creates a piece for every item. Five shirts become five records, each with a tag made of the tag prefix from Settings, the year and a running number. All the pieces start in the first plant stage, and the chatter notes how many were tagged.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-laundry-garment-tagging-plant-board/tagged-pieces.webp" alt="The Pieces tab of a received order listing each tagged piece with its tag number, garment, service, colour, stage Received, status In the Plant and any damage on arrival" width={1440} height={900} caption="After Take In: one tag per physical piece." />

Open a piece to describe it: colour, fabric, brand, stains, and the damage that was already there. A stain written here shows in red on the piece's card on the plant board, so the spotting bench sees it before cleaning.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-laundry-garment-tagging-plant-board/piece-record.webp" alt="A tagged piece record showing the order, customer, garment type, service and piece number, with colour, fabric, brand and stains, the promised date, and a Condition on Arrival box holding the damage noted at the counter" width={1440} height={900} caption="A piece: description, promise and condition on arrival." />

**Print Ticket** prints an 80 mm counter ticket for the customer with the items, totals, deposit and the list of tags. **Garment Tags**, from the Print menu on the pieces, prints one tag per piece with its barcode, garment, colour, service, customer and order, marked Express where it applies.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-laundry-garment-tagging-plant-board/garment-tags.webp" alt="Three printed garment tags, each with a tag number, garment and colour, service, customer and order number, an EXPRESS marker and a Code 128 barcode" width={480} height={460} caption="Printed tags, one per piece." />

## The plant board

**Start Cleaning** on the order moves it to In the Plant. From then on the work is done on the pieces, on **Plant, Plant Board**. It has one column for every stage, shown even when empty: Received, Sorted, Spotting, Cleaning, Drying, Pressing, Quality Check, and Packed and Racked. You can rename, reorder or fold the stages under Configuration.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-laundry-garment-tagging-plant-board/plant-board.webp" alt="The plant board with columns for each stage and a count of pieces in each, cards showing tag, garment, service and colour, order and customer, express badges, red stain warnings and a Next Stage button" width={1440} height={900} caption="Every piece in the stage it has reached." />

Each card carries the tag, garment, service and colour, the order and customer, an Express badge, any stain in red, and how long is left before the promise. **Next Stage** moves a piece one column on; you can also drag it. The list view shows the same pieces with how often each has been reworked, and filters for express work, reworked pieces, and anything lost or damaged. The count at the top of each column is the quickest way to see where work is piling up.

## Rework: sending a piece back through

When a piece fails at Quality Check, set its **Reprocess Reason**: Stain Remained, Poorly Pressed, Odour, Damaged in Process, Wrong Service or Other. Then press **Send Back Through**. Without a reason the button refuses, because rework you cannot explain is rework you cannot reduce.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-laundry-garment-tagging-plant-board/send-back-through.webp" alt="A two piece suit sent back to the Received stage with status Reprocessing, reprocess reason Wrong Service and a reprocess count of 1" width={1440} height={900} caption="A piece sent back through, with the reason on record." />

The piece returns to the first stage, its status becomes Reprocessing, its count goes up by one, and the order's chatter says which tag went back and why. **Plant, Rework** lists every piece that has been sent back at least once, grouped by service, which is the plant's quality figure.

<Callout title="Check the Rework list after hand over">
A piece sent back keeps the status Reprocessing until someone changes it. Next Stage moves the stage, not the status, and Hand Over only marks pieces that are In the Plant as returned. Clear the status by hand when the piece leaves.
</Callout>

**Lost**, on a piece, marks one that cannot be found, after a confirmation. It leaves the board and the order's chatter records it.

## Ready, racked and handed over

Fill in the **Rack** and **Slot** and press **Ready**. Ready is refused until a rack is set, so nothing is finished without a place on the rail. Every piece that is not lost moves to the final stage, the order becomes Ready, the ready time is stamped and the chatter names the rack. Turnaround in hours appears on the form.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-laundry-garment-tagging-plant-board/ready-on-rack.webp" alt="A ready order on rack A slot A25 with taken in, promised and ready times, a turnaround of 30 hours, and the chatter recording that it was taken in, racked, and that a collection reminder was sent" width={1440} height={900} caption="A ready order: rack, slot, turnaround and the reminder sent." />

When the customer comes in, **Hand Over** marks the order Collected with the time. An order delivered by van is handed over when the driver completes the delivery job, covered in the [pickup and delivery chapter](/odoo-guides/odoo-laundry-pickup-delivery-routes/).

An order is **late** if it was finished after its promise or is still unfinished past it; it shows a red ribbon and appears under the Late filter. The flag is recalculated when the order changes, so look at it after the day's work has been moved on.

Orders that sit on the rack too long can be chased: the scheduled action "Laundry: chase uncollected orders" emails each customer whose order has been ready longer than the number of days in Settings, seven by default, and notes it on the order. It ships switched off, needs outgoing mail set up, and writes again every day the order is still waiting.

## Damage and loss claims

Sooner or later something gets damaged or lost. Under **Counter, Claims**, a claim names the order and the piece, the kind (Damaged, Lost, Colour Run, Shrunk, Stain Not Removed or Other), a description and the claimed value. It then moves through its stages with a button for each:

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| Investigate | Marks the piece Damaged, or Lost for a loss claim |
| Make an Offer | Records the offered value and the resolution; a refund or credit needs an amount |
| Settle | Records the settled value and date; cannot be more than was claimed |
| Reject | Closes the claim with no payment |

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-laundry-garment-tagging-plant-board/claim-offer.webp" alt="A colour run claim at the offer stage with claimed value 120 AED, offered value 80 AED, resolution Refund and the handling manager, and the description of a navy shirt that bled onto a white one" width={1440} height={900} caption="A claim with an offer made and waiting for the customer." />

The resolutions are Refund, Credit Note, Replacement, Cleaned Again Free and No Compensation. Settling records the outcome; the refund or credit note itself is raised in Invoicing. On the demo one claim was rejected because the missing button had been written on the piece when it came in, which is the whole reason for the damage check at the counter.
