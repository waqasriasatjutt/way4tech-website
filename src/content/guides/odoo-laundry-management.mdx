A customer drops off five shirts, a suit and a silk blouse on Saturday. On Tuesday she collects four shirts, the suit has a mark on the lapel she says was not there, and the blouse is on a rail at the other branch. Nobody can prove what came in, the suit claim becomes an argument at the counter, and the fifth shirt turns up a week later in the sorting bins. Most of a laundry's losses come from exactly this: a paper ticket that says "5 shirts" and no record of the pieces themselves. This guide shows how a laundry or dry cleaner runs on Odoo 19 with our Laundry Management module, from the counter to the plant, the rack, the van and the monthly invoice.

## What a laundry has to track

A laundry looks like a shop at the front and a small factory at the back. The shop takes a promise: these pieces, cleaned this way, ready by this time, for this price. The factory has to keep it. The records that matter sit at the join between the two:

| What | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Every physical piece | A ticket that says 5 shirts cannot tell you which one is missing |
| Condition on arrival | The only thing that settles a damage claim at collection |
| The stage each piece has reached | Where the pile is building up in the plant today |
| Rework and its reason | The real cost of the plant, and a paper system never records it |
| Rack and slot | So the counter finds a finished order in seconds |
| The promised time | Late work loses regular customers faster than price does |
| Account rates and billing | Hotels and offices want their own rate card and one invoice a month |
| Collection and delivery | Proof at the door, and a run that closes with what it brought back |

The trade prices in several ways at once: per piece for a shirt, per kilo for a wash and fold bag, per square metre for curtains and rugs, with an express surcharge for same day work.

## What standard Odoo does and does not cover

Standard Odoo has no laundry app. People usually try one of three things:

- **Point of Sale.** Fast for taking money, but a POS order is a list of products. It has no idea that five shirts are five separate objects in a plant, or what condition each was in.
- **Sales plus Inventory with serial numbers.** You can force a serial number onto each garment, but the customer owns the garment, not you, so stock moves and valuation are the wrong model.
- **Field Service or Repairs.** Good for one item at a time, too heavy for three hundred pieces a day.

What Odoo does give you underneath is solid: contacts for customers and accounts, Invoicing with VAT, the chatter for an audit trail, and reports you can pivot and export. The [Odoo accounting overview](/modules/accounting/) covers what ships. The module builds the laundry on top of those.

## The module

[Laundry Management](https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/19.0/wt_laundry_management) (USD 199) runs on Odoo 19 Community and needs only Invoicing, which is part of Community. It adds one app, **Laundry**, with six menus:

| Menu | Screens |
|---|---|
| Counter | Orders, Claims |
| Plant | Plant Board, Rework |
| Collection | Collections and Deliveries, Routes, Drivers |
| Accounts | Contracts |
| Reporting | Revenue and Turnaround |
| Configuration | Settings, Branches, Services, Garment Types, Rate Card, Plant Stages, Racks |

Four roles control who sees what: **Driver** (his own runs only), **Plant Operator** (moves pieces, no pricing), **Counter Staff** (takes orders in, prices, hands back, invoices) and **Laundry Manager** (everything). A driver linked to a user account can read only the routes and jobs assigned to him.

## How the work flows

1. **Take the order in.** Pick the customer and the lines (service and garment). Prices come from the rate card, or from the account's own rates. Tick each line once it has been checked for damage; the module refuses to take an order in until every line is checked.
2. **Tag every piece.** Take In creates one piece per item, each with its own tag. Record colour, fabric, brand, stains and damage on arrival, and print the ticket and the tags.
3. **Work the plant.** The plant board shows every piece in its stage, from Received through Sorted, Spotting, Cleaning, Drying, Pressing and Quality Check to Packed and Racked. A piece that fails the quality check goes back through with a reason.
4. **Rack it and mark it ready.** Ready is refused until a rack is set. The counter sees the rack and slot when the customer arrives.
5. **Hand it over, or deliver it.** Hand Over at the counter, or a driver completes a delivery job with the name of whoever signed for it.
6. **Bill it.** A walk-in order is invoiced on its own. A hotel or office on a monthly contract gets one invoice for the month.

### Express work and the promise

Every branch has a standard and an express turnaround in hours, and an express surcharge as a percentage. Tick Express on an order and the promised time moves to the express turnaround and the surcharge is added. An order finished after its promise, or still unfinished past it, carries a Late ribbon.

### Accounts on contracts

Hotels, clinics, offices and households on a regular wash each get a contract: their own agreed rates, the branch that serves them, payment terms, and billing either per order or consolidated monthly.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-laundry-management/hotel-contract.webp" alt="A hotel laundry contract showing the account, the branch that services it, kind Hotel, start date, consolidated monthly billing, payment terms of 30 days, and the agreed rates for bedsheets, pillowcases, duvet covers, towels and tablecloths" width={1440} height={900} caption="A hotel contract: agreed rates and one invoice a month." />

### Collection routes

A job is one stop, a collection or a delivery, with a time window. A route is a driver's run for a shift. Send Out puts the stops on the road; a delivery cannot be completed without the name of whoever signed for it, and the run cannot close while any stop is still open.

### Reporting

Revenue and Turnaround is a pivot of collected and invoiced orders by branch and month, with a graph of hours from taken in to ready. Rework has its own list, grouped by service, and claims have a pivot of what was paid out.

## Limitations

We would rather you read these before you buy. Each one comes from reading the module's code:

- **No portal, app or point of sale.** Customers get a printed ticket and, if you switch it on, an email reminder. There is no online ordering or tracking page.
- **No scanning screen.** Tags print with a barcode and scanning into the search box finds the piece, but moving pieces is done with the Next Stage button.
- **Per square metre is priced like per piece.** A Per Square Metre line multiplies quantity by price, so type the square metres in the quantity.
- **The contract discount is not applied.** It is stored on the contract; put the discount into the agreed rates or onto the order.
- **Jobs and orders are not linked from the screen.** Ticking Deliver to the Customer does not create a delivery job, and the link between a job and its orders cannot be set from the order or the job form.
- **No loyalty scheme.** There is a discount with a reason on each order, nothing more.
- **Some figures only change when the order changes.** The Late flag is recalculated when the order is edited, not as the clock passes the promise.
- **The scheduled jobs ship switched off.** The monthly invoice and the uncollected order reminder both need switching on, and the reminder writes again every day an order is still waiting.
