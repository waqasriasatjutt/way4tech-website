Most of the money an agency loses on leases is lost at the two ends. At the start, a deposit is taken and written in a notebook, and at move-out nobody can say how much was held or what was agreed to be withheld. At the end of the term, the renewal date passes without anyone noticing, the tenant stays on at the old rent, and the escalation the owner agreed to in the first contract never happens. This chapter covers how a lease is set up in Odoo 19 with the Real Estate Management base and its Enterprise add-on so that both ends are on the record: the rent and deposit at the start, an escalation agreed up front, a warning before the term runs out, and a renewal that carries everything forward.

## The lease in the base module

In the base module a contract is a sale, rent or lease agreement, usually created from a deal with one button so it takes the property, buyer or tenant, seller or landlord, agent and agreed amount from the deal. It has a signing date, start and end dates, the terms and conditions as formatted text, legal documents attached (ownership papers, NOC, agreement copies) and internal notes. It moves through **Draft**, **Signed** and **Active**, with **Expired** and **Cancelled** to the side, and **Print Contract** produces the agreement as a PDF.

The base module holds one amount on the contract, the contract amount. That is enough for a sale, not for a lease paid quarterly with a deposit. The Enterprise add-on fills that gap.

## What the add-on adds to a lease

On a rent or lease agreement, the add-on adds two sections to the form.

**Rent and Deposit**

- **Rent / Period**: the amount of each payment. This is what the cheque book is built from.
- **Paid Every**: monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or yearly.
- **Security Deposit**: the amount agreed.
- **Deposit Held**: ticked once the deposit is actually received.
- **Deductions at Move-Out** and **Deposit Refunded**: filled in at the end.

**Renewal and Collection**

- **Annual Escalation (%)**: the step up agreed for renewal.
- **Cheque Pending**, **Cheque Cleared** and **Cheque Bounced Count**: worked out from the lease's cheques.
- **Renewal Of**: on a renewed lease, the one it replaced.

## Step 1: set up the lease at move-in

1. Create the agreement from the deal, or directly under **Transactions, Contracts**, with contract type Rent or Lease.
2. Fill in the start and end dates. Renewal needs an end date.
3. Fill in **Rent / Period** and **Paid Every**.
4. Fill in **Security Deposit**, and tick **Deposit Held** once the money or the deposit cheque is in.
5. Enter the **Annual Escalation (%)** the owner and tenant agreed.
6. Press **Generate Cheques** to build the cheque book, as covered in the [post-dated cheques chapter](/odoo-guides/odoo-post-dated-cheques-rent/). A deposit cheque can be added by hand with purpose Security Deposit.
7. Mark the agreement signed, then active.

## Step 2: watch for leases running out

The portfolio dashboard has a **Leases running out** card: active rent and lease agreements whose end date falls within the next sixty days. Clicking it opens the list. Sixty days is about the last moment to agree the renewal and the new rent with both sides before the old term ends.

<Callout title="Nothing expires by itself">There is no scheduled job that moves a lease to Expired. A lease past its end date stays active, and keeps counting on the dashboard, until somebody presses Mark Expired or renews it. That is deliberate in one way (a tenant holding over is still a tenant) but it means somebody has to own the list.</Callout>

## Step 3: renew with the rent stepped up

When the renewal is agreed, open the lease and press **Renew**. The add-on:

1. Refuses if the agreement is a sale, has no end date, or has already been renewed.
2. Creates a new agreement as a copy, in **Draft**, with today as the signing date.
3. Starts it the day after the old one ends, and gives it the same length of term as the old one (or one year if it had neither a start date nor a signing date).
4. Multiplies the **Rent / Period** and the **Contract Amount** by one plus the escalation percentage, rounded to the currency. With 80,000 a quarter and 5 per cent, the new rent is 84,000 a quarter.
5. Carries the deposit amount and the escalation over, but clears **Deposit Held**, the deductions and the refund, so the new lease starts its own deposit record.
6. Links the new agreement back with **Renewal Of**, and posts a note on the old one: renewed as the new reference, with the escalation used.

The new agreement opens straight away. Check the dates and rent, attach the signed renewal, mark it signed and active, and generate its cheque book. Then mark the old lease expired when its term ends. The **Renew** button disappears from a lease once it has been renewed, so a lease cannot be renewed twice by accident.

A change in the escalation for one tenant is simple: edit the percentage on the lease before pressing Renew, or edit the new rent on the draft renewal afterwards.

## Step 4: settle the deposit at move-out

When the tenant leaves:

1. Enter **Deductions at Move-Out**: damage, unpaid utilities, whatever the lease allows you to withhold.
2. Press **Refund Deposit**. The add-on works out the deposit less the deductions, writes it to **Deposit Refunded**, and posts a note with the refund and the amount withheld.
3. Pay the tenant from your bank and record it in Invoicing.

The button only shows while **Deposit Held** is ticked, and it refuses if no deposit is held.

## How the lease feeds the dashboard

The portfolio dashboard reads the leases directly:

- **Occupancy** and **Vacant units**: properties listed for rent or lease, and how many of them have an active rent or lease agreement.
- **Rent roll**: the sum of **Rent / Period** across active leases.
- **Leases running out**: active leases ending within sixty days.
- **Active leases**: the count of rent and lease agreements in Active.

<Callout title="Read the rent roll with care">The rent roll adds each lease's rent per period as it stands, so a monthly lease and a quarterly lease are added together without converting either to a common period. If your portfolio mixes payment periods, treat the figure as a sum of instalments, not as monthly or annual rent. Rent escalation only happens at renewal; there is no mid-term increase. And nothing on the lease posts to the accounts: the deposit, deductions and refund are recorded figures, not journal entries.</Callout>

## A renewal checklist

- Every active lease has an end date, a rent per period and an escalation, even if the escalation is zero.
- Somebody opens **Leases running out** every week.
- Renewals are created with the button, not by hand, so the link back is kept.
- The new lease gets its own cheque book and its own deposit record.
- Old leases are marked expired once their term ends.
