Most teams that move their projects into Odoo already have the data. Projects exist, tasks have stages, the invoices sit in the same database. What they miss is the part that makes a project tool get used every morning: one screen where the whole project can be read at a glance, a way to stop work moving forward before somebody signs it off, and reminders that arrive without a manager chasing people in a group chat. When that is missing, the team keeps its real status in a spreadsheet or in an outside board app, and Odoo turns into a place where tasks go to be forgotten.

This guide covers Project Pro, a module I built for Odoo 19 Community that adds those pieces on top of the standard Project app. This page explains what it is for, what standard Odoo already does, how the module is priced and how an administrator gets a team started. The two chapters after it go through the daily work step by step.

## Who this is for

Project Pro suits any team whose work is a list of items with an owner, a date and a status:

- **Agencies and consultancies** running client engagements, where an item waits on the client and someone has to invoice at month end.
- **Software and product teams** working in sprints with story points and a review step before done.
- **Marketing teams** tracking campaign assets from idea to live, with a budget against each.
- **Contractors and fit-out firms** tracking site work by area, with an inspection before an area counts as complete.
- **HR and office teams** running recruitment pipelines, onboarding checklists and events.

Each of these ships as a board template, covered in the [boards chapter](/odoo-guides/odoo-project-boards-timeline-workload/). The module is written for people who have never used Odoo: the printed guide behind these pages assumes no prior knowledge, and takes about twenty minutes to read.

## What standard Odoo Project does and does not do

The standard Project app in Odoo 19 Community is the base this module builds on. It already gives you projects, tasks grouped by stage, assignees, deadlines, priorities, tags, subtasks and the conversation on each task, and it can share a project with a client through the customer portal. That last point matters: the client sharing described further down is standard Odoo, and Project Pro works with it rather than replacing it.

What a team usually asks for, and does not find in Community, falls into four areas:

1. **A board with your own columns.** Standard task views show the fields Odoo defines. A team that wants to track a sprint, a budget figure, a channel or a room number on every row needs a column it can add itself, with totals per lane.
2. **A planning view.** The Gantt view in standard Odoo comes from an Enterprise module. On a Community server there is no timeline of tasks and no view of who is overloaded. Project Pro draws its own timeline and workload screens for that reason.
3. **Sign-off before a stage.** Nothing in standard Project stops a task from entering a stage until someone approves it.
4. **Notifications a person controls.** Odoo sends followers messages, but a team member cannot say "push me when I am assigned, email me when something is overdue, and send nothing after eight in the evening".

## What Project Pro adds

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-project-management/home.webp" alt="Project Pro home screen in Odoo with a four step setup checklist, tiles for open items, due today, overdue, done this week and unowned items, and cards for each project board" width={1400} height={875} caption="Home: your own numbers, every board, and a setup checklist that ticks itself off." />

The module adds a top menu with four parts: **Work**, **Approvals**, **Notifications** and, for managers, **Configuration**. Almost everything happens under Work.

- **Home** shows each person their open items, what is due today, what is overdue and what they finished this week. Managers also see items that nobody owns yet. A four-step checklist at the top guides a new user from first board to first automation.
- **Boards.** Each project gets a board where rows are items and columns are facts about them. Statuses are coloured lanes, and you can add columns of type status, dropdown, people, date, timeline, number, progress, checkbox, text or priority. Eight templates ship with the app.
- **Automations.** Ready-made rules that you switch on with one click and can preview against today's items first, plus a rule builder for your own.
- **Approvals.** Rules that guard a stage, with one or more levels of approvers, escalation after a set number of hours and delegation while someone is away.
- **Timeline and Workload.** Items as bars on a calendar, and hours per person per week against their capacity.
- **Notifications.** Each person picks, per event, whether it reaches the inbox, email, phone push or a daily digest, with quiet hours and a mute for holidays.

It runs in the phone browser as well as on a computer. On a phone, a board shows one card per item and an item opens as a sheet from the bottom.

## Modules and prices

There is one module to buy, and one optional add-on:

| Module | Technical name | Price | What it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Project Pro](https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/19.0/wt_project_pro/) | `wt_project_pro` | USD 900 | Boards, columns, templates, automations, approvals, timeline, workload, notifications |
| [PWA Mobile App with Push Notifications](https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/19.0/wt_pwa_push/) | `wt_pwa_push` | USD 500 | Optional. Push messages on an installed phone app |

Project Pro depends on three things only: Project, Discuss and the web client. All three are part of Odoo 19 Community, so nothing here asks for an Enterprise subscription. The push add-on is a soft link: if it is not installed, events meant for push go to the Odoo inbox instead, and nothing breaks.

## The five words used everywhere

The screens use a small set of words. Agree on them with the team before the first board, because they decide how people name things.

- **Project:** a piece of work with a start and an end, such as a campaign, a site or a client engagement.
- **Board:** the screen where a project lives.
- **Item:** one row on a board, small enough for one person to finish.
- **Status:** where an item stands. The starter set is Not Started, Working on it, Stuck and Done; templates bring their own.
- **Column:** a fact tracked for every item, such as people, due date, progress or a budget.

## Setting it up as the administrator

Plan ten minutes before the team starts.

1. **Users.** In Settings, Users and Companies, create each person with an email. Under Project, give team members **User** and give the people who will create boards, columns, rules and templates **Administrator**. A user with no Project rights will not see the app at all.
2. **Boards for existing projects.** Open Work, Boards and press **Set up a board for every project**. Projects created before the module was installed get their board in one step.
3. **Notification defaults.** Under Configuration, Notification Settings, set the event matrix, digest time and quiet hours that every new user starts with. People can change their own afterwards.
4. **Outgoing mail.** Email notifications and portal invitations need an outgoing mail server. Send yourself a test before go-live.
5. **Phone push.** Needs the site on https and the push add-on.
6. **Automations and approval rules.** Covered in the [approvals and automation chapter](/odoo-guides/odoo-project-approvals-automation/). Preview each one before switching it on.

Configuration also holds an **Automation Log**, which records what each rule did, to which item and when, and a **Notification Queue** that shows what is still waiting to be sent. Both are the first place to look when someone says "I was never told".

## Giving clients access

A client can follow a project without a back office login, through Odoo's own customer portal. Make sure the client is a contact with an email address, set the project's Visibility to include invited portal users, then press **Share Project** on the project form and add the client with Read or Edit access. They receive a link, set a password, and see the project under My Account, Projects. Comments they write land in the task conversation your team already uses, and your replies reach them by email.

<Callout title="The client sees Odoo's task list, not the board">The portal shows the standard task view: name, stage, deadline, assignees and the conversation. It does not show the Project Pro board, its coloured statuses or your custom columns, and it does not show internal notes. A client-facing board with a choice of public columns per project is not built; it would be a separate add-on.</Callout>

## A daily routine that keeps the board honest

The module only helps if the board is current. The routine the guide recommends is short. In the morning, open Home, look at Due today and Overdue, and either do each item, move its date or hand it to someone. Answer approvals and mentions from the bell. During the day, update statuses as you go and set Progress on long items so the manager sees movement without asking. If something is stuck, set it to Stuck and write one line in Notes; the manager is told automatically. In the evening, move finished work to Done. Once a week, managers check Workload and the list of items nobody owns.
