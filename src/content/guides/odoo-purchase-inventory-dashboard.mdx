Buying and stock go wrong quietly. Nobody notices a supplier creeping up to half the spend, a bill sitting unposted for three weeks or a fast mover dropping under its reorder point until a customer is told to wait. The Purchase and Inventory dashboards put those few figures where the buyer and the storekeeper see them every morning. This chapter goes card by card through both boards, explains what each one counts and what has to be set up in Odoo for it to be right.

## The Purchase Analytics board

Install **Purchase Dashboard** (`wt_purchase_dashboard`) and **Purchase Analytics** appears on the home screen for purchase users and managers. It opens on This Month; the period switch at the top right moves every figure at once.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-purchase-inventory-dashboard/purchase-year-top.webp" alt="Purchase Analytics dashboard in Odoo 19 with total spend, purchase orders, average PO value, RFQs, vendors, items ordered, bills to process, overdue bills and tax amount" width={1440} height={900} caption="The Purchase board on This Year." />

### What the nine cards count

The first six and the last card count purchase orders of the current company whose **order date** falls in the period.

- **Total Spend:** the untaxed total of confirmed purchase orders.
- **Purchase Orders:** how many were confirmed.
- **Avg PO Value:** the untaxed average per confirmed order.
- **RFQs:** requests for quotation still in draft or sent.
- **Vendors:** how many different vendors received a confirmed order.
- **Items Ordered:** the total quantity on confirmed order lines.
- **Bills to Process:** vendor bills still in draft. This card does not follow the period: it counts every draft bill there is.
- **Overdue Bills:** posted vendor bills past their due date and not fully paid, also across all time.
- **Tax Amount:** the tax on confirmed orders in the period.

Orders waiting for approval (the To Approve state, if you use purchase approval) are not counted in any card.

### The tabs

1. **Overview:** the Purchase Trend, spend as a filled line and order count as a dashed line, by day up to 90 days and by month beyond. Under it, Recent Purchase Orders lists the latest 20 in the period with Export CSV.
2. **Vendors:** the top ten vendors by spend and a Vendor Stats table with each vendor's number of orders, spend and average, with Export CSV.
3. **Products:** the top ten products by quantity ordered and a chart of spend by product category.
4. **Payables:** a chart of RFQs against confirmed orders by month. Despite the name it shows no bills.
5. **Performance:** the trend chart again.
6. **Comparison:** spend and orders against the period of the same length just before.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-purchase-inventory-dashboard/purchase-tab-vendors.webp" alt="Top 10 Vendors by Spend chart and Vendor Stats table in the Odoo purchase dashboard" width={1440} height={900} caption="The Vendors tab: how much of the spend sits with one supplier." />

The Vendors tab is the one to read before a price negotiation. In the demo one furniture supplier carries more than half the year's spend, which is a fact worth having on the table when the next price list arrives.

<Callout title="Limits of the Purchase board">There is no vendor filter. Bills to Process and Overdue Bills ignore the period, so they look the same on every setting. Clicking Overdue Bills opens a list built on a wider rule than the card, so the list can be longer than the count. In the Comparison chart spend and order count share one axis, so the order bars are too small to see; read the two figures above the chart instead.</Callout>

### What to set up

- **Confirm purchase orders in Odoo** before goods arrive, so spend lands in the month you committed it.
- **Use payment terms on vendors.** The due date of each bill, which decides Overdue Bills, comes from the payment terms.
- **Post bills when they are checked.** A bill left in draft stays in Bills to Process until someone posts or cancels it.
- **Keep product categories tidy.** Spend by Category is only as useful as the categories.

## The Inventory Analytics board

Install **Stock and Inventory Dashboard** (`wt_inventory_dashboard`) and **Inventory Analytics** appears for stock users and managers.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-purchase-inventory-dashboard/inventory-year-top.webp" alt="Inventory Analytics dashboard in Odoo 19 with products in stock, stock value, low stock alerts, out of stock, pending receipts and deliveries, stock moves and locations with stock" width={1440} height={900} caption="The Inventory board. Most cards show the warehouse as it is now, not for the period." />

### What the nine cards count

- **Products in Stock:** how many products have a positive quantity in an internal location. Now, not for the period.
- **Stock Value:** reads zero on Odoo 19, see the note below.
- **Low Stock Alerts:** products whose quantity at the location of their reorder rule is below the rule's minimum.
- **Out of Stock:** products with a zero or negative quantity record in an internal location. Odoo removes empty quantity records, so in practice this counts products that have gone negative, which usually means a delivery was validated without the stock being there.
- **Pending Receipts** and **Pending Deliveries:** the total quantity on receipts and deliveries that are waiting or ready but not done, across all time. These are units, not documents.
- **Stock Moves:** how many stock moves were completed in the period.
- **Avg Product Value:** reads zero on Odoo 19, like Stock Value.
- **Locations w/ Stock:** how many internal locations hold any stock.

### The tabs

1. **Overview:** the Stock Movement Trend of quantity received against quantity sent out, by day up to 90 days and by month beyond, and a doughnut of quantity on hand by product category. Moves between two internal locations are not counted as in or out.
2. **Stock Levels:** top products by stock value (empty on Odoo 19) and the stock by category chart.
3. **Movements:** incoming against outgoing as bars.
4. **Valuation:** the top ten products by value (empty on Odoo 19).
5. **Alerts:** the Low Stock Items table: each product below its reorder minimum with on hand, the minimum and the shortage, with Export CSV. This is the buyer's list for the day.
6. **Comparison:** quantity received, quantity sent and moves completed against the period before.

<Callout title="Stock value on Odoo 19">Stock Value, Avg Product Value, the Top Products by Stock Value chart and the Valuation tab read Odoo's old stock valuation layer table. Odoo 19 reworked stock valuation and that table no longer exists, so on Odoo 19 these show zero or nothing. Even where it exists, the card adds up the value of moves recorded in the period, not the value on hand. Until the module is updated, take stock value from Inventory, Reporting, Stock, which values on hand at cost.</Callout>

### Setting up the low stock list

The Alerts tab is the most useful part of the board, and it depends entirely on reorder rules.

1. In **Inventory, Configuration, Reordering Rules** (or from the product's Min/Max button), create a rule for each product you want watched.
2. Set the **location to the one you actually count**, normally WH/Stock. The board compares the quantity at exactly that location, so stock kept in WH/Stock/Shelf 1 is not added in. If you use sub-locations, put the rule on the sub-location, or keep stock at the parent.
3. Set the **minimum** at the level where you want to reorder. The shortage column is the minimum less on hand.
4. Choose the rule's trigger. A manual trigger keeps Odoo from raising purchase quotations on its own, which suits a business that wants the list but places orders by hand.

Archived reorder rules are still counted in this version, so delete rules you no longer want rather than archiving them.

## Reading the two boards together

The two boards answer the buyer's day in order. Alerts on the Inventory board says what to buy. Pending Receipts says what is already on its way, so you do not order twice. The Vendors tab on the Purchase board says who you already spend the most with. And Overdue Bills says which suppliers are waiting for money, which is worth knowing before you ask any of them for a better price. For the amount owed and how old it is, the [accounting dashboard](/odoo-guides/odoo-accounting-dashboard/) has payables aging. The [suite overview](/odoo-guides/odoo-dashboards/) lists every board.
