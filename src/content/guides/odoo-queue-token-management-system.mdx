At ten in the morning a clinic reception has eleven people standing at the desk, three of them with appointments, one elderly man who has been there forty minutes, and a receptionist typing the same patient into the system for the second time this week. Nobody knows who came first. The doctor in room 4 is free and does not know it. A queue system fixes the order, but most of them are a separate box with its own licence that knows nothing about your customers. This guide explains how a token queue works and what it needs, then shows how to run one from Odoo with our WT Queue and Token System.

## What a token queue actually does

A customer touches a service on a kiosk and gets a printed ticket: A023. The letter is the line (A for consultation, P for pharmacy, L for the lab) and the number is their place in it today. A TV in the waiting area shows the number being called with a chime and a spoken call, and which counter to go to. At each counter, staff press Call next, Recall, Start, Done, No show or Transfer. Numbers start again at A001 every morning.

Behind that, the business gets two measurements it never had before: how long people waited before they were called, and how long each counter took to serve them. Those two times, per service, per counter and per member of staff, are what a branch manager needs to decide when to open a second desk.

## What standard Odoo does and does not cover

Odoo has contacts, calendar appointments and, in some editions, an appointment booking app. It has no kiosk, no token numbers, no TV display and no way to measure a waiting room. A walk-in customer leaves no trace unless someone types them in.

The WT Queue and Token System fills exactly that gap and nothing more. It does not replace your clinic, pharmacy or bank software. It sits next to it in Odoo and links the queue to what Odoo already knows: the contact, their notes and their appointments.

|---|---|---|

## Who it is for

Clinics and medical centres, pharmacies, banks and exchange houses, government and visa service offices, telecom shops, service centres and any front office where people wait for more than one kind of service. It suits a single branch and a network of branches equally, because each branch keeps its own services, counters, languages and colours.

## The two halves: the branch and Odoo

In the branch, one installer of the **WT Queue** app for Windows gives three screens:

- the **kiosk**, a full screen touch page with one big button per service, in English, Arabic or Urdu, laid out right to left where needed. The customer can type a mobile number, and the ticket prints with a QR code;
- the **TV display**, with the number being called, every counter, the next numbers per service, a loop of your pictures or videos and a scrolling message;
- the **counter bar**, a thin window that stays on top of whatever the staff member is doing.

One PC is also the **hub**. It issues the numbers for the whole branch and the other PCs find it on the network and join with a six digit code. If the internet drops, the hub carries on and sends the timeline to Odoo later. Only one hub gives out numbers for a branch, so numbers never clash.

## How the numbers stay right

Tickets reach Odoo as a list of events: issued, called, recalled, serving started, transferred, done, no show, cancelled. After an outage those events can arrive late or twice. Odoo never patches a ticket step by step. It sorts the ticket's whole timeline and works the state, the counter, the staff member and the times out again, so a resent batch cannot leave a ticket wrong. The waiting time runs from issue to the first call; the service time runs from Start to Done and only counts tickets that were served.

<Callout title="What it does not do">
The module sends no SMS or WhatsApp when a number is called; the customer follows their turn on a phone page opened from the QR code on the ticket. The live dashboard shows today only, so use the analysis or the PDF report for other dates. Kiosk, TV and counter screens need the Windows app: there is no browser kiosk.
</Callout>

## What it costs and what it needs

The module is USD 199 on the Odoo Apps Store, for Odoo 19 Community or Enterprise. It depends only on Contacts and Calendar. The WT Queue app for Windows is a free download that updates itself with signed releases. In the branch you need Windows 10 or 11 PCs on one network, an ESC/POS thermal printer and a TV. A PC with a second HDMI output can run the kiosk and the TV at once, and a demo mode with a virtual printer lets you try every screen on one PC before buying hardware.
