A property agency runs on two kinds of work that look similar from the outside and are very different inside. One is brokerage: listing a villa, chasing the inquiries, getting a token payment and a signed agreement, and taking a commission. The other is management: holding a year of a tenant's cheques, banking each on its date, fixing the water heater, taking a fee and paying the owner what is left. Brokerage can live in a CRM. Management cannot, because it is money held on somebody else's behalf, and the owner will ask for a statement that reconciles. This guide covers both on Odoo 19, with the Real Estate Management module for the brokerage side and its Enterprise add-on for the management side.

## What a property agency actually needs

Before choosing any software, list what the office does every week:

1. **One record per property.** Type, purpose (sale, rent or lease), area in the unit your market uses, price, owner, agent, photos and its stage from available to sold or rented.
2. **Inquiries that do not get lost.** Who asked, about what, with what budget, from which source, and when to follow up.
3. **Deals with a token and a schedule.** Agreed price, token paid, agreement signed, installments due and paid, commission on the deal.
4. **Leases with the terms written down.** Rent per period, deposit held, the escalation agreed for renewal, start and end dates.
5. **Post-dated cheques.** In the Gulf and much of South Asia a year's rent is handed over as a book of cheques on the day the lease is signed. The office needs to know which ones to bank this week and which bounced.
6. **Owner statements.** Rent collected, the management fee, repairs paid on the owner's behalf, and what is owed.
7. **Maintenance.** A fault reported, assigned, done, and charged to the right party.

## What standard Odoo does and does not do

Standard Odoo has useful pieces. CRM tracks leads, Invoicing raises bills and records payments, and Contacts holds owners and tenants. None of them knows what a property is. There is no record with an area in marla or square metres, no owner on a listing, no lease that carries a deposit and an escalation, and no register for a tenant's cheque book. An agency that tries to fit this into CRM stages and product records ends up keeping the real register in a spreadsheet next to Odoo, which is the problem it was trying to solve.

## The two modules

[Real Estate Management](https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/19.0/wt_real_estate/) is the base, USD 35, for Odoo 19 Community. It installs with Employees (agents are employees) and Invoicing. [Real Estate Management Enterprise](https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/19.0/wt_real_estate_enterprise/) is a USD 69 add-on on top of it for agencies that manage property for owners. Despite the name, the add-on does not need Odoo Enterprise.

## The base: properties, inquiries and deals

**Properties.** Each property has a title, a reference, a type (house, flat or apartment, plot, commercial, farmhouse, hall, warehouse, agricultural land, or your own), a purpose (for sale, for rent or for lease), an area with its unit, sale or rent price, bedrooms, bathrooms, parking, amenities, tags such as Featured or Urgent Sale, a photo gallery, map coordinates, the agent and the owner. The board runs through Available, Reserved, Under Contract, Sold, Rented and Withdrawn.

**Inquiries.** An inquiry records the person, what they want (to buy, rent, lease or sell, or just information), the property if there is one, budget range, preferred location, source, priority and a follow-up date. It moves through new, assigned, in progress, and won or lost.

**Deals.** A transaction carries the property, buyer and seller, the agent, the agreed price, the token amount and date, and a commission percentage that gives the commission amount. It moves through negotiation, token paid, agreement signed and completed; completing it moves the property to the closed stage. Each deal has a payment schedule of token, installment, rent, final or commission lines, each marked pending, paid or overdue, and the deal shows what has been paid and what is outstanding. From a deal, one button creates the sale, rent or lease agreement, which prints as a PDF.

Access comes in three roles: Receptionist, Agent and Manager. An agent sees their own properties, inquiries and deals; a manager sees everything.

## The add-on: running a managed portfolio

The add-on adds a **Dashboard** at the top of the Real Estate menu and a **Management** menu with Cheques, Maintenance and Owner Statements. The dashboard is one screen for the property manager: occupancy, vacant units, the rent roll, leases running out in the next sixty days, cheques due to bank and bounced, rent cleared this month, quarter or year, open and urgent maintenance, statements waiting to be paid and the total owed to owners. Every figure opens the records behind it.

## How to set it up

1. Install Real Estate Management, then the Enterprise add-on if you manage property for owners.
2. Set users' roles under Settings, Users: Receptionist, Agent or Manager.
3. Check **Configuration** in the Real Estate app: property types, stages, amenities and tags. The default area unit and default commission rate are in the Real Estate settings.
4. Add agents as employees and owners and tenants as contacts.
5. Enter your properties, then open deals and agreements for the ones already let or sold.
6. On each active lease, fill in rent per period, how often it is paid, the deposit and the escalation, then build its cheque book.
7. Open the dashboard and check that occupancy and the rent roll match what you expect before you rely on it.

<Callout title="What these modules do not do">Nothing here posts to the accounts: cheques, payment schedules, owner statements and payouts are tracked records, not invoices or payments, so the bookkeeping still happens in Invoicing. Payment schedule lines are marked overdue by hand; there is no scheduled job that does it. Commission rules can be recorded per agent, but they are not applied to deals automatically; the commission percentage is entered on each deal. And there is no tenant or owner portal for leases, cheques or statements.</Callout>
