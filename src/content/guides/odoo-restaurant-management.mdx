A restaurant has more moving parts per square metre than almost any other business. Tables turn over, the kitchen works in rounds, a rider is somewhere on the road with your cash, the phone rings for a branch across town, an online order lands during the Friday rush, and every evening someone has to make the drawer add up. Most restaurants run that on paper chits, a till, a delivery app on a separate tablet and a spreadsheet for stock. Each part works on its own. None of them agree with each other at closing.

This guide is about running all of it on one Odoo 19 database, for one outlet or a chain of branches. It covers what a restaurant actually needs, what the standard Odoo Point of Sale already does, and where the Way4Tech Restaurant Suite fills the gaps. The chapters after this one go through each part of the day step by step, from setting up the first branch to reading the owner dashboard. The screenshots come from a two-branch demo restaurant, Lahore Grill House, with its Gulberg and DHA Phase 6 outlets.

## What a restaurant needs from its system

Leave the brochure language aside and a restaurant needs a short list of controls:

1. **An order that reaches the right station.** The waiter sends the table, the grill sees only grill items, the bar sees only drinks, with seat numbers and every option the guest asked for.
2. **A waiter who cannot take money.** The person who opens a table and the person who closes the drawer should not be the same login, and the system should enforce it, not the manager's memory.
3. **A name on every void.** Removing a dish after the kitchen has it, typing a price by hand, giving a large discount or refunding a paid order all cost money. Each one needs a reason and a manager.
4. **Cash that can be counted.** Cash, cards and mobile wallets recorded so they can be checked against the bank and the wallet portal, and a closing count note by note.
5. **Delivery with the cash coming back.** The right branch for the address, a rider assigned, the food out on time, and the cash the rider hands over matched to what was collected.
6. **Stock that follows the menu.** Every sold dish takes its ingredients out of the store, so the count at the end of the week shows what went missing.
7. **One view across branches.** Sales, covers, average ticket, best and worst dishes and staff, per branch or for the whole business.

## What standard Odoo does and does not do

Odoo 19 Point of Sale with the restaurant option turned on is a solid base. The [restaurant features documentation](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/sales/point_of_sale/restaurant.html) lists floors and tables, order management, notifying the kitchen or bar, printing and splitting bills, tips, courses, transferring and merging tables, and a booking button. The [pos_restaurant addon](https://github.com/odoo/odoo/tree/19.0/addons/pos_restaurant) that brings these is part of Community. If you run one cafe with a paper kitchen printer, that may be enough.

The gaps show up as soon as the restaurant grows or the owner wants control:

- **No kitchen screen on Community.** Odoo's preparation display is an Enterprise app. On Community the kitchen gets paper or nothing.
- **Printing needs an IoT box or an Epson ePOS printer.** Plain network thermal printers and USB printers are not reached directly.
- **No branches.** Odoo has companies and points of sale, but no branch with its own managers, opening hours, delivery zones and reports.
- **No manager PIN on risky actions,** and no waiter role that is refused at the payment screen.
- **No delivery riders, call center or online order inbox.**
- **No recipes, food cost or usage variance.** Stock is tracked per product, not per ingredient used by a dish.
- **No owner dashboard across branches** beyond the standard sales reporting.

You could build some of this with Studio and custom fields. The controls that matter most, a server that refuses a payment from a waiter or a void without a PIN, need code.

## What it runs on

- **Odoo 19,** Community or Enterprise, on Odoo.sh, a cloud server or a small PC inside the restaurant.
- **Tills and tablets:** any browser. Android tablets, iPads, phones, Windows touch terminals. The till installs as an app on the home screen.
- **Kitchen:** a tablet or wall screen per station, printers, or both.
- **Riders:** any smartphone.
- **Printers:** network thermal printers (Epson, Xprinter, Bixolon, Star and clones) straight from Odoo, or any printer Windows can see through the Way4Tech print server on a PC in the restaurant. No IoT box.

When the internet drops, the till keeps taking orders and payments and syncs when the line is back. Kitchen tickets wait on the till and are sent once, never twice. PIN approvals, the delivery board, the waitlist and the online inbox need the Odoo server, and refuse with a notice rather than half finishing. A restaurant with a poor line can run Odoo on a PC on its own network, so only online orders, messages, tax submission and the call center wait for the internet.

## Limitations

These are the suite's own statements about what it does not do:

- **No customer ordering website or app.** Online Orders receives orders from your channels; the page where customers browse and pay is yours, or a separate project that reads the menu and prices from this module.
- **Aggregators need their own accounts.** There is a Foodpanda style connector, but each marketplace still needs its partner account and store mapping.
- **No QR ordering at the table.** Odoo's own Self Ordering module does that and can be installed alongside; it is not part of the suite.
- **No payment gateway.** JazzCash, EasyPaisa and the other wallets are recorded with their transaction reference and checked against the merchant portal; a live gateway integration is separate work.
- **Tax registration is yours.** The Fiscal Compliance module is ready for FBR and the provinces, but the restaurant registers with the authority and moves the configuration out of sandbox.
