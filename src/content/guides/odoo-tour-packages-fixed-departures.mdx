A tour operator's risk sits in the calendar. Forty five seats on the Umrah departure on 19 December, a coach that needs fifteen paying passengers before it runs, three consultants selling the same seats from three desks. Sell too few and the departure loses money or has to be cancelled; sell too many and somebody is refunded at the last minute. The module treats each dated run of a tour as its own record with a seat count, so every file sold onto it comes off the same number.

## Packages: the product you sell more than once

Go to **Travel, Tours, Packages**. A package is the product, not the date. It carries:

- **Name and code**, printed on the brochure and the file.
- **Destination and country**, and a **kind**: escorted group, independent, cruise, pilgrimage, adventure or city break.
- **Nights**, with the days worked out.
- A one line **summary**, the **day by day itinerary** as the customer reads it, **inclusions** and **exclusions**, and a brochure image.
- **Net cost per person**, **sell price per adult**, **sell price per child** and the **single supplement**.
- **Minimum and maximum group** size. A minimum larger than the maximum is refused.

Margin per person and margin percent are worked out from cost and adult price. The package kanban shows each one with its image, how many departures it has and how many seats are sold across them.

## Departures: one dated run of a package

Under **Travel, Tours, Departures**, a departure is one run of a package with its own sequence reference, departure and return dates and a tour manager. The seat block holds:

- **Seats**: how many the operator has.
- **Guarantee At**: how many paying passengers it needs before it runs.
- **Seats Booked**, **Seats Available** and **Load Factor**, all counted from the files sold onto it.

Cost per person, adult price, child price and single supplement default from the package and can be changed for that date. **Expected margin** is seats booked times margin per person.

A guarantee threshold larger than the seat count is refused, and you cannot cut the seats below the number of passengers already booked.

### Departure states

A departure runs **Planned**, **On Sale**, **Guaranteed**, **Closed**, **Departed**, with **Cancelled** to the side. The buttons on it are Close Sales, Departed, Print Manifest and Cancel, plus Guarantee while it is on sale.

- **Guarantee** needs the departure on sale and the booked passengers at or above the threshold. The message tells you how many it has and how many it needs.
- **Cancel** is refused while the departure carries sold files. Cancel those files first, so the fees and refunds are worked out on each one.
- **Departed** cannot be pressed on a planned or cancelled departure.

A nightly job moves the programme on by itself: a planned departure with seats goes on sale, an on sale departure that reaches its minimum is guaranteed with a note on the chatter, a full one is closed, and one whose date has passed is marked departed. Like the other four nightly jobs, it ships switched off.

## Selling a file onto a departure

On a booking file, pick the **Package**, then the **Fixed Departure**. The departure list is limited to that package's departures, and picking a departure fills the package and the departure and return dates on the file. A departure that does not belong to the chosen package is refused.

Seats come off the departure when the file is **confirmed**, not when it is quoted. Confirm checks the departure first:

- A cancelled or departed departure is no longer selling, so the file will not confirm onto it.
- If the departure has fewer seats left than the file has passengers, Confirm stops and says how many are left and how many the file needs.

The seat count is worked out with full rights across the whole office. That matters: a consultant only sees their own files, and if the count were made with their rights it would only include their passengers and the coach would be oversold by everyone else.

Cancelling a file gives its seats back on its own, because seats are counted from the files still sold.

<Callout title="The package does not price the file">Choosing a package or a departure on a file fills the destination and the dates. It does not copy the package price or create segments. The file's total still comes from its itinerary segments, so build the flight, the hotels and the ground arrangements as segments with their net cost and sell price, exactly as for a tailor-made trip.</Callout>

## Watching the programme

**Reporting, Load Factor** graphs seats booked against seats total across departures, so you can see which dates need pushing and which are about to fill. The departures list shows the same figures as columns with the expected margin, and the package kanban shows seats sold per package.

Inside a departure, the **Files on This Departure** tab lists every file sold onto it with lead passenger, pax, total, amount due and state, so the tour manager can see who has not paid the balance.

## The passenger manifest

**Print Manifest** on the departure gives the tour manager the passenger list: each traveller with passport expiry, file reference and special requests. Special requests come from the traveller record on each file, which is why it is worth filling them in at booking rather than on the coach.

## What this part does not do

- **No seat map or rooming list by room.** Seats are a count. If you need to allocate rooms per passenger, that is outside the module.
- **No date based pricing grid.** Each departure can carry its own prices, but there is no season or occupancy matrix.
- **No online sale of departures.** Departures are sold by your consultants on booking files.
