Most FMCG distributors carry several manufacturers, called principals. The principals fund promotions, pay back part of the discount and the damaged goods, and judge the distributor on how their products look on the shelf. That money is easy to lose. A "buy 10, get 2 free" runs for three months, the free cartons are given away on the road, and at the month end nobody can say how many went out, at what cost, or how much the brand owes. The claim gets sent late with a spreadsheet the principal does not accept.

This chapter covers schemes, budgets, return on promotion, claims and the perfect store checks that prove shelf presence. All of it is in the Enterprise edition. It is part of the [FMCG distribution guide](/odoo-guides/odoo-distribution-management/).

## Brands and principals

Every product has a brand and every brand has a principal. On the demo, Sparkle and Fresh Valley belong to Northwind Beverages, Crunchy and Dairy Gold to Golden Harvest Foods, and Brightly and Pure Care to BrightHome Brands. Set the brand on the product; the principal follows. From then on, sales, stock, promotions and claims can all be read per principal, and the order booker can filter the catalogue by brand.

## Schemes

Open **Configuration, Schemes**. There are four types:

- **Buy X get Y free**, for example buy 10 Cola 1.5L, get 2 free.
- **Quantity slab discount**, a discount that grows with the quantity.
- **Amount slab discount**, a discount that grows with the order value.
- **Bundle price**, a fixed price for a set of products.

Each scheme can be limited to products, customer tags, territories, routes or price lists, with dates, a priority, a maximum per order and a stacking rule (exclusive or combined with others).

The **Funding and Budget** tab says who pays: the company, a principal, or shared by a percentage. That choice decides what later goes into the claim.

### How a scheme is applied

1. The scheme says what the shop gets: free goods, a slab discount or a bundle price.
2. It applies by itself in van sales, the counter, the order taker app and quotes, when the order matches its conditions and dates. On the phone, a green line on the shop says how many promotions apply.
3. Each promotion line stores its cost: free goods at real cost, discounts at the value given away.
4. The funder decides who pays. The principal's share is claimed back.

## Promotion budgets

**Principals, Promotion Budgets** sets a budget for a scheme or a principal over a period, with used, left and a status. A scheme budget counts the whole cost of the scheme; a principal budget counts what the principal pays back on every scheme it funds. Only confirmed orders count. The manager gets an alert at 80% and at 100%, and with **Stop the promotion when used up** ticked, the scheme stops by itself.

## Did the promotion pay?

**Promotion ROI** compares sales of the promotion's products during the promotion with a baseline: the same days last month, or the average of the four weeks before. The difference is the extra sales. Divided by the promotion cost, it gives the extra sales per 1 spent, with the part paid by the principal shown separately.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-trade-promotions-principal-claims/bo-pr-promo-roi.webp" alt="Promotion ROI list with six schemes, their type, dates, funder, sales in promotion, baseline sales, incremental sales, promotion cost, paid by principal and incremental sales per 1 spent" width={1600} height={1000} caption="Promotion ROI: sales against baseline, cost and who paid." />

Read it honestly. On the demo most schemes show negative extra sales against their baseline, which is exactly the kind of result this report exists to surface before the next scheme is agreed. **Promotion Cost** shows the same spend split between principal and company.

## Claims to principals

Open **Principals, Claims**. One claim covers one principal and one period.

1. Create the claim with the principal, the dates and how it will be settled.
2. **Fetch Lines** collects the principal's share of promotions, damaged and expired goods taken back, and price differences.
3. **Submit to Principal** and print the **Claim Form** for the principal to sign.
4. Accept it in full or in part, line by line, as the principal agrees.
5. Settle it by a credit note from the principal or as a receivable. **Claim Accounts** in Settings decide where the money is booked.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-trade-promotions-principal-claims/x-bo-claim.webp" alt="Claim CLM/2026/0003 to Golden Harvest Foods from 1 to 27 September, settle by credit note, with totals for promotions 37.80, damaged or expired 3.80, claimed 41.60, and lines for a damaged return and several promotion orders" width={1600} height={1000} caption="A claim built from the period's promotion lines and damaged returns." />

Expired returns sit in Quarantine until the claim is done, as the [counter and returns chapter](/odoo-guides/odoo-trade-counter-cash-and-carry/) explains. **Claim Ageing** shows what each principal still owes and for how long.

## The principal scorecard

For the monthly meeting with a principal, the **Principal Scorecard** shows for any period, per principal or per brand: net sales, gross margin, returns, promotion cost, stock value, days of stock, outlets that bought, numeric distribution and open claims. Days of stock is units on hand in warehouses and vans divided by units sold per day; numeric distribution is outlets that bought divided by outlets on active routes. It prints as a PDF.

## Perfect store checks

Brands pay for shelf presence, so the distributor needs proof. **Configuration, Store Checklists** holds one checklist per shop type, with weighted items:

- **Must-stock products** the shop should always have.
- **Share of shelf**: our facings against competitors, against a target share.
- **Yes or no questions**, such as price tags on our products or a clean fridge.
- **A photo of the shelf.**

Set the score that counts as a perfect store (85 on the demo).

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-trade-promotions-principal-claims/bo-cf-checklists-form.webp" alt="Store checklist Supermarket and mini market check with perfect store from 85, average score 80 percent, and weighted checks for must-stock range, share of shelf, price tags, end-cap display, fridge clean and photo of the shelf" width={1600} height={1000} caption="A checklist: what is checked, and how much each item weighs." />

At the shop, the order booker taps **Store check**, ticks the must-stock products he sees, counts our facings against competitors, answers the questions and takes the photo. A ring shows the score as he goes. The score is the weighted average out of 100.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-trade-promotions-principal-claims/o08-store-check.webp" alt="Store check on the phone for Sweet Tooth Confectionery with a score ring at 36, 85 for a perfect store, must-stock items Energy Drink and Hand Sanitizer ticked and Chewing Gum missing, share of shelf 30 of 30 and Save store check button" width={800} height={1720} caption="The store check on the phone, scoring as the rep works." />

**Store Checks** in the back office lists each check with its score, share of shelf, failed items and photos, and **Reporting, Perfect Store** shows scores by territory and shop type. Points and badges can reward store checks too, covered with the other team tools in [tracking and reports](/odoo-guides/odoo-field-sales-tracking-reports/).

<Callout title="Scope">Schemes, budgets, ROI, claims, the scorecard and store checks are Enterprise only. Price lists themselves are standard Odoo and work in every edition. The claim form is a printed document for the principal to sign. Lots and expiry dates are not tracked, so expired goods in a claim are counted from return reasons, not from batches.</Callout>
