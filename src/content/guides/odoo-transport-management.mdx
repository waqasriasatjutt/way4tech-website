A small haulier usually knows two numbers well: what came in from customers last month and what the diesel bill was. What it rarely knows is the number in between, which truck and which customer actually made money. A trip that looks good on the invoice can lose it on tolls, a waiting day, a driver allowance and a tyre nobody tied to that run. Odoo can hold every one of those costs against the trip that caused it, but only if trips, expenses and vehicles are joined up in one place. This guide covers what a transport operation needs from software, what standard Odoo gives you, and how the two Way4Tech transport modules fill the gap on Odoo 19 Community.

## Who this is for

The modules are written for businesses that run their own vehicles for other people's goods or passengers:

- **Hauliers and trucking companies** with tractor units, rigids, tankers, low loaders or reefers.
- **Distribution and delivery fleets** running city vans and box trucks for one or many customers.
- **Passenger transport**: staff buses, crew buses and coaster services for contractors and camps.
- **Companies that subcontract** part of their work to owner operators and need to pay them for it.

The live demo is set up as a fictional Eastern Province haulier with twelve trucks, eleven drivers, depots, ports and industrial sites, so most screens in this guide show Saudi plates and riyals. The modules themselves are not tied to one country: distance can be in kilometres or miles, document types are your own, and taxes follow the chart of accounts you install.

If your business moves freight as a forwarder, booking other people's ships, planes and trucks, the [freight forwarding guide](/odoo-guides/odoo-freight-forwarding/) is the closer fit. If you sell goods from your own vans, see the [van sales guide](/odoo-guides/odoo-van-sales/).

## What a transport business needs from its system

Strip it back and a fleet needs the same handful of things whatever it carries:

1. **A trip record.** Vehicle, driver, customer, origin, destination, departure and arrival, odometer out and in. Everything else hangs off this.
2. **Costs against the trip.** Fuel, tolls, weighbridge charges, parking, fines and allowances recorded when they happen, approved by someone, and paid or billed.
3. **Paperwork that does not lapse.** Registration, insurance, inspection certificates, operating cards and driver licences, each with an expiry date someone is warned about in time.
4. **A price the customer agreed.** A contract per customer, per lane, per tonne or per kilometre, so the charge on the invoice is not whatever the dispatcher typed.
5. **The real cost of a kilometre.** Lease payments, insurance, depreciation and road tax do not appear on a trip, but they decide whether a quote makes money.
6. **Pay that matches the work.** Drivers and subcontractors paid per trip, per kilometre or per drop, with advances taken off.

## What standard Odoo covers

Odoo 19 Community ships the [Fleet app](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/hr/fleet.html) ([fleet addon, 19.0 branch](https://github.com/odoo/odoo/tree/19.0/addons/fleet)). It keeps vehicles with models, plates and tags, assigns drivers, logs odometer readings, records services with a vendor and a cost, and tracks leasing and insurance contracts. That is a solid vehicle register, and the transport modules build on it rather than replacing it: your vehicles stay Fleet vehicles and your services stay Fleet service records.

What Fleet does not have is the business of moving things for money. There is no trip with a customer and a charge, no trip expenses with an approval flow, no invoice raised from a trip, no profit per trip or per vehicle, no driver licence expiry, no rate card, no cost per kilometre that includes standing costs and no driver settlement. The [Fleet module page](/modules/fleet/) on this site goes through the standard app in more detail.

[Transport Management Enterprise](https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/19.0/wt_transport_management_enterprise/) is a USD 90 add-on that needs the base. The base module is a trip logbook: it tells you what a trip cost after it ran. The add-on supplies the other half, what the job should have been charged and what the vehicle really costs to own, which is where most small fleets lose money without seeing it.

## How a trip works

Every trip moves through Draft, Scheduled, In Progress and Completed, then Invoiced once the invoice is raised. Picking a vehicle fills in its assigned driver and its current odometer. Completing the trip stamps the arrival time and moves the vehicle's odometer forward if the reading is higher. Distance comes from the two odometer readings, and profit is the trip charge minus the expenses booked to the trip and the driver allowance.

With the Enterprise add-on, the same trip gains tabs for stops, readiness and a true margin, and its charge is written by a confirmed charge sheet instead of being typed in. A trip with no stops and no loads still behaves exactly as it does in the base module, so installing the add-on does not change trips you already have.

## How to start

1. Install Fleet, Employees and Accounting with a chart of accounts for your country, then install Transport Management. A Transport menu appears.
2. In **Transport, Configuration, Settings**, choose kilometres or miles and the reminder lead times for documents, licences and services.
3. Add your depots, ports, customer sites, fuel stations and workshops under **Transport, Locations**.
4. Create or import your vehicles in **Transport, Fleet, Vehicles** and set each one's transport driver and base location.
5. Mark employees as drivers in the Driver / Transport tab of their employee form and enter licence numbers and expiry dates.
6. Review the shipped expense categories and document types under **Configuration** and add the ones your country uses.
7. Run a week of real trips and expenses, then check the dashboard and Trip Analysis with whoever does the books.
8. When you are ready to price from contracts and see true cost, install the Enterprise add-on. It pulls nothing in that the base does not already need.

<Callout title="What these modules do not do">There is no GPS tracking, no route optimisation, no drag and drop dispatch board and no driver mobile app, in either module. Drivers do not record anything from a phone; the office records trips, stops and fuel. The Enterprise add-on is English only today and carries some European driver fields (Driver CPC, tachograph card, ADR) alongside the licence. In the base module, the daily departure reminder adds a fresh to-do for each trip that is past its scheduled time and still not started, so old unstarted trips pile up reminders until someone starts or cancels them.</Callout>
