A transport company can be busy, invoice every trip and still lose money, because the costs that decide profit never appear on a trip. The lease payment on the tractor unit, the annual insurance, depreciation and the operating permit are paid whether the truck runs or not. Spread them over the kilometres it actually ran and a rate that looked fine is below cost. Fuel is the other leak: a few litres a fill disappears without anybody seeing it unless consumption is measured fill by fill. This chapter covers how the Enterprise add-on measures both, how tyres are followed, and how drivers and subcontractors are paid for the work.

## Standing costs

**Transport, Costing, Standing Costs** holds every commitment that is not tied to a trip. Each one has a cost type, an amount and a basis (per day, per month, per year or per distance unit), a validity window, and either one vehicle or a vehicle tag. A tag lets one record cover many vehicles, for example a fleet insurance policy or head office overhead on every company owned truck.

The cost types are depreciation, finance or hire purchase, lease or rental, insurance, road tax, operator licence, permit, inspection or certification, telematics or subscription, allocated overhead and other. Each row shows its cost per day, and a cash flag separates cash commitments from depreciation, so you can see a cash cost per kilometre as well as a full one.

## Vehicle cost periods

A nightly job builds a cost period per vehicle per month, or per quarter if the company is set that way. It refreshes the period in flight and closes the previous one. Each period shows:

- **Distance**: distance run, earning distance, empty running and its percentage, and the number of trips.
- **Cost**: running costs (fuel, driver and subcontractor pay), standing costs and how much of them is non cash, and the total.
- **Result**: revenue, margin and margin percentage, cost per distance unit, cash cost per distance unit, and break even per earning kilometre.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-truck-cost-per-km-fuel/cost-period.webp" alt="Vehicle cost period in Odoo for a tanker for October, marked incomplete because only 220 distance units were run, with running costs, fuel, driver and subcontractor pay, standing costs, total cost, revenue, a negative margin and cost per distance of 17.12 riyals" width={1440} height={900} caption="A cost period that is flagged incomplete: the figures show, but are not published." />

A period that cannot be trusted is flagged incomplete with the reason, and **Costing, Incomplete Figures** lists them. Complete periods publish a **true cost per distance** to the vehicle. On the vehicle you can also set a **target margin**; the add-on refuses a target of 100 per cent or more, because that would mean a price with no cost in it. **Rebuild** recomputes one period after you enter a lease you forgot, and **Costing, Rebuild Cost Periods** does it for a window and a set of vehicles.

### True margin per trip

Every trip in the add-on gets a true cost and a true margin beside the base module's net profit, which is left unchanged. When costs were booked against the trip, true cost is those costs plus the trip's share of standing costs. When nothing at all was booked, the trip falls back to the vehicle's published cost per distance times its distance. The rate is frozen onto the trip when it is completed. This is the figure a charge sheet is compared with when it is flagged below cost, as described in the [rate cards chapter](/odoo-guides/odoo-transport-rate-cards-billing/).

## Fuel entries and fuel alerts

**Transport, Fleet, Fuel Entries** holds every fill: date, vehicle, driver, volume, total, odometer and whether the tank was filled to full. Entries come from three places: typed by hand, imported from a fuel card statement, or created from a fuel expense.

Consumption is measured tank to tank, only between two consecutive full fills, and compared with a **baseline** for the vehicle. A baseline is either an agreed figure someone has written down or a rolling average the add-on builds; an agreed figure wins over a rolling one, and a vehicle baseline wins over a company one. Baselines are dated, so an alert from March is still explained against the figure in force in March.

Six checks run on each fill:

1. **Over consumption** against the vehicle's baseline.
2. **More than the tank holds.**
3. **Odometer went backwards** since the last fill.
4. **Impossible distance** between two fills for the days between them.
5. **Two fills too close** together on the same vehicle.
6. **Fill while off road**, on a vehicle the workshop has grounded.

Each alert carries the money it represents and the list is sorted by that value, so an hour a week on fuel goes on the worst case first. Where a check cannot price what it found, such as an odometer typed wrongly, it records no value rather than a guess. Alerts move through New, Investigating, Confirmed, Recovered and Dismissed.

### Importing a fuel card statement

**Fleet, Import Fuel Card Statement** reads a CSV file from your fuel card provider. You set the separator, encoding, decimal mark, date format and whether the first row holds headings, then name the columns: date, vehicle, card, driver, station, transaction reference, volume, unit price, total, odometer and full tank. Vehicles are matched by number plate, chassis or VIN, vehicle name, or a fuel card used before.

**Check File** is a dry run: it reads everything, shows each row as Will Import, Already Imported or Problem with the raw text, and writes nothing. **Import** then creates entries for the ticked rows only. Unknown stations are only created if you allow it.

## Tyres

**Fleet, Tyres** follows each casing through its life rather than "six tyres on a truck". A tyre has a brand, size, the vehicle and wheel position it is on (axle, side, single or inner and outer), tread depth, number of retreads, distance covered, total cost and cost per distance. Events are fit, rotate, inspect, remove, retread and scrap, each with the odometer at the time. Two casings cannot sit on one position, and nothing can happen to a casing after it is scrapped, which catches the common mistake of logging against the wrong tyre.

## Paying drivers: the base payroll

The base module has a simple payroll in **Transport, Drivers, Payroll**: a basic salary for the period, components typed as allowance, bonus, deduction or other, and an expense reimbursement that **Compute Reimbursement** fills from the employee's approved and paid transport expenses in the period. Gross and net are worked out, and the record goes Draft, Confirmed, Paid. A Payslip prints from it.

<Shot src="/odoo-guides/odoo-truck-cost-per-km-fuel/driver-payroll.webp" alt="Transport payroll record in Odoo for one month with housing and transport allowance lines and a GOSI employee share deduction, allowances, bonuses, expense reimbursement, gross pay and deductions" width={1440} height={900} caption="Base payroll: basic, typed components and reimbursed expenses." />

It does not calculate statutory deductions or post to Accounting: the GOSI line in the demo was typed. For country payroll on Odoo Community, see [running payroll on Odoo Community](/odoo-guides/run-payroll-odoo-community/).

## Settlement runs (Enterprise)

A settlement run pays one payee for the work in a window, and the same model handles an **employed driver** and a **subcontractor**, because the trade pays both the same way.

- **Pay schemes** in **Configuration, Pay Schemes** are built from components: per distance, per tonne, per pallet, per drop, per hour on duty, per waiting hour, per day, per trip, a percentage of revenue or a fixed amount, each an earning or a deduction. A scheme can be limited to drivers, subcontractors or anyone, and a trip type.
- **Gather Trips** rebuilds the run's lines from the payee's trips in the window. Lines typed by hand are kept. Trip allowances can be included.
- **Advances** handed out for fuel floats, tolls or a salary advance are records with a running balance, recovered all on the next settlement or by instalments. Each recovery line names the advance it came off.
- **Confirm** freezes the figures. **Post** then creates the payment document: a self billed vendor document for a subcontractor, or a transport payroll record for an employed driver. **Mark Paid** closes it.

Subcontractors live in **Drivers, Subcontractors** as carriers: owner operator, haulage company, broker or forwarder, or driver agency. A carrier has a vendor contact for the self bill, a default pay scheme and a goods in transit policy with an expiry that the readiness check reads. A carrier never appears in payroll.

Settlement lines have their own type for damage and fuel recoveries, and the recovery on an agreed claim goes onto the responsible payee's next open run, as described in the [rate cards chapter](/odoo-guides/odoo-transport-rate-cards-billing/).

<Callout title="What the money side does not do">Fuel entries are not read from telematics or fuel tank sensors; they come from people, card statements or expenses. Cost periods are only as complete as the standing costs you enter. The employed driver's settlement lands in the transport payroll, which has no statutory calculations and no journal entry, so connect it to your real payroll process. Settlements must be in the company's own currency.</Callout>

## Setting it up

1. Enter standing costs for each vehicle: lease or finance, depreciation, insurance, road tax and permits. Use tags for fleet wide policies and overhead.
2. Choose monthly or quarterly cost periods, wait for the nightly build or run **Rebuild Cost Periods**, then clear what **Incomplete Figures** lists.
3. Set a target margin on each vehicle class you quote.
4. Enter tank capacities and either agreed fuel baselines or a few weeks of full fills so rolling baselines can build. Map your fuel card statement once.
5. Register tyre casings on the vehicles where tyre spend matters most.
6. Write your pay schemes, add subcontractors as carriers, enter outstanding advances, and run a first settlement alongside your current method before switching.
