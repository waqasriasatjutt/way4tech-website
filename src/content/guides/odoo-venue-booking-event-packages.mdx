The two most expensive mistakes in a function business both happen at the booking stage. One is holding the same room for two parties, or for one party with no time to turn the room round after the last. The other is selling a package that loses money, because nobody added up what the canapes, the wine and the centrepieces cost per head before it went on the price list. This chapter covers the venue, the package and the first money, in the [Event Management](/odoo-guides/odoo-event-management/) module for Odoo 19 Community.

## Venues: what the room can take

Each venue sits under **Configuration, Venues**. The record holds what the diary needs to know about the room:

- **Kind**: your own venue, a partner venue, client premises, or an outdoor or marquee site.
- **Seated and standing capacity.** No layout may seat more than the seated figure, and a standing reception layout is capped at the standing figure.
- **Rooms available.** A hotel with two function suites can run two events at once; a single hall runs one.
- **Turnaround (h).** How long the room needs between events to strip and reset.
- **Curfew.** The latest the music can play, as a clock time such as 23:30.
- **Hire fee.** Copied onto the event budget as the venue hire line when the event is quoted.
- **Licences.** Alcohol licence number and expiry, public entertainment licence number and expiry, with a status of in date, expiring soon or expired.
- **Access and notes**: where the vans go, door widths, lifts, anything the crew needs.

### Layouts and the crew call

A venue has one or more layouts: banquet rounds, long tables, theatre, cabaret, boardroom, U shape or standing reception. Each layout carries its own capacity, the number of tables it puts in the room, and the set up hours it takes to dress. The event's **Venue Access** time is worked back from the guests' arrival by those set up hours (three hours if the layout does not say), so the crew call is a real time. A layout belongs to one venue, and an event cannot use a layout from a different one.

## The double booking check

The clash check runs whenever an event's venue, dates or status change. It looks for other events at the same venue that overlap the window, with the venue turnaround added to both ends. If the clashes already fill every room the venue has, the save is refused with the name of the event in the way and the turnaround it needs.

Two details matter in practice:

- **Enquiries do not hold the room.** Only events at Proposal Sent or later count, so you can take three enquiries for the same Saturday and the first to be quoted wins.
- **Cancelled events release the room** straight away.

Capacity is checked on the same save. The working number (final numbers if set, otherwise expected) has to fit the layout's capacity, or the venue's seated capacity if no layout is picked, plus an overbooking allowance set in Settings (5 per cent by default; set it to zero where the fire officer counts heads).

<Callout title="Licences and curfew are checked at confirmation">Event types say whether they need an alcohol licence or a public entertainment licence. Confirm the Date refuses if the venue has no licence number on file, or if the licence expires before the event day. It also refuses an event that finishes after the venue curfew, read in local time, unless the curfew extension box is ticked because the venue agreed in writing.</Callout>

## Event types

Event types live under **Configuration, Event Types**. The module ships ten (wedding, civil ceremony and lunch, corporate dinner, conference, product launch, awards night, birthday party, anniversary, charity gala, Christmas party). Each has a typical length used to fill the finish time, a usual lead time in days that flags short notice enquiries, the two licence flags, a wedding flag that turns on guest side and top table fields, and a usual package offered first on a new enquiry.

## Packages priced the way the trade sells them

Packages sit under **Configuration, Packages**. A package is sold either **per head** or as a **flat fee**, and carries:

- **Price per head** or the flat fee, and the product it is invoiced as, so revenue lands on the right income account with the right tax.
- **Minimum numbers.** The package is not sold below this headcount.
- **Covers (h).** How many hours of service the price covers. It also sets the finish time on an event using the package.
- **Sold for.** The event types it is offered on. An event cannot use a package that is not sold for its type.
- **Inclusions.** Each line has a category (catering, drinks, staffing, sound and lighting, decor and flowers and so on), a quantity per head, what one costs you, and a note for the proposal. Three canapes a head is a quantity of 3; one centrepiece per table of ten is 0.1.

From the inclusions, the package shows its cost per head, margin per head and margin percentage. For a flat fee package the margin is worked out per head at the minimum numbers.

## From enquiry to proposal

1. Create the event under **Diary, Events**: title, client, event type, venue, layout, package, the guests' arrival time, the expected number and the contracted minimum. Picking the type offers its usual package; picking the package raises the contracted minimum to the package minimum if it was lower.
2. Check the **Charges** tab. The package line is kept in step with the headcount: per head packages charge the guests charged figure (final numbers, otherwise expected, never below the contracted minimum); flat fee packages charge one fee. Add extra charge lines for anything outside the package.
3. Press **Send Proposal**. It needs a venue and at least one charge. It builds the estimated budget from the package inclusions plus the venue hire fee, creates the payment schedule, moves the event to Proposal Sent and emails the proposal if the client has an email address.
4. Print the client proposal from the Charges tab when you want a PDF: package description, inclusions, the charges, tax and total, and the payment schedule.

## The payment schedule and the deposit

When the event is quoted, the module builds a standard schedule of three stages:

| Stage | Share | Due |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit to hold the date | Deposit percentage from Settings (25 by default) | Seven days from today, or the event day if sooner |
| Interim payment | Half of the remainder | 60 days before the event |
| Balance on final numbers | The other half | The final numbers deadline (14 days before by default) |

You can change any stage or add fixed amount stages on the Payments tab, and an event with no schedule gets the standard one from the "Build the standard payment schedule" link there. The schedule refuses to add up to more than the event is worth, allows only one deposit, and refuses a stage due more than a month after the event.

Each stage is billed with **Raise the Invoice**, which creates a draft customer invoice in Accounting against the package product, with the event's tax. When the money is in, **Mark Paid** records it. With the default setting on, **Confirm the Date** stays refused until the deposit stage is marked paid.

### Chasing what is behind

A stage counts as behind once it is past its due date plus the grace days from Settings (three by default, because bank transfers take time). **Money, Overdue Payments** lists those stages and **Money, Events Behind on Payment** lists the events. The scheduled job "Events: chase overdue payment milestones" emails the client and notes the chase on the record, once a day at most per stage. It ships switched off; turn it on under Settings, Technical, Scheduled Actions (developer mode) once your mail server is set up.

<Callout title="Marking paid is a manual step">The milestone does not watch the invoice. When the client pays and your accountant reconciles it in Accounting, someone still presses Mark Paid on the stage. The invoice's payment status is shown beside the stage so the two are easy to compare.</Callout>

## Questions to settle before you load your venues

- Do you want enquiries to hold a room? In this module they do not. If your venue pencils dates in for a week, quote them sooner or keep pencil bookings as a note.
- What overbooking allowance does each venue accept? The setting is company wide, so set it to the strictest venue you work with.
- Is your package price the same for children? The package has one price per head. Child pricing goes on a separate charge line.
