Odoo's website app is a perfectly good site builder, and most businesses that run Odoo never use it, because an empty page and a palette of generic blocks is a long way from a website that looks like a dental clinic or a law firm. A theme closes that gap. It does not add features to the website app. It gives you a finished homepage, four inner pages, menu entries, a colour palette and fonts, all written for one trade, so the work left is replacing sample text with yours rather than designing from nothing.

This series covers the seventeen Way4Tech website themes for Odoo 19 and the two back office themes. It is written from the module code and from the live demos, and it says plainly where a theme stops.

## What standard Odoo gives you, and what a theme adds

Odoo 19 Website already has the editor, drag and drop blocks, a theme tab for colours and fonts, page and menu management, SEO fields, languages and a form block that sends email or creates leads. Odoo also ships its own free themes, which are general purpose. What it does not give you is content: the words, the sections and the page structure that a clinic, a gym or an estate agent actually needs.

Each Way4Tech theme adds:

- **Nine sections** (Odoo calls them snippets) written for the trade: an opening banner with a headline, two buttons and three stat figures; a six card services grid; four reasons to choose you; a showcase grid that changes by trade; a team; three testimonials; a questions accordion; a call to action band; and a contact block with address, hours and a form.
- **A homepage** made of those nine sections.
- **Four inner pages**, published and indexed: About, a services page, a showcase page and Contact. The salon theme adds a fifth, Team.
- **Menu entries** for those pages, and it removes Odoo's own Contact us menu entry once, at install.
- **One five colour palette** named after the theme, Google fonts for headings, body, menu and buttons, and defaults for the header and footer layout and the button corners.

The showcase is where the trade shows. The gym theme has six membership plans with prices, the estate agent six featured listings, the restaurant six dishes, the event theme a schedule, the SaaS theme a pricing grid. Every card is text on the page. No theme reads Odoo records, so your listings, rooms or menu do not update the site by themselves.

## The step people miss: picking the theme

Installing a theme from Apps brings in its pages and menu entries, but Odoo loads a theme's stylesheet, palette and fonts only for a website whose theme is set to that module. If you install it and stop there, the text and layout appear but the banner, button shapes and coloured bands look plain. The fix is one click: open the editor, go to the Theme tab, click Switch Theme and pick Use this theme on the card. The [install and customise chapter](/odoo-guides/odoo-website-theme-install-customise/) walks through it with screenshots.

<Callout title="What the themes do not do">No theme takes bookings, payments, orders, tickets or donations, and none reads Odoo products, rooms, events or listings. The buttons are links you point at the right page or app. The contact form in the code posts to an address Odoo 19 does not answer, so swap it for Odoo's Form block before you publish. On every theme except salon, the theme's sections are on the pages it creates but not in the Blocks panel; you reuse them by duplicating. The theme's own sections take their colours from the theme stylesheet, so the Theme tab palette recolours Odoo's blocks, header and footer but not those sections.</Callout>

## The backend themes

Two more modules change the screens your staff see after they sign in, not the public website. **Enterprise Backend Theme Lite** (USD 10) gives Community the Enterprise look: a full page app grid as the home screen, a white top bar and the purple and teal palette. **Backend Theme with Color Picker** (USD 15) adds seven colour presets, 22 individual colours, dark mode and a side menu layout. They cannot be installed together. The [backend themes chapter](/odoo-guides/odoo-backend-theme-community/) covers both.

## Which theme fits

Pick by the showcase, because that is the section that is hardest to rebuild yourself: plans and prices (gym, SaaS), listings or trips (real estate, travel), dishes or rooms (restaurant, hotel), cases or wins (consulting, law firm), projects (construction, photography), products (eCommerce, fashion), impact figures (NGO), graduates (education), doctors (clinic), a schedule (event). If none matches your trade, take the one whose showcase is closest and retitle it; the sections are plain text and icons, so a consulting theme becomes an accounting firm's site with an afternoon of typing.

## What it costs to get a site live

The theme itself is USD 39 and the Website app is part of Odoo Community, so the software cost of a basic company site is that one module. The time goes into content, most of it into text and photographs. Budget for real photographs on the themes that ship with icons, for replacing the contact form, and for linking each booking or shop button to an app that actually takes the order.
