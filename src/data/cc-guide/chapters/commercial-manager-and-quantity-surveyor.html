The contract

Every job has a contract: Contracts & Setup › Contracts.

It holds:

  • the employer, the contract sum and the form of contract (for example FIDIC 2017 Red),
  • the dates: possession, completion, extensions of time, practical completion and the defects period,
  • retention: percentage, limit and the first moiety,
  • the payment cycle: valuation day, days to certify and days to pay,
  • liquidated damages: rate, period and cap.

Tip

Set the Standard Form on the contract. The notice periods and time bars of that form then apply to every notice you raise (see "Notices and time bars" below).

Step 1. Measure the work

  1. Open Commercial & Billing › Measurement › Measurement Book and click New.
  1. Enter the project, contract, date and who measured and witnessed.
  2. Add a line for each bill item measured: the location, and the number, length, width and depth. The quantity is worked out for you.
  1. Click Book as Measured. When the client's side has agreed the figures, click Mark Agreed, then Certify.

Watch out

A sheet that takes an item beyond its bill quantity is refused. Extra quantity is a variation: raise one.

Note: If an inspection and test plan has an open hold point on a bill item, measurement of that item is blocked until the hold point is released.

Step 2. Raise the valuation

  1. Open Commercial & Billing › Valuations and Certificates › Raise a Valuation.
  1. Pick the Contract and the Valuation Date. The system pulls certified measurement, agreed variations and signed dayworks up to that date.
  2. Enter materials on site and time-related preliminaries.
  3. Check the gross to date, this period, retention, advance recovery and the amount due.
  4. Click Create and Value (or Create as Draft to review it first).

Step 3. Apply for payment

On the valuation, click Apply for Payment. The application records what you claim, with the date payment is due and the final date for payment. Click Submit when it is sent to the client.

Step 4. The payment certificate

When the client's certifier replies:

  1. On the application, click Certify. A certificate opens, filled with what you claimed.
  2. On each line, enter what was certified. Where it differs, write the reason.
  1. Check retention, previously certified, advance recovery and the amount payable.
  2. Click Issue Certificate. Retention is written to the retention ledger and finance can raise the invoice.

Why it matters

The shortfall column (claimed less certified) shows exactly what you were not paid and why. It is the starting list for the next valuation and for the final account.

Commercial & Billing › Valuations and Certificates › Payment Certificates lists all certificates with claimed, certified, payable, shortfall and due dates.

Step 5. Invoice

Finance clicks Create Invoice on the issued certificate (Chapter 18). You can see the invoices under Finance › Invoices and Bills › Client Invoices.

Step 6. The monthly cost value reconciliation (CVR)

The CVR compares value with cost at the month end and gives the true margin.

  1. Open Commercial & Billing › Cost Value Reconciliation › Reconciliations, then New. Pick the contract and the period end.
  2. Click Pull Figures. The value side (certified, measured not certified, variations agreed and submitted) and the cost side (actual, accrued, provisions) fill in.
  1. Click Raise Accruals to accrue anything delivered or done but not yet billed.
  2. Review each cost code line. Add provisions for known risks.
  3. Click Sign Off. The CVR is locked and its margin feeds Margin by Job and the dashboards.

Tip

Dashboards & Reports › Cost and Margin › Jobs Losing Money lists every CVR with a negative margin.

Variations

  1. Commercial & Billing › Variations and Claims › Site Instructions: record each instruction the day it is received, even if verbal.
  2. Commercial & Billing › Variations and Claims › Variations, New: describe the change, link the instruction, choose the valuation method and price it.
  1. Submit it to the client. When the client agrees, click Agree (through approval if the amount needs it). It adds to the revised contract value and flows into the next valuation.
  2. Commercial & Billing › Variations and Claims › Variation Exposure shows the value of variations not yet settled.

Also under Variations: Daywork Sheets (work paid by time and materials, signed by the client's representative) and Provisional Sums (allowances in the bill, spent and adjusted).

Subcontractors

  • Procurement & Stores › Subcontracting › Subcontract Packages: tender a package to several firms, compare levelled quotes and Award it.
  • Commercial & Billing › Subcontractor Accounts › Subcontract Valuations: value the subcontractor's work each month at their rates, Certify, then Create Bill.
  • Order Variations change the order value. Contra Charges deduct costs you paid on their behalf from the next certificate.

Watch out

If supplier compliance is switched on, certifying or paying a subcontractor whose insurance or licence has expired may be blocked. The red banner on the valuation says what is missing.

Retention

Commercial & Billing › Retention › Retention Ledger shows every amount held and every release, with due dates.

  • Nobody Chased This: releases that are due and not paid.
  • Snags Holding Retention: open defects that stop a release.

Notices and time bars

Contracts give strict periods for notices (for example 28 days to notify a claim). Miss one and the right may be lost.

  1. From a delay event, variation, instruction or claim, click Raise Contract Notice. Or open Contracts & Setup › Contract Administration › Notices Register and click New.
  2. Choose the notice type. The clause, time bar and Due By date fill in.
  3. Write the letter and click Print Letter.
  4. Click Issue when it is served.

Contracts & Setup › Contract Administration › Time Bars This Week and Time Bar Calendar show what is due. Alerts are raised at 7, 3 and 1 day before, and when a date passes.

The final account

At the end of the job, Closeout › Final Account › Final Accounts draws the statement: original sum, agreed variations, dayworks, provisional sum adjustments, fluctuations, claims and liquidated damages.

Everything Holding a Job Open lists every item still open: unagreed variations, unsigned dayworks, open claims, unissued certificates, unreleased retention and open snags.
