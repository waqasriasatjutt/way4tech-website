The estimator prices work before the company wins it. You run tenders from first sight to award, build the estimate on the bill of quantities, keep the rate library, and look after bid bonds. When a tender is won, your bill becomes the contract bill and the budget for the job.

What you see when you log in

Your dashboard is the Tender / Estimating Dashboard. Quick actions: Tenders and New Tender.

Tenders

The pipeline

Sales & Tenders › Tenders shows every tender as a card in its stage.

Running a tender

  1. Click New (or New Tender on Home).
  2. Enter the client, the project name, the submission date, the expected value and the chance of winning.
  3. Work through the Go / No-Go checklist on the tender. Decide whether to bid.
  1. Build the estimate: create or import the bill of quantities for the tender and price each line (see below).
  2. Record the bid bond if one is required (see "Bid bonds").
  3. Move the tender to Submitted when it goes in.
  4. When the result comes: - Won: Award creates the project, the contract and the contract bill from your estimate. - Lost: Mark Lost, with the reason and the winning price if known. Sales & Tenders › Competitor Analysis builds up a picture of who wins and at what price.

Why it matters

The loss reasons and competitor prices make the next bid better. Lost Value by Reason on the dashboard shows where you keep losing.

The bill of quantities and the estimate

Contracts & Setup › Bills of Quantities › Bills of Quantities lists every bill, tender estimates included.

Each item has the client rate (value) and the estimated cost (budget). Price it in one of three ways:

  1. Type the rate directly.
  2. Pick a rate build-up from the rate library: labour, plant, materials and subcontract components with their outputs and waste.
  3. Copy from a bill template (Configuration › Jobs and Bills › Bill Templates).

Importing a bill from Excel

  1. Open the bill and click Import from Excel (or Import the Bill from Excel in the setup wizard).
  2. Click Download Template, fill it in (section, item code, description, unit, quantity, rate), and upload it.
  3. Check the preview. Rows with problems are marked.
  4. Click Confirm.

See Chapter 80 for the template columns.

The rate library

Configuration › Jobs and Bills › Rate Library holds your standard rates, each built from its resources.

Tip

Keep the rate library up to date with real outputs from finished jobs. Dashboards & Reports › Dashboards › Labour Productivity shows the actual output against the norm.

Bid bonds

Sales & Tenders › Bond Register holds the bid bonds. Bid Bonds lists the ones expiring soon.

  1. On the tender, record the bond amount and the bank.
  2. In the bond register, Request from Bank, then enter the number and dates and click In Force.
  3. When the tender is decided, Request Release, then Released.

Client quotations

For smaller works priced as a quotation, use Sales & Tenders › Opportunities and Quotations. A tender estimate can be turned into a client quotation.

The estimate workspace

An estimate is where you build the price of a tender: its structure, the cost of every item, the takeoff, the quotes from subcontractors and suppliers, and the markup. You can keep several versions.

Starting an estimate

  1. Open the tender and click Start Estimate at the top. If the tender already has a priced bill, the estimate starts from it.
  2. The Estimates button on the tender lists every version. All estimates are under Sales & Tenders › Estimating › Estimates.
  1. On the Structure tab, add the sections: a code and a name, with a parent for sub-sections (1, 1.1, 1.2).
  2. On Cost Items, add a line per bill item: reference, description, unit, quantity, and where the rate comes from: Rate Build-Up, Selected Quote or Typed Rate. The cost and price columns fill in.
  3. Click Push to Bill to write the items into the tender bill with cost rates, sell rates, build-ups and cost codes.

Versions

  • New Version copies everything into the next version (v2, v3) and marks the old one superseded. Write what changed in the line under the title.
  • Compare Versions shows every item as changed, added, removed or the same, with the cost difference and the price and margin of each version.

The resource library and price books

The estimating library (Sales & Tenders › Estimating Library) holds the resources every build-up uses.

Resource type How its cost is set
Labour Rate per hour, or Use Payroll Cost Rate to take the trade's cost per hour from HR. Click Refresh Rates after a pay change.
Crew A gang of labour and plant resources (for example 1 mason and 4 labourers). Cost per hour is the sum of its members.
Plant Hire or ownership rate per hour, plus fuel and operator unless the hire is operated.
Material Supplier price lists with validity dates, minimum order and lead time. The cost is the cheapest valid price, or the preferred supplier's.
Subcontract and Other A base rate.

Regional Price Books are per country and city, with their own currency and a location factor.

Rate build-ups (unit rate analysis)

  1. Create a build-up with a unit (for example m3), the gang's Output per Hour and the Gang Hours per Cycle (usually 8).
  2. Add components from the library. For labour and plant enter the Number in the gang. For materials enter the Qty per Unit.
  3. The net cost per unit and its labour, plant, material and subcontract split appear. Overhead and margin give the sell rate.
  4. Tick Reusable Template to offer it to other estimates.
  5. On an estimate, Apply Build-Up to Items applies one build-up to many items: Link (all follow the template) or Copy (each gets its own copy).

Tip

Refresh from Library re-reads every resource rate after prices change.

Quantity takeoff

  1. Create a takeoff sheet per drawing: drawing number and revision, and the drawing file. A warning shows if a newer revision has been issued.
  2. Add a row per measurement: item, location, times, length, width, height. For other shapes write a Formula using L, W, H and N. Tick Deduct for openings.
  3. Click Use as Item Quantities. Each item takes its quantity from the net total of its rows.

You can also import takeoff from Excel with Import Takeoff from Excel on the estimate (columns: Item ref, Description, Times, Length, Width, Height, Deduct, Formula). Download Template gives a filled example.

Subcontractor and supplier enquiries

  1. Create an enquiry: package name, subcontract or supply, trade, the estimate items in it, the scope and the date quotes are due.
  2. Add Approved Vendors fills in the vendors from the approved vendor list for that trade.
  3. Send Enquiry emails them.
  4. Record Quote for each reply: rates per item, validity, inclusions and exclusions.
  5. The Comparison tab lines the quotes up, lowest first, with the lowest rate per item in green and the exclusions underneath.
  6. Select on a quote carries its rates into the estimate.

Watch out

The cheapest quote is often the one with the most exclusions. Read the exclusions before you select.

Markup, summary and approval

The Markup Sheet tab adds overheads, risk, profit, preliminaries, bond cost and escalation, each worked out in order. A new estimate starts with time-related preliminaries, overheads 5 percent, risk 2.5 percent and profit 8 percent.

The Tender Summary tab shows cost by type, direct and indirect cost, profit, discount, the tender price, the margin and the spread factor used to turn cost rates into bill rates.

  1. Click Send for Review. The commercial manager gets an activity.
  2. The commercial manager or director clicks Approve. The tender's go / no-go score must reach the company minimum, or a director approves and the override is written on the estimate.
  3. Click Mark Submitted. The estimate is pushed to the bill, the version is closed and the tender moves to submitted.

Programme import

A tender or project programme can be imported from Primavera P6 (.xer) or MS Project (.xml) with Import Programme on the tender, and exported back to MS Project XML.

When the tender is won

Award on the tender pushes the current estimate to the bill and copies it to the new project: quantities, cost rates (the approved budget), sell rates, build-ups and cost codes. A Resource Budget is written on the project (labour and plant hours, materials and subcontract packages) and the tender programme becomes a draft programme on the project.

Why it matters

The estimate becomes the budget the site is measured against. Nothing is typed twice, and the project team can see exactly how each rate was built.
