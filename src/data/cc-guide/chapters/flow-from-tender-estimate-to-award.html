This flow prices a tender in detail and carries the price into the job. The estimate holds the work breakdown and cost items, rates come from build-ups, quotes or typed rates, quantities come from takeoff, the markup sheet turns cost into a tender price, and on award the approved estimate becomes the job budget.

Tip

Open Home › How it works to see the processes as step cards with what is waiting for you at each step, and press Open to jump to the screen. Each document also shows a What's next banner that says what the current stage means and who acts next.

The flow at a glance

Blue boxes are steps. Orange boxes are checks or holds that can stop the flow.

Who does what

Step Who
Estimate, takeoff, enquiries Estimator
Approve Commercial Manager or Director
Award Commercial Manager

Step 1. Start the estimate from the tender

  1. Open the tender in Sales & Tenders › Tenders.
  2. Press Start Estimate at the top of the tender. If the tender already has a priced bill, the estimate starts from it.
  3. The Estimates smart button on the tender lists every version.

Why it matters: One place holds the whole price, version by version.

Step 2. Build the structure and the cost items

  1. On the Structure tab add the sections: code and name, with a parent for sub-sections (1, 1.1, 1.2).
  2. On Cost Items add a line per bill item: reference, description, unit, quantity and where the rate comes from (Rate Build-Up, Selected Quote or Typed Rate).

Step 3. Measure the quantities (takeoff)

  1. Press Takeoff on the estimate, or open Sales & Tenders › Estimating › Takeoff Sheets.
  2. Create a sheet per drawing and add a row per measurement: item, location, times, length, width, height. Tick Deduct for openings. For other shapes write a formula with L, W, H and N.
  3. Press Use as Item Quantities. Or use Import Takeoff from Excel on the estimate.

Why it matters: Quantities are traceable to a drawing and revision.

Step 4. Price with build-ups from the library

  1. Keep labour, crews, plant and materials in Sales & Tenders › Estimating Library › Resource Library and the price books.
  2. In a build-up, set the unit, the gang output per hour and the gang hours per cycle, and add the components.
  3. On the estimate press Apply Build-Up to Items. Refresh from Library re-reads every rate.

Why it matters: Every rate can be explained line by line.

Step 5. Get subcontract and supply prices

  1. Open Sales & Tenders › Estimating › Sub and Supplier Enquiries, create an enquiry per package, press Add Approved Vendors and Send Enquiry.
  2. Press Record Quote for each reply. The Comparison tab lines the quotes up, lowest first.
  3. Press Select on the chosen quote. Its rates are carried into the estimate items.

Why it matters: Only approved vendors are asked, and the choice is on record.

Step 6. Mark up and summarise

  1. Fill the Markup Sheet tab: overheads, risk, profit, time related preliminaries, bond and escalation lines.
  2. Read the Tender Summary tab: direct cost, indirect cost, profit, tender price and margin.
  3. For a new version press New Version; Compare Versions shows what changed.

Step 7. Approve and submit

  1. The estimator presses Send for Review. The commercial manager gets an activity and finds it in Sales & Tenders › Estimating › Estimates to Approve.
  2. The commercial manager or a director presses Approve. Below the company go / no-go minimum, only a director can approve, and the override is written on the estimate.
  3. Press Mark Submitted: the estimate is pushed to the tender bill and the tender moves to the submitted stage.

Why it matters: A tender cannot reach the submitted stage without an approved estimate (this check can be switched off in Settings).

Step 8. Award: the estimate becomes the budget

  1. When the tender is won, press Award on the tender as usual.
  2. The project bill carries the estimate quantities, cost rates (the approved budget), sell rates, build-ups and cost codes.
  3. A Resource Budget is written on the project (Dashboards & Reports › Cost and Margin › Resource Budgets) and the tender programme becomes a draft programme on the project.

Why it matters: The job is controlled against the same figures it was priced with.
