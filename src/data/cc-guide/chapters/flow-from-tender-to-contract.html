This flow follows a bid from the first sight of an opportunity to a signed contract with a project and a contract bill. One record, the tender, carries the whole story: qualify it, price it, hear back. When it is won, one action creates the project, the contract and the site bill.

Tip

Open Home › How it works to see the processes as step cards with what is waiting for you at each step, and press Open to jump to the screen. Each document also shows a What's next banner that says what the current stage means and who acts next.

The flow at a glance

Blue boxes are steps. Orange boxes are checks or holds that can stop the flow.

Step 1. Record the opportunity

  1. Open Sales & Tenders › Opportunities.
  2. Press New and enter the client, the job name and the expected value.
  3. When the client sends the tender documents, press Create Tender on the opportunity. The tender opens with the client and the value filled in.

Why it matters: Every job you chase is written down, so nothing is forgotten when the invitation arrives.

Tip

You can also start straight from Sales & Tenders › Tenders with New if there was no opportunity.

Step 2. Work the tender through the pipeline

  1. Open Sales & Tenders › Tenders. Tenders are shown as cards in columns, one column per stage.
  2. Open the tender. Fill in the client, the submission deadline, the estimated value and the probability.
  3. Drag the card, or click the stage at the top of the form, as the bid moves on (for example Enquiry, Prequalification, Estimating, Submitted).

Why it matters: The pipeline is the company's future workload. Directors read it every week.

Step 3. Decide whether to bid

  1. Open the tender and the go / no-go checklist on it.
  2. Answer each question Yes, No or Not applicable. Questions carry weights, so "do we have the bond capacity" counts more than "do we like the architect".
  3. Read the score and decide. If you do not bid, press Mark Lost and pick the reason.

Why it matters: A bid costs money to prepare. The checklist stops you pricing work you should not take.

Step 4. Price the bill

  1. On the tender, open its estimate bill. Load a bill template if you have one (Configuration › Jobs and Bills › Bill Templates), or import the client's bill from Excel.
  2. Price each line. Use the rate library (Configuration › Jobs and Bills › Rate Library) and Apply to Bill Lines to fill built-up rates.
  3. Back on the tender, press Apply Markup to add overhead and profit to the estimate.

Why it matters: The estimate is kept with the tender, so it can be compared with what the job really cost.

Tip

For a detailed estimate with versions, takeoff, build-ups, enquiries and a markup sheet, use Estimating. It is walked through in the flow chapter "From tender estimate to award".

Step 5. Post the bid bond

  1. Enter the bid bond amount, bank and expiry on the tender.
  2. Check Sales & Tenders › Bid Bonds Expiring for bonds close to expiry.
  3. To register the bonds in the bond register, open Sales & Tenders › Bond Register and use the Actions menu, then Register Bid Bonds from Tenders.

Why it matters: A bid bond that expires before the award can void the bid.

Step 6. Get sign-off and submit

  1. Press Request Sign-Off on the tender. The approver named in the approval matrix gets it in Waiting on Me.
  2. Once signed off, submit the bid to the client and move the tender to the Submitted stage.
  3. Record competitors and their prices as you learn them, on the tender or in Sales & Tenders › Competitor Analysis.

Why it matters: Competitor prices across many tenders show you where you are too expensive.

Step 7. Award: one click makes the job

  1. When the client accepts, open the tender and press Award.
  2. The system creates the project, the contract and a fresh site bill copied from the estimate. It does this once only.
  3. Open the new contract from the tender and check the dates, retention, advance and the standard form (see the next flow).

Why it matters: No retyping between the bid and the job, so the priced bill is exactly what was won.

Watch out

Award can only be pressed once. If the tender was lost instead, press Mark Lost and pick a loss reason so the reports can learn from it.

Tip: The Tender / Estimating dashboard (Dashboards & Reports › Dashboards › Tender / Estimating) shows the pipeline by stage, the win rate and the deadlines.
