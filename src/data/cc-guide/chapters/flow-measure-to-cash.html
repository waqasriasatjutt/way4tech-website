This is how the job gets paid. Work is measured against the bill, valued for the period, applied for, certified by the client, invoiced and paid. Retention is held on every certificate, the advance is recovered, and escalation adds a price adjustment line where the contract allows.

Tip

Open Home › How it works to see the processes as step cards with what is waiting for you at each step, and press Open to jump to the screen. Each document also shows a What's next banner that says what the current stage means and who acts next.

The flow at a glance

Blue boxes are steps. Orange boxes are checks or holds that can stop the flow.

Who does what

Step Who
Measure Site Engineer, Quantity Surveyor

Step 1. Measure the work

  1. Open Commercial & Billing › Measurement › Measurement Book and press New.
  2. Pick the project, the date, the measurer and the witness from the client's side.
  3. Add a line per bill item with the location and the dimensions.
  4. Press Book as Measured. When the client agrees the quantities press Mark Agreed, and Certify once they are in a certificate.

Why it matters: Nobody argues about the rate later. They argue about the quantity. The sheet settles it.

Watch out

A bill item covered by an open ITP hold point cannot be measured. The message names the hold point and who releases it. See the quality flow.

Step 2. Raise the valuation

  1. Open Commercial & Billing › Valuations and Certificates › Raise a Valuation. Pick the contract and the period end and run it.
  2. The valuation opens in Commercial & Billing › Valuations and Certificates › Interim Valuations. Press Pull Figures: measured work, agreed variations, materials on site, retention and advance recovery are read and frozen.
  3. Check the six numbers: gross to date, less retention, less advance recovery, less previously certified, equals due this time.
  4. Press Book as Valued.

Why it matters: Every figure is a snapshot, so a valuation issued in March still adds up in November.

Step 3. Apply for payment

  1. On the valuation press Apply for Payment. An application is created in Commercial & Billing › Valuations and Certificates › Applications for Payment.
  2. Print it and send it to the client. Press Submit on the application.

Why it matters: The application date starts the contract payment clock.

Note

If the client does not certify by the due date, the application can become the notified sum. The system gives a named person an activity when the date passes.

Step 4. Record the certificate

  1. When the client's certificate arrives, press Certify on the application. A payment certificate is created in Commercial & Billing › Valuations and Certificates › Payment Certificates.
  2. Enter what they certified on each line. Where it differs from what you claimed, the reason is required.
  3. Press Issue Certificate. Retention moves to the retention ledger.

Why it matters: Claimed and certified are kept apart so the shortfall can be argued later.

Step 5. Advance recovery

  1. The advance and its recovery rate come from Commercial & Billing › Advances and Escalation › Advance Payments.
  2. Each valuation deducts the recovery. The Recovery Schedule tab on the advance shows the balance still to recover.

Why it matters: The advance guarantee follows the balance down.

Step 6. Escalation line

  1. Open Commercial & Billing › Advances and Escalation › Escalation Statements, create a statement and pick the valuation (or open Escalation on the valuation).
  2. Press Compute. The factor is a + sum of (weight x current index / base index).
  3. Press Approve, then Add to Certificate. A line "Price adjustment (escalation) to date" is added to the draft certificate.

Why it matters: Inflation is recovered where the contract allows it.

Step 7. Invoice the client

  1. On the issued certificate press Create Invoice.
  2. The invoice is in Finance › Invoices and Bills › Client Invoices and in Invoicing › Customers › Invoices.
  3. Confirm and send it.

Why it matters: The invoice matches the certificate line by line.

Step 8. Receive payment and check retention

  1. Register the client's payment on the invoice. The certificate shows Paid.
  2. Open Commercial & Billing › Retention › Retention Ledger to see retention held and the release dates.

Why it matters: Retention is money owed to you. The ledger makes sure it is collected.

Tip

For South Asian jobs the payment certificate prints as a Running Account (RA) Bill: previous bill, this bill, cumulative, with the net payable in words.
