Change and delay are where contracts are won or lost. The rule is simple: record it the day it happens, serve the notice inside the time bar, keep the evidence, and settle it into the final account.

Tip

Open Home › How it works to see the processes as step cards with what is waiting for you at each step, and press Open to jump to the screen. Each document also shows a What's next banner that says what the current stage means and who acts next.

Variations from instructions

Step 1. Record the instruction

  1. Open Commercial & Billing › Variations and Claims › Site Instructions and press New.
  2. Record who gave it, when, and what was said. Tick verbal if it was not in writing.
  3. Press Issue. When the written confirmation arrives, press Confirmed in Writing. If it never comes inside the contract window, Mark Lapsed.

Why it matters: Unconfirmed verbal instructions are the most common reason extra work is never paid.

Tip

Check Dashboards & Reports › Site and Time › Unconfirmed Verbal Instructions every week.

Step 2. Raise the variation

  1. On the instruction press Raise Variation. The variation opens in Commercial & Billing › Variations and Claims › Variations.
  2. Choose the valuation method (bill rates, pro rata, star rate, daywork, lump sum) and enter Our Estimate.
  3. Press Submit to Client, then Under Review when they start assessing.
  4. Enter Their Assessment. When settled press Agreed. The agreed value is written only by this button.

Why it matters: Our estimate, their assessment and the agreed value are kept apart, so the negotiation is on record.

Step 3. Support it with dayworks

  1. For extra work done on dayworks, open Commercial & Billing › Variations and Claims › Daywork Sheets and record labour, plant and materials used.
  2. Press Submit for Signature, then Record Signature when the client signs.
  3. Press Value into Variation to carry it into the variation.

Why it matters: Unsigned dayworks are rarely paid.

Delays and extensions of time

Step 4. Record the delay event

  1. Delays are recorded on the site diary or in Site › Daily Records › Delays and Stoppages.
  2. Pick the cause (from Configuration › Site and Labour › Delay Causes), the hours or days lost and who is responsible.
  3. Press Notice Served when the notice has gone (see step 6).

Why it matters: Every delay may support time or money later.

Step 5. Build the EOT claim

  1. On the delay event press Assemble EOT Claim, or open Commercial & Billing › Variations and Claims › Extension of Time Claims.
  2. Press Gather Evidence Pack: diaries, weather, RFIs and programme updates for the period are collected.
  3. Press Submit, then Under Assessment, and record the outcome with Award or Reject.

Why it matters: Time awarded means no liquidated damages for those days.

Notices and time bars

Step 6. Serve the notice on time

  1. On the instruction, variation, delay event, EOT claim or claim press Raise Contract Notice. The contract, source, trigger date and notice type fill in.
  2. The clause, time bar and Due By date come from the contract's standard form.
  3. Write the letter and press Print Letter. When it is served press Issue. A notice served after its due date is flagged Served Late.
  4. Record Acknowledged and Reply Received when they come.

Why it matters: A missed time bar can lose the right to time or money entirely.

Watch out

A notice still unserved after its due date is marked Lapsed and its owner gets a to-do. Check Contracts & Setup › Contract Administration › Time Bars This Week every Monday.

Step 7. See the deadlines

  1. Open Contracts & Setup › Contract Administration › Time Bar Calendar for a month view.
  2. Alerts are raised at 7, 3 and 1 days and when past (Dashboards & Reports › Alerts › Alerts).

Why it matters: Deadlines are visible to everyone, not just in one person's diary.

Claims and the final account

Step 8. Run the claim

  1. Open Commercial & Billing › Variations and Claims › Contract Claims and press New. Link the notices, delay events, variations and instructions behind it.
  2. Press Raise Notice, then Notice Served.
  3. Write the narrative and build the quantum (head, basis, quantity, rate). Attach the records and press Submit Claim.
  4. Record the assessed amount and press Agreed or Disputed.
  5. Press Post to Final Account to add the agreed sum to the contract's final account.

Why it matters: A claim with its notices and evidence linked is a claim that can be won.

Step 9. Loss and expense

  1. For the cost of delay, open Commercial & Billing › Variations and Claims › Loss and Expense Claims.
  2. Press Add the Prolongation Head to price prolongation from the time-related preliminaries over the weeks awarded.
  3. Submit, then record Agreed or Rejected head by head.

Why it matters: Claims are settled head by head, so they are recorded that way.
