The programme says when work should happen, and work orders give the work out. Delay is measured against the frozen baseline programme.

Tip

Every section follows the same pattern: where the menu is, what it is for, who uses it, a picture of the list, a picture of a typical record, the important fields, the stages, the buttons and the linked records. On the live screens, the ? button next to the pager opens the same kind of help for the screen you are on.

Planning

Where: Planning

What it is for: The programme as a Gantt chart with links, critical path and baseline. Drag bars to reschedule.

Who uses it: Project Manager

Key steps:

  1. Pick the project at the top.
  2. Drag a bar to move an activity, or its end to change the duration.
  3. Turn on the critical path and baseline to see slippage.

How to use it:

  1. Open Planning.
  2. Pick the company, the project and the period at the top, if the screen has them.
  3. Read the tiles and charts. Click a tile, a bar or a table row to open the records behind it.
  4. Press the refresh button to read the latest figures.

Who uses it: Project Managers, Planners.

Why it matters: Same screen, another door.

Note

This is the same screen as Planning ("Planning"). The fields and buttons are described there.

Programmes

Where: Planning › Programmes

What it is for: Programmes: the baseline and updated programmes with activities and the critical path.

Who uses it: Planners, Project Managers.

Why it matters: Delay is measured against the programme.

Key steps:

  1. Press New, pick the project and add the activities with dates and predecessors.
  2. Press Recalculate Critical Path.
  3. Press Freeze as Baseline when the programme is agreed.
  4. Use New Revision for an updated programme; the baseline stays as it was.

The list shows: Reference, Programme, Project, Version, Date Start, Planned Finish, Forecast Finish, Baseline Finish, Slippage Days, Activity Count, Critical Count, State.

A typical record. Click any line to open it. This is "PRG/2026/0001":

Field What to enter Where it comes from
Programme Type it. You enter it.

Project Pick from the list. The Projects list.
Contract Pick from the list. The Construction Contract list.
Planner Pick from the list. The Users list.

Buttons

Work Orders

Where: Planning › Work Orders › Work Orders

What it is for: Work orders: a piece of work for a crew or subcontractor, with the bill item, dates, quantity and progress.

Who uses it: Project Managers, Site Users.

Why it matters: Work is planned, given out and tracked against the bill.

Key steps:

  1. Press New, or use Generate Work Orders from BoQ.
  2. Assign the gang or subcontractor and planned dates, then press Release.
  3. Press Start when work begins; record progress on job sheets.
  4. Press Mark Done when finished and Close once it is measured.

The list shows: Reference, Project, Assigned To, Gang, Subcontract, Supervisor, Planned Start, Planned Finish, Progress %, Priority, Status, Activities.

A typical record. Click any line to open it. This is "WO/2026/00006 - Northgate Wharf, Residential Block B":

Field What to enter Where it comes from

Phase Pick from the list. The Construction Phase list.

Priority Choose one: Normal, High, Urgent. You enter it.

Generate Work Orders from Bill

Where: Planning › Work Orders › Generate Work Orders from Bill

What it is for: A wizard that creates work orders from bill items.

Who uses it: Project Managers.

Why it matters: Saves typing: one work order per bill item in a click.

Key steps:

  1. Pick the project and the bill items.
  2. Choose how to group them into work orders.
  3. Press Generate.

This menu opens a small window (a wizard). Fill it in and press the main button to run it, or close it to do nothing.

What to fill in

Field What to enter Where it comes from
Project Pick from the list. The Projects list.
Bill Pick from the list. The Bills of Quantities list.
Section Pick a section to pull in every one of its lines, instead of choosing lines one at a time below. The Boq Section list.
One Work Order per Section On: one work order per bill section, carrying every selected line under it. Off: one work order per bill line. You enter it.
Phase Pick from the list. The Construction Phase list.
Location Pick from the list. The Site Location / Area list.
Planned Start Pick the date. You enter it.
Planned Finish Pick the date. You enter it.
Inspection Required Tick when it applies. You enter it.
Bill Lines Pick one or more. The Bill Items list.
Assigned To Choose one: Internal Gang, Subcontractor. You enter it.
Gang Pick from the list. The Labour Gang list.

Buttons in the window: Generate: Builds the lines from the current data.; Cancel: Cancels the record. It stays in the list for the record.

Delayed Work Orders

Where: Planning › Work Orders › Delayed Work Orders

What it is for: Work orders past their planned end and not finished.

Who uses it: Project Managers.

Why it matters: The late list to chase every morning.

Key steps:

  1. Open each work order to see why it is late.
  2. Re-plan the dates or add resources.
  3. Raise a delay event if the cause is not yours.

The list shows: Reference, Project, Assigned To, Gang, Subcontract, Supervisor, Planned Start, Planned Finish, Progress %, Priority, Status, Activities.
