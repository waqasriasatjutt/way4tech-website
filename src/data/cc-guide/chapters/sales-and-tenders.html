Before there is a job there is a bid. This app holds opportunities, the tender pipeline, client quotations, bid bonds and what competitors priced. Estimating, which prices the tender, has its own chapter next.

Tip

Every section follows the same pattern: where the menu is, what it is for, who uses it, a picture of the list, a picture of a typical record, the important fields, the stages, the buttons and the linked records. On the live screens, the ? button next to the pager opens the same kind of help for the screen you are on.

Sales & Tenders

Where: Sales & Tenders

What it is for: Possible jobs before there is anything to price: the client, the scope and how likely it looks. A promising opportunity becomes a tender.

Who uses it: Director, Estimator

Key steps:

  1. Press New and enter the client and the job.
  2. Move the card through the stages as it develops.
  3. When it is worth bidding, open it and create a tender from it.
  4. Mark it Won or Lost when the outcome is known.

Tenders

Where: Sales & Tenders › Tenders

What it is for: Tender pipeline: every bid from enquiry to won or lost, with value, dates and probability.

Who uses it: Estimators, Directors.

Why it matters: The future workload of the company.

Key steps:

  1. Press New, enter the client, the deadline and the bid bond.
  2. Open Estimate Bill to price the bill of quantities, then Apply Markup.
  3. Press Request Sign-Off before the price goes out.
  4. Use Create Client Quotation to send the price to the client.
  5. When won, press Award to open the project and contract; otherwise Mark Lost.

Client Quotations

Where: Sales & Tenders › Client Quotations

What it is for: Quotations sent to clients for smaller jobs.

Who uses it: Estimators.

Why it matters: Quick priced offers outside a full tender.

Key steps:

  1. Open a tender and press Create Client Quotation.
  2. Check the lines and press Send to email it to the client.
  3. When the client accepts and the tender is Won, press Confirm.

The list shows: Number, Order Date, Promised Delivery, Customer, Total, Status, Invoice Status, Activities.

Bid Bonds Expiring

Where: Sales & Tenders › Bid Bonds Expiring

What it is for: Bid bonds on tenders, with expiry.

Who uses it: Estimators, Finance.

Why it matters: A lapsed bid bond can void a bid.

Key steps:

  1. Open each tender in the list.
  2. If the bid is still live, renew the bond and update its expiry date.
  3. If the bid is decided, ask the bank or client for the bond back.

The list shows: Reference, Project Title, Client / Authority, Stage, Estimator, Submission Deadline, Days to Deadline, Tender Value, Expected Value.

Competitor Analysis

Where: Sales & Tenders › Competitor Analysis

What it is for: Competitor analysis: who bid what on each tender.

Who uses it: Estimators, Directors.

Why it matters: Learn how competitors price.

Key steps:

  1. Record competitor bids on the Competitors tab of a decided tender.
  2. Group this report by competitor or by client.
  3. Compare their price against yours to set the next markup.

Bond Register

Where: Sales & Tenders › Bond Register

What it is for: Bond register filtered to bid bonds.

Who uses it: Estimators, Finance.

Why it matters: All bid bonds in one list with the bank.

The list shows: Reference, Type, Direction, Contract, Subcontract, Beneficiary, Issuing Bank or Surety, Bond Number, Face Value, Current Value, Expires On, State.
