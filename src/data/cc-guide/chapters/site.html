What it is for: Opens the phone Site App at /site in a new tab.

Site Diaries

Where: Site › Daily Records › Site Diaries

What it is for: Site diaries: one per site per day with weather, manpower, plant, work done, delays, visitors and photos.

Who uses it: Site Users (write), Project Managers (check), Commercial (read).

Why it matters: The diary is the daily record that wins disputes about delay and dayworks.

Key steps:

  1. Use Today to open today's diary for your site.
  2. Press Copy Yesterday to start from yesterday's entries.
  3. Fill in labour, plant, work done and any delays, and add photos.
  4. Press Post Day at the end of the shift.

The list shows: Date, Site, Recorded By, Conditions, Rain (mm), Adverse, Total Headcount, Total Man Hours, Total Plant Hours, Plant Idle Hours, Delay Hours, Incident, State.

My Site Check-In

Where: Site › Attendance › My Site Check-In

What it is for: The phone check-in page for the person logged in. It checks the phone position against the site geofence.

Who uses it: Site Workers, everyone on site.

Why it matters: Attendance is recorded at the site, with proof of place and time.

This menu opens the check-in page at /construction/checkin. It is built for a phone.

  1. Open the page on your phone (or the Check In quick action on Home).
  2. Allow the browser to use your location.
  3. Press the big Check In button. The page compares your position with the site geofence set on the project.
  4. Inside the geofence the check-in is recorded. Outside it, the company policy decides: the check-in is refused, or it is recorded and flagged as an exception for the supervisor.
  5. Press Check Out at the end of the day.

Note

The time recorded is the server's time, so the phone needs a connection to check in.

The records it creates are listed under Site › Attendance › All Attendance.

Why it matters: Sites do not run out of cash.

  2. Check the spending since the last top-up.
  3. Press Replenish.

A typical record. Click any line to open it. This is "Harlow Court Van Float (P. Nowak)":

The important fields

Field What to enter Where it comes from
Float What the holder and the office call it, e.g. "Harlow Court Site Float" or "Central Purchasing Float". You enter it.
Reference Nothing, it fills in by itself. Set by the system.
Holder The person accountable for this cash. Every move on the float is a change to what they are holding on the company's behalf. The Employees list.
Holder User The holder's login, for notifications and for who can submit a claim as themselves. Defaults from the employee record when one is linked. The Users list.
Project Leave empty for a purchase agent float that buys across several jobs. Set it for a site float, where every expense is assumed to be this project unless a line says otherwise. The Projects list.
Company Pick from the list. The Companies list.
Float Ceiling The most this float may ever be holding. An issue or top-up that would take the balance over this is refused. You enter it.
Per-Transaction Limit The most a single expense may claim. Zero means no limit beyond the float ceiling itself. You enter it.
Receipt Required Above An expense strictly above this amount cannot be submitted without a receipt attached. Set to zero to require a receipt for everything. You enter it.
Replenish Below (%) When the balance falls to this percentage of the ceiling or lower, the float is flagged as needing a top-up. You enter it.
Balance Cash the holder is accountable for right now: every approved or posted move, netted. Never edited directly. Worked out by the system.
Allowed Categories Leave empty to allow any category. Set it to stop this float's cash being claimed for something it was never meant to cover. The Construction Expense Category list.
